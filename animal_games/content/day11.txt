[[DAY]]
DAY 11 — SPACE_FAN'S BIRTHDAY

[[SYSTEM_STATE]]
Day index: 11.
Participant count: 18.
Challenge type: creative — non-lethal.
Special event: birthday observance.
Lethality: none. No system selection.
Animal presence: limited — Drake leads, Soup and Bunny remain in hub.

[[NARRATIVE]]
There is a song.

It arrives in the rooms before consciousness does, the way certain sounds arrive — not sharp enough to startle, not loud enough to demand attention, just present, woven into the half-awake state where the body has registered information that the mind hasn't categorized yet. The song is soft. It has the quality of music that has been chosen rather than weaponized.

space_fan hears it first because space_fan sleeps lightly, the watchful instinct that has kept her at the edge of formations for eleven days extending into sleep itself, the body alert at low frequency even when the mind is not. She hears the song and her brain's first attempt at recognition is incomplete — familiar, definitely familiar, the kind of melody that exists in everyone's memory, but the recognition is foggy and the song is soft and she rolls onto her back and listens with eyes closed.

In the Wardens' sector central room, Schwalbe wakes second. He is also a light sleeper — more from training than instinct, the specific alertness of someone whose first response to any new stimulus is to verify the situation. He hears the song. His brain catches the melody three notes into the second phrase.

He sits up.

In the Floofs' sector, CharliSpirit wakes and listens and does not place it because the song is in a register her tired brain hasn't sorted yet. lumallie's absence is still being processed — the gap where the complementary system used to be — and waking up has been a slower process since Day 9.

In the Syndicate's sector, Drug wakes the way she always wakes — completely, in one motion, no transition between sleep and observation. She hears the song. She places it immediately. She turns her head to look at Rabbit, who is also awake, who is also listening, who is also doing the math of what this song means.

"It's happy birthday," Drug says.

"Yes," Rabbit says.

"Whose."

"I don't know yet."

The song repeats. Or doesn't repeat — extends, the same melody played gently and continuously, the ambient music of a moment that wants to be noticed but doesn't want to insist. Eighteen people in three sectors gradually arrive at the same recognition, the song surfacing through the layers of sleep until it announces itself fully.

Happy birthday.

It is the most ordinary song in the world. It has been sung at every birthday party for a hundred years. It has the quality of a song that doesn't need to be good because the occasion does the work, and hearing it played as ambient morning music in a facility that has killed twelve people in the last week is the most disorienting thing that has happened in this place that wasn't designed to disorient.

"Whose birthday?" Galaxy asks, when the Floofs gather in their central room.

"Has anyone said when their birthday is?" Polaris asks.

"Loyal2Schwab once mentioned his was in March," CharliSpirit says.

"That was March."

"I know. I'm just saying. He mentioned it."

Nobody can place the date. Nobody has been keeping calendar — the days have been counted in increments of horror rather than in increments of time, and asking which of these days is somebody's actual birthday requires data that nobody has.

"We'll find out when we get to the hub," Ninnginni says. "Get up."

[[THE HUB]]
The hub is decorated.

Not garishly. Not in a way that looks performed. The lighting has shifted to something warmer than usual — a yellow-gold register that makes the whole space feel softer, the edges of things slightly more forgiving. There are no streamers, no balloons, no party-store paraphernalia. The decoration is in the light and in the air and in the specific quality of a space that has been prepared with care.

And on the central table — the table that has held three teams' worth of meals for eleven days — is a cake.

It is large. Three tiers, white frosting, decorated with what look like real flowers — small white blossoms arranged around the base of each tier in a pattern that is intentional but not symmetrical, the kind of arrangement that someone made by hand rather than by template. On top of the cake, written in gold cursive that catches the warm light:

*Happy Birthday space_fan*

Eighteen people stop walking when they enter the hub.

space_fan is in the middle of the Wardens' loose formation. She sees the cake. She sees her name on the cake. She stops. The Wardens around her stop. Across the hub, the Floofs and the Syndicate stop in their own formations.

"It's yours," Schwalbe says. Quietly. Beside her.

"Yes," space_fan says.

Her face does something complicated. Not a smile, not a flinch, not anything that has a single word — the specific expression of someone who has been managing eleven days of survival in a facility designed to kill her and has just been presented with a cake that has her name written on it in gold cursive in a hub that has been prepared to honor her.

The Drake moves.

He has been at the Wardens' edge, electric veins at low brightness, watching the morning. Now he steps forward, walks to space_fan with the unusual gait of a creature designed for air doing something deliberate on the ground, and stops in front of her.

He is carrying something.

In his front claws — held delicately, the way the Drake holds anything precious — is a small bundle. It is wrapped in soft fabric, gray and white. The bundle is moving.

The Drake places the bundle in space_fan's arms.

It is a cat.

A small one — young, but not a kitten, the awkward in-between age where the body has grown but the proportions haven't settled. Tabby-patterned, the gray-brown striping that tabby cats have been wearing for thousands of years. And along its back, three distinct black stripes — perfectly parallel, running from shoulders to tail, the kind of marking that looks like it was designed rather than evolved.

The cat is alive. It is warm. It looks up at space_fan with the specific curiosity of a young animal encountering a new person.

space_fan holds the cat. She does not move for several seconds.

"Drake," she says.

*Yours,* the Drake communicates. *A gift.*

"From you?"

*From the facility. Through me.*

"Why?"

*Because today is your day. Because you have carried a notebook you cannot read. Because the facility is not always cruel, and today it has chosen not to be. Take the cat.*

space_fan takes the cat. The cat — now held against her chest with both arms — leans into her with the immediate acceptance of an animal that has decided this person is the right person. It tucks its head under her chin. It begins to purr. The purring is loud — unreasonably loud for an animal this size, the rumble of a small motor, the specific sound that says a cat is comfortable in a way that goes beyond words.

The figure's voice arrives. Warm — not the warm-controlled tone of the briefings, the actual warmth, the same register as the Easter morning.

"Happy birthday, space_fan."

space_fan looks up. At the ceiling. At nowhere in particular. At wherever the figure is when the figure is speaking.

"Thank you," she says.

"Today is yours. There will be a gentle activity, with no consequence to anyone other than the gift of a small comfort to the winner. But first — eat. Free cake for everyone. Take what you want. The cake is a cake."

The hub holds this — the figure speaking the way a relative speaks at a family gathering, the strangeness of it, the disjunction between this voice and the voice that has announced lethality and selection and the system's arithmetic. Today the voice is just a voice.

"Get cake," Schwalbe says.

[[THE CAKE]]
The cake is good.

This is the simple fact that takes a few minutes for everyone to fully process. The cake is not facility-cake, not calibrated-to-memory cake, not strategically deployed psychological-comfort cake. It is just cake — vanilla sponge, white buttercream, the kind of cake that exists in bakeries on the other side of doors that nobody has walked through in eleven days. It tastes like cake. It is exactly what cake is supposed to be.

space_fan gets the first slice. The cat is in her left arm. The plate is in her right hand. She sits down at the central table with the cat on her lap and the cake in front of her, and the cat watches the cake with the specific interest that cats reserve for any food the human is eating, which is all food.

"Don't give the cat any," Schwalbe says, sitting beside her with his own slice. "Cats can't have most of the things in cake."

"I know," space_fan says.

The Wardens distribute around the table — Duccarian, Shroud, Rally Mally, Orphan Annihilator. Six Wardens. The Reddening Day's gap is present at every table now, the absence persistent, but today the gap is filled by cake, which is what cake does.

The Floofs come over. This is unusual — the team boundaries have been somewhat permeable since the Easter shared meal, but moving entirely to another team's section is new. Ninnginni leads, carrying a slice. CharliSpirit beside him. The other four Floofs following.

"Can we?" Ninnginni asks, gesturing at the table.

"It's space_fan's day," Schwalbe says. "She decides."

space_fan looks at the Floofs. At the cake. At the cat in her lap.

"Sit," she says.

The Syndicate joins. Six members — Drug, Rabbit, JIKOSU, janhk, NinjaFlout, Lyrix. They bring slices. They sit. Eighteen people at one table for the second time since the Easter morning, the team structure relaxed for a day that has decided to be different.

The cat watches the room from space_fan's lap with the calm assessment of a small animal evaluating territory. It blinks slowly at people who blink back. It tucks its paws under itself. It purrs.

"Does it have a name?" Polaris asks.

space_fan considers this.

"Not yet," she says.

"You should name it."

"I will. Later."

"Tabby with three stripes," Galaxy says, looking at the cat. "Distinctive."

"Striped tabbies are common," Duccarian says. "Three perfectly parallel black stripes are not. The pattern is unusual."

"It's not a normal cat," Drug says.

space_fan looks at her. "What do you mean?"

"I mean it came from the Drake's claws. Things that come from the Drake's claws are not normal."

The cat purrs more loudly, as if confirming this.

"Is that a problem?" space_fan asks.

"No," Drug says. "I'm just naming it. Nothing in here is normal. The cat is not normal. The cat is also a cat. Both things are true."

space_fan accepts this. She continues eating cake. The cat continues purring. The hub continues to hold eighteen people who are eating cake at a table on a morning that smells like vanilla and feels, briefly, like a place where eighteen people might just be eighteen people.

[[THE ROOM]]
The Drake leads them.

Not Soup. Not the Bunny. Just the Drake — the other two animals remain in the hub, Soup hovering at its usual ceiling height, the Bunny sitting at the edge of the room with the specific posture of something that has been told today is not its day and is honoring that.

The Drake moves toward a corridor that has not been used before. The floor-lights activate in a soft warm tone — not white, not gold, something warmer, the same register as the cake's frosting. The corridor is short and gentle, no curves, no descent, no tricks. Just a corridor.

It opens into a room.

The room is full of orchids.

Not metaphorically — full of orchids, the air visibly humid, the temperature warm, the lighting specific — the kind of full-spectrum diffused light that orchids require. The room is the perfect environment for them. There are dozens of varieties — phalaenopsis with their wide flat petals, dendrobium with their cascading sprays, oncidium with their dancing-lady flowers, paphiopedilum with their slipper-shaped pouches. The colors range from pure white through every shade of pink, purple, yellow, orange, red. Some are massive. Some are tiny — the miniature orchids that fit in a thimble, with flowers the size of a fingernail.

The room smells like every orchid in it. The combined fragrance is gentle — orchids are not heavily scented, most of them, but the cumulative effect of dozens in one warm room produces a soft floral atmosphere that is the exact opposite of every smell the facility has ever produced.

In the center of the room: eighteen aquariums.

Empty. Glass. Each one a rectangular tank, mid-sized, the kind of tank that would normally hold fish but today is holding nothing yet. Beside each aquarium: a small numbered tag in team colors. One per person.

The figure's voice:

"Happy birthday, space_fan. Today you will choose a winner. Everyone will create something. The winner gets a softer mattress. So do you, space_fan, regardless of who else wins — that is your gift. Your task is to look at what they make and decide which one you like best."

A pause.

"The rules are simple. Take orchids from the room. Decorate your aquarium. Make something. There is no right answer. There is no time limit. When you are finished, raise your hand. When everyone has raised their hand, space_fan will choose."

Another pause. The warmth in the voice is still present. It has not left since the cake.

"Have fun."

Eighteen people stand in a room of orchids in front of eighteen empty aquariums and hear the words have fun for the first time in eleven days.

[[THE MAKING]]
The first few minutes are slow.

Nobody has done this before. Nobody knows what it should look like. Nobody has decorated an aquarium with orchids, ever. The instruction is simple and the freedom of it is the disorienting part — the facility's challenges have been about navigating constraints, not about creating without them.

CharliSpirit is the first to commit. She walks among the orchids, looking, the cartographic eye finally given a task it can handle — survey, select, organize. She gathers small phalaenopsis with white petals and miniature dendrobium with deep purple flowers. She returns to her aquarium and begins arranging them in a vertical composition, the small white flowers as a base and the purple cascading from above, building height the way she would build a map.

Ninnginni works methodically. He selects a single large phalaenopsis — pink with magenta speckling — and places it as a centerpiece. Then he adds smaller flowers around it, building a composition that has one focal point and several supporting elements, a hierarchy of visual weight that mirrors the way he organizes a team.

Galaxy goes wild. He gathers everything that catches his eye — a yellow oncidium, a red orchid he doesn't know the name of, three different miniatures, a white flower that smells like vanilla. He fills his aquarium with so much variety that the composition is essentially every orchid having a conversation at once, which is somehow the right answer for Galaxy.

Polaris moves slowly because of her ankle, but the movement gives her time to think. She picks two orchids — only two — both white, both small, and places them at opposite corners of her aquarium with empty space between them. Minimalist. Quiet. The visual equivalent of her contribution to the team: present, deliberate, not asking for attention.

Drug is precise. She selects orchids with the same attention she gives to everything — the white phalaenopsis with the slight pink veining, the dark purple miniature, a single yellow flower for accent. She arranges them in her aquarium in a composition that is balanced and careful and beautiful, and then she stands back and looks at it with the same flat expression she has carried through eleven days, which today carries the specific quality of someone who is doing something for someone else's birthday and is taking the doing seriously.

JIKOSU is tactical. He surveys the orchid varieties first, identifying which ones produce the strongest visual impact, then selects accordingly — three medium phalaenopsis in colors that complement each other, arranged in a triangle composition that is structurally sound and aesthetically reasonable. He makes a good aquarium the way he does everything: by treating the task as a problem with parameters and solving it.

Rabbit gathers orchids slowly. He doesn't have a vision. He picks ones he likes and brings them to the aquarium and tries arrangements and changes them and tries again. The Bunny is not here — it stayed in the hub — and Rabbit notices the absence the way you notice the absence of a hand you usually use, the asymmetry of working without something that has been beside him for eleven days.

Orphan Annihilator sits to work. His ankle prefers it. He gathers a small selection — three slipper orchids, the ones with the pouch-shaped lower petals, all in different colors. He arranges them in a row in his aquarium, low and stable, the kind of composition that doesn't require height or grandeur to be effective.

Schwalbe is functional. He picks orchids that look like they should go together, places them in his aquarium in a way that makes sense, and stands back. He is not artistic. He has never claimed to be. His aquarium is organized and dignified and looks like it was made by Schwalbe, which is what space_fan would expect.

Duccarian thinks about it for a while. Then he picks three orchids — one large white phalaenopsis, one tiny purple miniature, and one yellow oncidium with cascading flowers — and arranges them as a meditation on scale. The big one. The small one. The medium one. The composition is intentional, philosophical, the visual equivalent of a sentence with three clauses.

space_fan watches them work.

She is not making one. The figure said her job is to choose. She holds her cat — she has been holding the cat the whole time, the small animal sitting in her arms with the patient acceptance of a creature that has chosen its person and intends to remain — and she walks slowly around the room, watching eighteen people try to make her something beautiful.

[[THE FINISHED]]
Hands rise.

CharliSpirit first. Her aquarium is a vertical composition of white and purple, the small phalaenopsis grounding the structure and the dendrobium cascading from a small piece of mounted bark she found at the back of the room — bark she added without being told she could, the orchid technically growing from it the way orchids grow from trees in nature. The composition has implied movement, like the flowers are mid-fall.

Ninnginni. His central pink phalaenopsis is surrounded by smaller satellite orchids in white and yellow, the arrangement reading like a planet with moons. Stable. Hierarchical. Beautiful in the same way that a well-organized team is beautiful.

Drug. Her aquarium is small but exquisite — three orchids in perfect balance, the colors talking to each other across the empty space, the composition having the specific quality of something that has been considered without being labored. It looks easy. It is not easy.

Galaxy. His aquarium is chaos — fifteen different orchids crammed in, every color, every shape, the visual equivalent of pure enthusiasm. It is also somehow good. The chaos has a warmth to it. The aquarium feels happy.

Polaris. Two white orchids at opposite corners. The empty space between them is the composition. The aquarium has the quality of a haiku.

JIKOSU. The triangle of complementary phalaenopsis is solid, structurally clear, aesthetically pleasing. It is a good aquarium. It is not a memorable aquarium.

Schwalbe's is honest — orchids placed sensibly, the way Schwalbe does everything. It looks like Schwalbe. space_fan smiles when she sees it.

Duccarian's three-scale meditation is intellectually beautiful. The big, the small, the medium. The empty space around each one giving it room to be considered. It looks like an essay made of flowers.

Orphan Annihilator's row of slipper orchids is grounded and quiet — three pouches in three colors, sitting low in the aquarium, the kind of composition that feels reliable.

Rabbit's aquarium is sweet. He kept rearranging, never settled on a single vision, and the result is a composition that has the quality of a person who tried — visible care, visible uncertainty, the orchids placed slightly imperfectly but with affection.

The other seven aquariums vary in style and quality. Lyrix made one that is dramatic — a single very large purple phalaenopsis taking up most of the tank, the boldness of a single statement. NinjaFlout's is mathematical, the orchids arranged according to some ratio he calculated. janhk's is tactile — orchids selected for their textures rather than their colors, the visual differences felt by hand. Shroud's is environmental, with bark and small stones added to suggest a habitat. Rally Mally's is generous, large orchids placed warmly. Elv's is precise, every orchid considered. Godly_Aura's is symmetrical, the careful balance of someone who appreciates order.

Eighteen aquariums. Eighteen hands raised.

space_fan walks among them.

She holds the cat. She stops at each aquarium. She looks at it for a long time. The room is quiet — eighteen people who have worked for an hour and are now waiting for one person to choose, the silence of a moment that is not stressful because nothing bad happens to whoever doesn't win.

She walks past Galaxy's chaos and smiles. She lingers at Drug's precision. She stops at Polaris's haiku and stays there for a while. She admires Duccarian's meditation. She touches the rim of Schwalbe's honest aquarium with her free hand.

She stops at Drug's again.

"This one," she says.

Drug looks up. The Syndicate looks up. The room looks up.

"Drug's."

"Why?" Drug asks. Not protesting. Genuinely asking.

space_fan considers this.

"Because it looks like you," she says. "It's calm. It's exact. It doesn't ask for anything. And the orchids are in conversation with each other in the way that I think you and Mainthemainstar were in conversation. The empty space is part of the composition. It's not missing. It's part of it."

Drug doesn't respond immediately. The room holds its silence. The cat, in space_fan's arms, makes a small sound.

"Thank you," Drug says, finally. Quietly.

"Thank you for making it," space_fan says.

The figure's voice:

"Drug receives a softer mattress. space_fan receives one as well — that is the birthday gift. Congratulations, both."

The room eases. Eighteen people relax. Nobody won and nobody lost, except for Drug and space_fan winning, and the winning is small and the losing isn't a real loss because nothing real was at stake. The orchids remain in the aquariums. The aquariums remain on the floor.

"Your aquariums will be moved to the dorms," the figure says. "They will be present in your sleeping spaces from today forward. A small permanence."

[[RETURN]]
They walk back through the warm corridor. The Drake is ahead. The orchid room recedes behind them. The cake is gone but the memory of it is present — the specific feeling of having eaten cake that was just cake on a morning that was just a morning.

space_fan walks with her cat. The cat has not stopped purring since it was placed in her arms. The other Wardens walk around her in the loose formation that has always been their structure, but today the formation is happier. Schwalbe is beside space_fan. Rally Mally is talking to Orphan Annihilator about cake. Duccarian is thinking about his three-scale meditation, the philosophy of it, whether scale itself was the right meditation or whether he should have meditated on something else.

The Floofs walk behind. CharliSpirit is processing — she made something, it was good, and the making used a part of her that hasn't been used since the maps stopped working. The orchid composition required mapping in a different sense — visual mapping, aesthetic mapping, the kind of structure that doesn't break when geometry shifts.

The Syndicate walks last. Drug holds nothing — her aquarium will be in her sleeping space tonight — but her hands are at her sides in the specific position of someone who has made something. Rabbit is beside her. He is not holding the Bunny's fur today; he left it in his sleeping space. He is, instead, walking like a person who attended a birthday.

The hub is as they left it. Soup descends from the ceiling to greet the returning Floofs. The Bunny rises from its position at the room's edge and walks to Drug — past Rabbit, to Drug, the same continuation of yesterday's permission — and brushes against her leg before continuing to Rabbit.

The cat in space_fan's arms looks at the Bunny. The Bunny looks at the cat. They consider each other with the wordless assessment of two animals whose existence in a place like this requires an introduction. The Bunny's red eyes hold the cat's calm gaze for several seconds. Then the Bunny continues on its way.

The cat purrs.

"What's its name?" Schwalbe asks, in the hub.

space_fan thinks about it.

"Orchid," she says.

"Orchid," Schwalbe repeats.

"It's the first thing the day was, before it was anything else," space_fan says. "Cake came after. Drake came after. The room was orchids first."

"Orchid," Schwalbe says again, and the cat — Orchid — purrs at the sound of its new name.

[[DORMS]]
The aquariums are in the sleeping spaces.

Each member's aquarium has been placed beside their sleeping platform — the orchid arrangements they made, preserved, now part of the dorm. The altered geometry of the rooms accepts the new objects without complaint, the wrongness having stabilized enough by now that adding eighteen aquariums full of orchids feels like a soft correction, the rooms looking less like dreams and more like rooms that have flowers in them.

space_fan's aquarium is at the head of her sleeping platform. It is empty — she didn't make one. Beside it is Drug's orchid composition, transferred from the orchid room to space_fan's sector by some mechanism nobody saw operate. The composition is in space_fan's space because the gift includes Drug's gift — the winner's aquarium, given to the birthday person.

Drug's actual aquarium is duplicated. The original sits beside Drug's sleeping platform in the Syndicate's sector. A perfect duplicate — same orchids, same arrangement, same composition — is in space_fan's space. The facility, when it chooses to be generous, is generous in detail.

space_fan sits on her sleeping platform. Orchid the cat curls up beside her. The new mattress is softer than the old one — substantially softer, the kind of softness that the body recognizes immediately as different.

Schwalbe sits beside her, briefly.

"Good day?" he asks.

"Good day," she says.

"Eleven days in."

"Eleven days in."

"You think there will be more days like this?"

space_fan looks at the cat. At the orchid composition. At the notebook sitting on the floor near her platform — still unreadable, still present.

"Probably not," she says.

"No."

They sit with this. It is not a sad acknowledgment. It is a real one. The day was good. The day was almost certainly an outlier. The days that come will not be like the day that was.

But the cake was real. The cat is real. The orchid is real.

"Happy birthday, space_fan," Schwalbe says, and stands, and goes to his own sleeping platform.

In the Floofs' sector, Ninnginni places his book on a shelf — the book from yesterday, the one he refused to put down — and sits down beside his orchid composition, and Soup descends to face height, and the day ends quietly.

In the Syndicate's sector, Drug looks at her aquarium for a long time. The Bunny is beside her. Rabbit is across the room. The aquarium is in conversation with itself — orchids talking to orchids, empty space participating, the composition that space_fan saw and named for what it was.

It does look like her.

It also looks like Mainthemainstar — the soft-focus attention applied to flowers, the empty space that was him, the composition that was always the two of them.

Drug closes her eyes.

The cat in space_fan's sector purrs softly. The Bunny in the Syndicate's sector watches the door. Soup in the Floofs' sector glows at its low resting register. Three sectors. Eighteen people. Eighteen aquariums.

A birthday. A day. A small kindness in a long week of harder things.
