[[DAY]]
DAY 12 — THE DARK MAZE

[[SYSTEM_STATE]]
Day index: 12.
Participant count: 18.
Challenge type: dispersed search — no teams, sound-suppressive environment, trap-laced.
Lethality: high. Conditions: be found or die. Trap exposure permanent.

[[NARRATIVE]]
space_fan wakes early.

She does not wake to a song. She does not wake to a vibration. She wakes to Orchid — the cat — sitting on her chest, paws tucked beneath her body, looking down at space_fan's face with the patient expectation of a cat who has decided that the human has been asleep long enough. Orchid's purring is the only sound in the room.

space_fan opens her eyes. Orchid blinks slowly. space_fan blinks back.

The aquarium beside her sleeping platform — Drug's duplicate, the precise composition with three orchids and the empty space that was him — is the first thing she focuses on after the cat. She looks at it for a long moment. Then she gets up.

She doesn't think about what she's going to do. She just starts doing it.

The orchid room from yesterday is gone — she knows this without checking. The facility doesn't keep rooms. Yesterday's spaces dissolve into tomorrow's needs, the architecture endlessly reconfiguring itself. But the materials are not gone. There is a small shelf in her sleeping space, beside her own empty aquarium, where the facility has placed things — moss in a small container, a piece of curved branch, three small clay pots, a folded paper that turns out to contain instructions for caring for what is already there. And orchids. Six of them, in small pots, the varieties she lingered at yesterday — orange dendrobium, white phalaenopsis with pink edges, a tiny purple miniature, a yellow oncidium, a red one she doesn't know the name of, and one with a slipper-shaped pouch in deep magenta.

The facility, generous when it chooses to be, has given her the materials to make her own.

space_fan sits on the floor beside her empty aquarium with Orchid in her lap and begins to build.

[[THE BUILDING]]
She builds vertically.

The aquarium is the same dimensions as the ones from yesterday, but the design she has in her head is taller than wide — a slice of a tropical hillside, the way orchids actually grow in their natural habitat, mounted on bark and tree branches and the mossy slopes of mountains where the air is humid and the light is filtered through canopy.

She lines the back wall with moss. The moss adheres to the glass with the help of a thin substrate that the facility has provided in a small jar — a binding agent, food-safe, harmless, the kind of detail that suggests the facility has been preparing this since before space_fan was born. The moss covers the back surface in a deep green carpet, uneven, organic, the kind of texture that makes a glass tank look like the side of a cliff.

She mounts the curved branch across the top of the aquarium — the branch arching from the upper left to the upper right corner, suspended just below the open lip of the tank, the way a fallen tree might lie across a ravine in a mountain forest. The branch is real wood, weathered, the kind of branch that orchids grow on naturally.

Then the orchids.

She mounts them on the moss wall and on the branch — not in pots, in mounted positions, the roots bound with moss and attached to bark or the branch directly, the way epiphytic orchids attach to their hosts in the wild. The orange dendrobium goes on the upper left, its flowers spilling forward and down, the bright color drawing the eye like fire. The white phalaenopsis with pink edges goes on the right side, mid-height, its broad flat petals catching the room's light. The tiny purple miniature attaches to a small piece of bark in the lower right corner, almost hidden by the moss, the kind of detail you only notice if you look for it.

The yellow oncidium cascades from the branch, its small dancing flowers hanging down into the tank's interior. The red orchid she doesn't know the name of attaches to the upper middle of the moss wall, between the orange and the white, its color anchoring the composition. The slipper orchid in deep magenta sits low — almost at the floor of the tank, its pouch flower facing outward, the unusual shape of it visible through the front glass.

She adds smaller plants. Small ferns at the base — the facility provided three. Tiny moss balls scattered across the floor between the ferns. A piece of weathered stone with lichen growing on it, placed to one side. Another smaller branch as a fallen log on the floor of the tank, additional surface for moss to climb.

She works for over an hour. Orchid the cat watches from the floor beside her, occasionally batting at a piece of moss that drops, then settling back to watch with the focused attention of a creature that finds the human's hands deeply interesting.

The Wardens wake gradually. Schwalbe is up first, as always — but space_fan has been up before him today, which is unusual, which is interesting enough that he notices and doesn't immediately approach. He goes about his morning routine. Check the perimeter. Verify conditions. Then he comes to find her.

He stops at her sleeping space.

He does not say anything for a long moment.

"space_fan," he says eventually.

"Mm," she says, not looking up. She is placing the last small fern.

"This is."

"Yeah."

"This is something."

"Yeah."

Duccarian arrives. He looks at the aquarium and his face does the specific thing his face does when he encounters something that exceeds his analytical categories — the soft surprise of a mind that builds frameworks meeting something that doesn't need a framework to be understood.

"That is not what we did yesterday," Duccarian says.

"No," space_fan agrees.

"Yesterday was good. This is — this is different. This is a habitat. The orchids are mounted the way they grow."

"Yes."

Rally Mally and Orphan Annihilator arrive together. Shroud after them. Six Wardens in space_fan's sleeping space, watching her place the final pieces, Orchid purring on her lap, the aquarium taking its final form as a tropical vertical slice that has no business existing in an underground sector of a facility designed to kill them.

"It looks like outside," Rally Mally says. Quiet.

space_fan looks at it. The aquarium is finished — the orange flowers cascading from the upper left, the white blooms on the right, the purple miniature hidden, the slipper orchid low, the moss carpeting everything in deep green, the curved branch arching across the top, the small ferns at the base, the lichen stone, the fallen log, the whole composition having the specific quality of a thing that was made by someone who looked at the world before the facility and remembered what living spaces were supposed to be.

"It looks like outside," space_fan says.

Orchid purrs louder.

[[THE HUB]]
The figure's voice arrives in the sectors:

"Breakfast is served."

The warm-controlled tone is back. Not the warmth of yesterday. The neutral tone of standard operations. The day-of-the-actual-thing tone.

The Wardens leave space_fan's sleeping space reluctantly. Schwalbe goes first, the others following. space_fan goes last — she stays for an extra minute, looking at her aquarium, then picks up Orchid and carries the cat to the hub.

The food on the tables is canteen food.

Eggs. Toast. A grain. Some kind of meat that fills protein requirements without making a case for itself. The kind of breakfast that exists in institutional settings, prepared in volume, calibrated for nutrition rather than enjoyment. After yesterday's cake — the actual vanilla sponge with actual buttercream, the actual celebration food — the canteen food is jarring in a way that nobody quite expected.

Galaxy looks at his plate.

"Oh," he says.

"Yeah," Polaris says.

"It's just—"

"I know."

Eighteen people eat institutional breakfast on the day after they ate cake, and the contrast is its own kind of statement. Yesterday was the exception. Today is the rule. The facility is reasserting its categories the way it always does, and the food is the first message of the day.

space_fan eats with Orchid in her lap. She gives the cat a small piece of the meat — checking first that it's something cats can have — and Orchid eats it delicately and resumes purring.

Drug eats slowly. The aquarium beside her sleeping platform — her own composition from yesterday, the precise three-orchid arrangement — was the first thing she saw when she woke up. It is still there. The duplicate, the one in space_fan's sector, is also still there. Two aquariums, two compositions, the same arrangement existing in two places, which is something the facility does because it can.

Rabbit eats beside her. He has the Bunny's fur in his pocket today — the small bundle from Day 8, carried again, the talisman returning after a day's rest.

The Bunny is at his feet. Soup is at the Floofs' table, hovering at face height. The Drake is at the Wardens', standing tall, electric veins at moderate brightness.

The animals are watching the doorway to the challenge corridor.

This is information. The animals are positioned to see whatever comes through that door before the humans do. Their attention is forward. The Drake's wings are slightly open — not flight-ready, but not folded either. The Bunny's ears are tracking something none of them can hear.

"What do they know?" janhk asks Rabbit.

Rabbit looks at the Bunny.

*Something wrong is coming,* the Bunny says. *I can't see what yet. The shape is hidden.*

"Hidden how?"

*The room is built to hide things. Even from us.*

Rabbit relays this. The Syndicate absorbs it. Across the hub, Soup is communicating something similar to the Floofs — Ninnginni's expression goes still in the way it goes still when Soup is delivering important information. The Drake speaks to space_fan, low and electric.

*Today the room is dark. Stay near Orchid. Her senses work where ours don't.*

space_fan looks at her cat. Orchid has finished her piece of meat and is now grooming a paw.

"Dark how?"

*Without light. The animals can perceive things you cannot, but in true darkness many of those perceptions fail. Orchid is built for darkness in ways we are not.*

"Cats see in low light."

*Cats also smell what darkness contains. Orchid smells better than any of you. Today that matters.*

space_fan files this. She pets Orchid. The cat's purring continues.

The meal ends. The hub is quiet. The animals turn from the food tables and move toward the challenge corridor — all three at once, the synchronized motion of three creatures who have been waiting for the right moment, plus Orchid, who hops from space_fan's lap and trots after the Drake with the confident assumption that she belongs with the other animals because she is also an animal.

space_fan stands up to follow her. The Wardens follow space_fan. The Floofs and Syndicate follow the animals. Eighteen people and four animals walking toward a corridor, the lead position occupied by the cat that was a birthday gift yesterday and is, today, evidently essential.

"Orchid leads," Drug says. Quiet. To Rabbit.

"Orchid leads," Rabbit agrees.

[[THE CORRIDOR]]
The corridor is normal. White-lit, straight, the same architecture as Day 1's challenge access. After eleven days of corridors that did things — curved, descended, dimmed, warmed, narrowed — a normal corridor is its own kind of warning.

Halfway down it: the breeze.

Not wind. A breeze — a single soft current of air, the kind of air movement that exists in old houses when someone has opened a window in another room. It comes from ahead, blows past them gently, and pushes everyone slightly off-balance — not enough to stagger anyone, just enough that the body's autonomic balance system has to make a small correction. The kind of push that feels like someone tapping you on the shoulder from behind, or in front, or from a direction you can't quite identify.

The animals respond.

The Drake's wings open fully — not aggressive, alert. It does not lift off. The Bunny's ears go flat against its body, a posture nobody has seen it adopt before, the specific compression of an animal preparing for something unpleasant. Soup's luminescence flares brighter and then dims rapidly, the cycle that means it is reading something it doesn't like.

Orchid hisses.

The cat, who has been trotting confidently in the lead, stops dead, arches her back, and hisses at the corridor ahead. Her tail puffs to twice its normal size. Her ears flatten. The hissing is not loud — cat hissing rarely is — but the focus of it is absolute, directed at empty corridor space, at something Orchid has perceived and is naming with the only language she has.

space_fan crouches. "What is it?"

Orchid hisses again. The Drake's electric veins pulse at full brightness for a second.

The lights go out.

Not gradually. Not the dimming-toward-darkness of the orchid room. The corridor lights cut at once, and the corridor becomes black — true black, the absence of light, the kind of darkness that the eye cannot adjust to because there is nothing for it to adjust to.

The floor moves.

It is a conveyor belt. The sensation is unmistakable — the floor is sliding, smoothly, in a direction that is not exactly forward but at an angle, and eighteen people who were standing on a normal corridor are now being moved by the floor itself, their bodies in motion without their feet doing the moving.

"Don't move," Schwalbe says, but his voice is louder than he meant — the corridor has changed acoustically, the sound of his voice arriving back to him muffled, attenuated, as if the walls are absorbing what he says faster than the air can carry it.

"Don't yell," Schwalbe corrects, quieter. "The room eats sound."

The floor carries them forward and laterally. Somewhere in the darkness Orchid is hissing — the sound coming from an indeterminate direction, the cat being moved by the floor along with the humans. Rabbit reaches for the Bunny and finds nothing. Drug reaches for Rabbit's hand and the hand is not there.

Walls rise.

They come up from the floor — fast, but not violently — partitions extending from the floor upward, dividing the moving floor into channels, and each channel has a person in it, and the channels separate, and within seconds eighteen people are eighteen people in eighteen separate moving compartments, the floor still sliding, the darkness still total.

Then the floor stops.

Eighteen people stand in eighteen separate spots in absolute darkness, the compartment walls having retracted just before the stop, leaving each of them in an open space whose dimensions they cannot perceive.

The figure's voice.

"Welcome to the maze."

[[THE RULES]]
"You have been distributed throughout a single dark room with maze geometry. You will find each other. There are no teams today. There are eighteen people in this room and you must locate every one of them — all of them, not most, not nearly all. Eighteen humans found, and the challenge ends."

A pause.

"Six hours. After six hours, anyone not yet found is eliminated."

Polaris is breathing.

Just breathing. In and out. The darkness is total. She cannot see the walls. She cannot see her hand in front of her face. Her ankle is tender from the conveyor belt's awkward motion and the tenderness combined with the darkness combined with the unknown dimensions of the space combined with the sudden absence of the eight Floofs she has been beside for twelve days produces a specific feeling that has been building since the lights cut off and is now arriving in full: the walls are close. The space is small. She cannot see the space but she knows it. She knows it the way bodies know things. The room is closing on her.

It is not. The room is not closing. The room is what it is.

Polaris's chest tightens anyway. Her breathing goes shallow. Her hands reach out and find nothing — no walls, no people, no edges — and the not-finding makes the closing-feeling worse, because if she could feel a wall she could measure the space, and what she cannot measure is what will hurt her.

She kneels. Not from the ankle. From the body's instinct to make itself smaller, lower, less of a target for whatever the closing room intends.

"The rules," the figure continues. "Be found or die. Do not push others into traps — there are traps in this maze, and pushing someone into one is grounds for disqualification of both pusher and pushed. Do not yell or speak loudly. The walls absorb sound. They absorb it specifically. If you are loud, your voice will not carry — but you will draw the attention of the walls themselves, and the walls will become traps. Speak softly. Move slowly. Find each other. Six hours begins now."

The voice ends.

The darkness continues.

[[THE EARLY HOURS]]
For the first ten minutes, almost nobody moves.

The body needs time to accept the conditions. The eyes try to adjust to darkness and fail and try again and fail. The ears extend their range to compensate and find that the extension is also useless — the walls are eating sound, the way the figure said, and even the breathing of nearby people is muffled by the time it crosses any distance.

space_fan stands still. She holds out her hands. Nothing on either side. She takes a step forward — the floor is solid now, no longer moving, the same hard surface as the hub. Another step. Her outstretched hand touches a wall. The wall is smooth. She runs her hand along it. The wall continues. There is texture but no obvious pattern.

Orchid.

space_fan can hear Orchid. Faintly, at the edge of perception, the cat's breathing — different from a human's, faster, lighter — and somewhere in the darkness Orchid is also moving, because Orchid is a cat in the dark and Orchid is at home in conditions that have just rendered every human helpless.

space_fan crouches. Holds out her hand low, palm up, the way you call a cat when you can't see it. She doesn't make a sound — the rules said no loud noises, and she is not sure how soft is soft enough, so she does not even click her tongue.

Orchid arrives.

The cat brushes against space_fan's hand within twenty seconds. space_fan's fingers find the small body, the soft fur, the three black stripes she can feel even though she can't see them. Orchid butts her head against space_fan's palm and then immediately turns and walks a few steps away, looks back — space_fan can sense rather than see this — and waits.

She wants space_fan to follow.

space_fan follows.

[[THE SEARCHERS]]
Rabbit knows the Bunny is somewhere. Not nearby — the Bunny was on the conveyor with him but was carried in a different direction by the random distribution of the maze. He knows this because the Bunny is silent in his head — the communication channel exists but is empty, the way a phone line is empty when the other person isn't speaking.

He does what he knows. Hands out. Walls to one side. He tracks the wall slowly, moving along it at a steady pace, the methodical search of someone who has decided that systematic coverage is better than guessing.

Drug works alone. She has been alone the most often of any of them — alone with grief, alone with observation, alone with the calm that the rooms read as neutrality. The dark is not unfamiliar. She moves through it carefully, hands out, the mental map building as she walks: corner here, intersection there, dead end. She has CharliSpirit's mapping instinct, it turns out, just at a slower pace and to less precise specifications.

She finds the first person.

Schwalbe. He is moving along the same wall as her, in the opposite direction, and they meet in the middle. Their hands touch first — Drug's outstretched fingers meeting Schwalbe's. They both freeze. Then Drug speaks, very softly:

"Drug."

"Schwalbe."

"Together."

"Together."

Two people. The challenge requires eighteen.

[[THE FIRST DEATHS]]
Elv is moving carefully.

They have been the most careful person in the maze since the rules were announced. Their watchful instinct, the orientation toward the door, the specific paranoia that has kept them alive for twelve days — all of it has activated, and Elv is moving through the dark with the slowest, most deliberate pace anyone in the maze is using. They check every step before placing weight. They run a hand along the walls. They listen for sounds that they know aren't going to come because the walls eat them.

The trap is in the floor.

Elv's foot finds it — a panel that depresses, a slight click, a moment where the entire body registers wrong before the mind can catch up — and they react fast, pulling their weight back, but the trap has already triggered. The floor opens beneath them. The space below has no light, the way the maze has no light, but the falling is unmistakable — the body's relationship to gravity changing, the specific terror of an empty space below where a floor was supposed to be.

Elv falls.

They do not yell. The instinct to yell is suppressed by twelve days of training and the specific awareness that yelling is also a death — not a punishment, but a separate path to the same end. They fall in silence. The floor closes above them.

The maze is unchanged. The other seventeen people do not know that Elv is gone. The information will arrive at the end, when the count is taken, and not before.

NinjaFlout is moving fast. He has been the team's geometry-tracker, the one who calls grid positions, and his instinct in a maze is to move quickly to gather data — to find walls, intersections, the structure of the space. He moves faster than Elv. He is more confident in the dark.

He finds his trap by stepping on it directly.

The mechanism is the same. The floor opens. NinjaFlout falls. He has the instant to understand what is happening — the geometric tracker noting the precise dimensions of his own falling — and then he is gone.

Sixteen.

[[THE FINDING]]
Orchid leads space_fan to Schwalbe and Drug.

She finds them by smell — the specific scents that humans carry, that cats can identify across distances, that the maze's walls do not absorb because the walls are not designed for that kind of sensing. Orchid trots ahead, stops, waits for space_fan to catch up, and trots forward again. The path she chooses is not the path space_fan would have chosen. It involves doubling back twice. Orchid knows the way and space_fan doesn't and the trust required is the same trust space_fan has placed in the cat since the morning, when the cat was a small thing in her arms and now is a guide in pure darkness.

When they meet Schwalbe and Drug, the four of them join — three humans and one cat, hands meeting in the dark, the specific quiet of a small group that has just become a slightly larger group.

"Orchid finds people," space_fan says, very quietly.

"How," Drug says.

"Smell. The Drake said her senses work where ours don't."

"Then she leads."

Orchid leads.

She finds Rabbit next. Rabbit has been searching for an hour and has found nothing — his methodical wall-following has not crossed paths with anyone, and the absence of the Bunny in his head is a low-grade distress that he is managing but feeling. Orchid finds him by smell, stops in front of him, and waits for space_fan's group to arrive.

When Rabbit reaches them, his hand finds space_fan's first.

"How are you doing this?" he asks.

"Orchid," space_fan says.

"Orchid."

"She finds people. She knows where everyone is."

Rabbit absorbs this. Five people now, plus the cat.

[[THE TRAP NEAR SHROUD]]
Orchid leads them to Polaris next.

Polaris is in worse shape than anyone they've found. She is on the floor, kneeling, breathing in the slow controlled way of someone who is trying to manage a panic attack with technique and limited success. The walls around her — she has found them, they are close, the space she's in is genuinely smaller than other parts of the maze — and the closeness is the thing she has been negotiating with for the entire first hour. Her ankle is throbbing. Her chest is tight. Her hands are flat on the floor.

space_fan reaches her. Touches her shoulder.

Polaris flinches, then exhales, then leans into the touch.

"It's space_fan."

"It's small," Polaris says, very quietly. "It's so small here. The walls."

"I know," space_fan says. "We're here. Can you stand?"

"I don't know."

"Try."

Polaris tries. With space_fan's hand on her shoulder and Schwalbe's hand under her arm, she stands. The standing is bad. The ankle protests. The ribcage is still tight. But she stands, and the small group becomes six.

Orchid leads them on.

[[THE GROUP GROWS]]
By the third hour they are eleven.

Space_fan and Orchid in the lead. Schwalbe and Drug as a kind of inner circle, the people who joined first and have been steady. Rabbit. Polaris, leaning slightly on space_fan when needed. Then Duccarian, found in a corner where he had been thinking about the maze rather than searching it, the philosophical instinct activating in the absence of light. Then Rally Mally and Orphan Annihilator, who had found each other early — the persistent pair, together as always — and were moving slowly together when Orchid scented them.

Then janhk, who had been navigating by texture, and Lyrix, who had been going slowly, and CharliSpirit, who had been sitting still because the geometry was unmappable and trying to map it had been the failure of the altered rooms, and CharliSpirit had decided that this maze was a similar lesson and had simply waited for someone to find her.

Eleven. Seven still missing.

Orchid hisses.

Not at empty space this time — at a section of wall that turns out to have a trap embedded in it, a panel that the cat has identified by smell as wrong. The group routes around it. Polaris steps especially carefully past the section. The cat is correcting them in real time, leading them safely through a maze that has eaten two people already, though the group does not know this yet.

[[SHROUD]]
They find Shroud in the fifth hour.

Or — they almost find Shroud. Orchid leads them down a corridor that smells like Shroud is at the end of it. The corridor is straight. Orchid walks faster than usual, the urgency of a cat that has identified a target close. space_fan keeps pace.

They hear something.

A sound — small, near the edge of audibility, the walls eating most of it but enough getting through to register. The sound of someone breathing fast. The sound of someone moving.

The figure's voice arrives:

"Time check. Five hours and forty-five minutes elapsed. Fifteen minutes remaining."

Fifteen minutes for seven more people. Orchid breaks into a run. space_fan runs after her. The group runs as best they can in darkness, hands extended, voices silent.

Orchid stops.

Ahead, at the end of the corridor — there is a door. Or what functions as one. A demarcation in the maze, the edge of a section, the place where Shroud is on one side and they are on the other. The cat hisses at the door. The smell of Shroud is just beyond it.

Shroud is also running. They can hear him — the muffled approach of a person moving fast through the maze, sound being eaten but enough getting through to confirm presence.

space_fan reaches the door first. She finds the handle by feel. She turns it.

The door does not open immediately. There is a mechanism — a delay, the specific resistance of a door that requires a moment to disengage. space_fan turns the handle harder. The mechanism clicks. The door is opening.

On the other side, Shroud is two meters away. They can hear his footsteps now, clear, the door's threshold thinner than the maze's walls.

The figure's voice:

"Three."

Shroud is one meter away.

"Two."

He is at the door. His hand is on the other side of the handle. space_fan can feel the pressure of his grip through the metal.

"One."

The door opens.

The figure's voice:

"Time."

Shroud stands in the doorway. His hand is on the handle. space_fan's hand is on the handle.

The door is open.

Shroud is not on space_fan's side of the door yet.

"Shroud is one tenth of a second late," the figure says. "Five hours and fifty-nine minutes and fifty-nine point nine seconds. The threshold was six hours exactly. Shroud has not been found in time."

space_fan's hand is still on the handle. Shroud is still in the doorway, frozen, the precise moment of his too-late arrival caught in the position he was running into. His face — visible now, because the dim emergency lighting has activated with the time call — has the specific expression of someone who can see the people who would have saved him from the wrong side of an arbitrary line.

"I made it," he says.

"I know," space_fan says.

The floor beneath Shroud opens. The same mechanism. He goes down without struggling — the specific dignity of someone who came that close and understood that close was not enough, who does not waste his last seconds arguing with the math.

The door closes.

Thirteen people in the maze. Thirteen, plus the count of those eliminated.

[[THE COUNT]]
The lights come up — not full lighting, but enough. The maze becomes visible. It is smaller than they thought. The walls are closer than they thought. The dimensions have been compressed by the darkness into something that felt vast and is, in fact, ordinary.

The figure's voice:

"Thirteen found. Five unfound."

The math is done in thirteen heads simultaneously.

"Elv is missing," Ninnginni says. He is in the group — Orchid found him in the fourth hour, asleep against a wall, exhaustion having taken him before any trap could.

"NinjaFlout," Rabbit says.

"Galaxy," CharliSpirit says.

"Godly_Aura," Polaris says.

"Shroud," Schwalbe says.

"That's five," Drug says.

Thirteen people look at each other in the dim light, counting the people present, the names they just spoke landing in the room one by one. Five gone. Thirteen remaining.

"Galaxy. Godly_Aura. Both Floofs," Ninnginni says. "We're at three Floofs."

"Three," space_fan confirms.

The count settles. Six Floofs going in: CharliSpirit, Galaxy, Godly_Aura, Polaris, Elv, Ninnginni. Three remain: CharliSpirit, Polaris, Ninnginni. Galaxy and Godly_Aura are gone. Elv is gone. The Floofs are three.

"Three Floofs," Ninnginni says.

The figure's voice:

"Five eliminated. Elv and NinjaFlout encountered traps within the first hour. Galaxy and Godly_Aura were not located within the time limit. Shroud was located zero point one seconds past the time limit. Thirteen survivors. Return to your sectors."

The door at the end of the corridor opens. The path back is normal — a regular corridor, regular lighting, the kind of return path that the facility offers when the day is over.

[[RETURN]]
They walk back slowly.

Thirteen people and four animals and one cat. The Drake limps slightly — something happened to it during the maze, something it doesn't communicate about, the specific quiet of an animal that has been bruised and is processing it privately. Soup glows at very low brightness. The Bunny has rejoined Rabbit and is walking at his side, ears slightly down.

Orchid walks beside space_fan. The cat is tired. Her purring has stopped. She has done the work of finding people for six hours and the work shows in her gait — the small body moving slower than it moved this morning, the cat conserving the energy she has left.

space_fan picks her up in the corridor. Orchid leans into her chest immediately and resumes purring, low and quiet, the specific sound of a cat that is being carried by her person after a long day.

The hub is dim. The dorms are open. Thirteen people enter their sectors with the specific weight of a day that has eaten a third of them.

[[DORMS]]
The Wardens' sector is five.

Schwalbe, Duccarian, Rally Mally, Orphan Annihilator, space_fan. Shroud's sleeping space is empty. The aquarium he made in the orchid room is still beside his platform. The orchids are still alive. He is not.

space_fan sits on her sleeping platform with Orchid in her lap and looks at her own aquarium — the vertical tropical hillside she built this morning, before the maze, before any of this — and the contrast is enormous. The aquarium is alive and beautiful and sits beside her platform like a small piece of a different world, and on the other side of the dorm, Shroud's empty space contains an aquarium that he made and won't see again.

Orchid stops purring.

The cat is asleep in space_fan's lap, body curled into the smallest configuration, exhausted from finding people in the dark for six hours. space_fan strokes her gently. The cat's breathing is slow and steady. The day has been long for everyone.

The Floofs are three.

Ninnginni sits in the central room. CharliSpirit is on the floor near him. Polaris is on her sleeping platform, her ankle elevated, her eyes closed. Three Floofs in a sector designed for ten. Soup descends to face height in front of Ninnginni. The luminescence is at the lowest register Ninnginni has ever seen.

"Three," Ninnginni says.

*Three,* Soup confirms.

"Yesterday was a birthday."

*Yes.*

"And today—"

*And today,* Soup says.

Ninnginni does not finish the sentence. The sentence is finished by the room. By the absence of Galaxy and Godly_Aura and Elv. By the silence in the sector that used to have ten voices in it and now has three.

The Syndicate is five.

Drug and Rabbit and JIKOSU and janhk and Lyrix — Lyrix is still here, NinjaFlout is not. Six going in. Five out.

Drug sits on her platform. The aquarium beside her — her composition, the precise three-orchid arrangement, the empty space participating — looks back at her. She made it for space_fan's birthday yesterday. Yesterday was less than a day ago. Yesterday feels like a different timeline.

Rabbit sits across from her. The Bunny is between them. Three figures in a configuration that has held since Day 8 — the constants, the people who have not lost themselves to fear or curiosity or trap.

"Thirteen," Rabbit says.

"Thirteen," Drug says.

"Yesterday was eighteen. Now it's thirteen."

"Yes."

They sit with this. The yesterdays in this facility are short — measured in the gap between celebration and the next thing, which is always something. The cake was real. The cat is real. The orchid is real. The dark maze is also real. The traps are real. The not-finding is real.

space_fan in the Wardens' sector strokes Orchid in her sleep. The cat continues to breathe slowly. The aquarium beside the platform glows softly under the dim lighting — the orange flowers, the white blooms with pink edges, the purple miniature, the slipper orchid low, the moss carpeting everything. A vertical slice of a world that exists somewhere outside this one.

She built it this morning. This morning was twelve hours ago.

"I'm sorry," she says, to the cat, to the aquarium, to the empty sleeping space across the room where Shroud should be. "I'm sorry I built something so happy on a day this bad."

The aquarium does not respond. The cat sleeps. The day ends.
