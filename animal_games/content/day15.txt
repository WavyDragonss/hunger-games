[[DAY]]
DAY 15 — THE HUNT

[[SYSTEM_STATE]]
Day index: 15.
Participant count: 8.
Challenge type: pursuit — survival against active hunters, single enclosed environment.
Lethality: maximum. Team structure dissolved upon completion.
Animal presence: none during the hunt.

[[NARRATIVE]]
The morning is quiet.

Eight people wake in three sectors that are mostly empty now — the Wardens' sector holding four, the Floofs' holding three, the Syndicate's holding one. The thinning has reached the point where the sectors feel less like dormitories and more like the rooms of a house after most of the family has moved out, the same furniture in the same places but the proportion of presence to absence inverted.

JIKOSU wakes alone.

This is new. For fourteen days the Syndicate's sector held more than one person, and JIKOSU woke to the sounds of a team — Drug's quiet movement, Rabbit's translation of the Bunny's morning communications, janhk's texture-checking of the day's first surfaces. This morning the sector is silent. The Bunny is not here — it stayed with Rabbit's body in the elevator yesterday and has not returned. JIKOSU wakes in a room built for ten that contains one, and he lies still for a moment, doing the inventory that his body does every morning: systems check, situation assessment, the tactical mind cataloguing the conditions before committing to the day.

The condition is: he is the last of the Syndicate.

He gets up. He stretches — the methodical sequence, each muscle group, the ritual that has survived every challenge. He does it alone. The stretching looks the same. It feels different.

space_fan wakes with Orchid against her chest.

The cat returned last night — appeared under the blanket after two days of absence, grooming herself as if she had never left — and space_fan slept the deepest sleep she has had since the facility took her, the small warm weight of the cat anchoring her to something that was not loss. She wakes and Orchid is still there. The cat opens one eye, registers that space_fan is awake, and resumes purring.

"Good morning," space_fan says.

Orchid purrs.

The aquarium beside the platform glows in the morning register — the impossible aquarium, the two-weeks-in-one-sleep growth, the orchids continuing their slow expansion across the moss wall. space_fan looks at it. She looks at Schwalbe's empty platform across the room. She looks at Orchid.

Three Wardens remain besides her. Duccarian, who processes loss through philosophy. Rally Mally and Orphan Annihilator, the persistent pair, together since Day 6. Four Wardens. The most of any team. The Wardens have lost the fewest — a fact that nobody says out loud because saying it out loud feels like inviting correction.

Polaris wakes in the Floofs' sector.

Her ankle is bad this morning. It has been bad every morning since Day 6, but the badness varies, and today it is at the high end — the joint stiff and hot, the specific protest of a body part that has been compensated for so long that the compensation has become its own injury. She tests it. It holds her weight. It complains. She accepts the complaint and stands anyway.

Three Floofs. CharliSpirit, who lost her mapping instinct in the altered rooms and has been rebuilding a different kind of seeing ever since. Ninnginni, who has been carrying Loyal2Schwab's junction mark since Day 8 and is the last leader of a team that started with ten. And Polaris herself — the one with the ankle, the underdog, the person who has survived fourteen days of physical challenges with a body that should have failed her on Day 6.

She does not think of herself as a survivor. She thinks of herself as someone who hasn't died yet. The distinction matters to her even if it matters to no one else.

[[THE LAST BREAKFAST]]
The hub is normal.

Standard lighting. The tables up. The food is good — not the German feast of yesterday, not the cake of the birthday, but good, solid, the kind of breakfast the facility produces when it is not making a point about generosity or scarcity. Eggs. Bread. A grain. Fruit. The numbered tags are present — violet, silver, red — though with eight people across three teams, the tags feel almost ceremonial, the categories maintained past the point where the categories mean much.

Eight people eat.

The animals are present at the edges. The Drake at the Wardens' table, electric veins at moderate brightness. Soup at the Floofs', hovering at face height. The Bunny is — the Bunny is here. It returned overnight. It is at the Syndicate's table, beside JIKOSU, the only Syndicate member left, and the Bunny's presence beside him is the first acknowledgment JIKOSU has received that he is still part of something even though the something is mostly gone.

"You came back," JIKOSU says, quietly.

The Bunny's red eyes hold the six-second-ahead distance. *Rabbit is gone. You are the Syndicate now. I stay with the Syndicate.*

"I'm the whole Syndicate."

*Then I stay with you.*

JIKOSU eats. The Bunny sits beside him. The tactical mind, which has spent fourteen days reading situations, reads this one: the last challenge thinned them to eight, the teams are nearly dissolved, the animals are repositioning — the Bunny moving from Rabbit to JIKOSU, the inheritance of leadership passing down the line as the line shortens. The pattern suggests an ending approaching. He does not know what the ending is. He knows the shape of it is close.

space_fan eats with Orchid in her lap. The cat is calm this morning, restored, the two-day absence apparently forgotten. space_fan gives her a piece of egg. Orchid eats it delicately.

The Drake communicates to space_fan: *Today the animals do not come.*

space_fan looks at the Drake. "What do you mean?"

*The challenge today does not permit us. We cannot enter. You will be alone — all of you, without us. The first time since Day 1 that we cannot help.*

"Why?"

*Because today is a hunt. And the hunters do not allow the animals near.*

"What hunters?"

The Drake's electric veins dim. *You will see.*

space_fan looks at Orchid. The cat looks back, calm, unaware of the conversation happening over her head.

"What about Orchid?" space_fan asks. "She's not a spirit animal. She's just a cat."

The Drake considers this. *I don't know. Orchid is not bound the way we are bound. The rules that exclude us may not exclude her. But I cannot promise. Keep her close. Decide for yourself.*

space_fan tightens her hold on Orchid slightly. The cat does not object.

The figure's voice arrives.

"Good morning. I hope you slept well."

The warm-controlled tone. But there is something underneath it today that has not been there before — not exhaustion, not warmth, not the standard weight. Anticipation. The figure sounds like someone who has been waiting a long time for a specific day and the day has arrived.

"Finish your breakfast. Today is different from every day that came before it. Today, I will be joining you."

Eight people stop eating.

"Today," the figure says, "you meet me."

[[THE REVEAL]]
The tables descend.

Not at the figure's command — on their own, the meal cut short, the food withdrawn into the floor before anyone has finished. Eight people stand in the hub. The animals are at the edges, and as the tables descend, the animals move — not toward the humans, away, toward the corridors, the Drake and Soup and the Bunny retreating to the perimeter of the hub with the specific motion of creatures that have been told to clear a space.

The lighting changes.

Not dimming. Focusing. The diffuse hub light contracts into a single bright column at the center of the room, a spotlight on an empty patch of floor, and the rest of the hub falls into relative shadow.

A figure walks into the light.

Not a projection. Not a voice from the ceiling. A person — a physical person, walking from one of the corridors into the spotlit center of the hub, and eight people watch the figure they have heard for fifteen days acquire a body for the first time.

He is young. That is the first surprise. The voice has always carried an ageless authority, the warm-controlled neutrality of something that could be any age, and the body that arrives is young — a teenager, or barely past it, dressed plainly, moving with the slightly self-conscious gait of someone who is aware of being watched and is choosing to walk anyway.

He stops in the center of the light. He looks at the eight people. He smiles — and the smile is the same smile from Easter, the genuine one, the expression of someone who has prepared something and is pleased to finally present it.

"Hello," he says. The voice matches. It is the figure's voice, coming from this young person's mouth, the disjunction of it landing in eight stomachs at once. "My name is WavyDragons. I built this place. I built all of it — the challenges, the rooms, the rules, the animals. I've been watching you since Day One. And today, I'm going to hunt you."

The hub is silent.

"I want to be honest with you, because we've come a long way together and you've earned honesty. Today is the second-to-last day. Tomorrow is the finale. To get to the finale, you have to survive today. And today is the hardest thing I've ever asked you to do."

He gestures. Behind him, two more figures walk into the light.

The first makes Polaris's breath catch.

It is Schwalbe. Or — not Schwalbe. The face is close. The build is close. But the details are wrong — the jaw slightly different, the eyes a different color, the way of standing similar but not identical. It is someone who looks like Schwalbe the way a brother looks like a brother, because that is what he is.

"This is alex_awesomeness," WavyDragons says. "He's Schwalbe's brother. He asked to come here — to see the place his brother died, and to participate in the end of it. I let him." A pause. "He's good at finding people. And he has a reason to be thorough."

alex_awesomeness says nothing at first. His eyes move across the eight survivors and stop on the Wardens — on the team his brother belonged to, the team that watched Schwalbe fall into a wheel on Day 13. His expression is not cruel. It is something worse than cruel. It is grief that has been given a task. When he finally speaks, his voice is low and even and carries the same flat steadiness that Schwalbe's voice carried.

"My brother died here," he says. "I want to understand what kind of place does that. And I want to be the one holding the knife when it's over."

The word knife sits in the hub. None of the previous challenges have involved a knife. The facility's deaths have been clean — floors opening, lights, mechanisms. A knife is a different promise.

The second figure steps into the light, and the hub makes a sound — a collective intake of breath, eight people responding to the same impossibility at once.

It is Rabbit.

"Rabbit died yesterday," space_fan says. Her voice is flat with disbelief. "We watched him die. The red light. The elevator."

"You watched him be eliminated," WavyDragons says. "Elimination is not always death. Sometimes it is recruitment." He looks at Rabbit. "Rabbit volunteered. When he pressed the button yesterday, he knew. He chose to die for his team because dying for his team meant joining mine. The sacrifice was real. The death was not."

Rabbit looks at the survivors. His face is the same face — the level, unchanging face that translated the Bunny for fifteen days. But there is something different in it now. He is on the other side of something.

Polaris is staring at Rabbit.

She and Rabbit were never close — different teams, different orbits — but Rabbit was a constant, one of the leaders, one of the people whose presence defined the structure of the facility. Watching him die yesterday was watching a pillar fall. Watching him stand here now, alive, on the side of the hunters, is something her mind cannot immediately process. The pillar fell and then stood back up facing the wrong direction.

"Why," Polaris says. To Rabbit specifically. "Why would you—"

"Because the alternative was staying dead," Rabbit says. His voice is quiet. "And I'm not ready to be dead." A pause. "I'm sorry, Polaris. I'm sorry to all of you. But I'm going to hunt you today, and I'm going to be good at it, because being good at it is the only way I get to keep existing."

The honesty of this is the worst part. He is not pretending to be conflicted. He is conflicted and hunting anyway.

[[THE RULES]]
"Here are the rules," WavyDragons says.

"You have twelve hours. For eleven of those hours, you hide. For the final hour, we hunt. The environment is a single enclosed space — large, complex, full of places to hide and places to be found. The three of us will hunt you during the final hour. We will be thorough."

He lets this settle.

"The animals cannot help you. They are not permitted in the hunting ground. For the first time since Day One, you are alone — no Drake, no Soup, no Bunny. Just yourselves and the space and us."

"What's the win condition?" JIKOSU asks. Tactical, always.

"Survive the hour. When the twelfth hour ends, anyone still alive has won. Anyone we catch has not." WavyDragons's smile returns, smaller now. "I'll tell you honestly: most of you will not survive. The hunting ground is designed for us. The final hour is designed for us. But survival is possible. Three of you surviving is — let's say it's the number I'm expecting. Maybe fewer."

"Three," Ninnginni says. "Out of eight."

"Three out of eight," WavyDragons confirms. "The teams end today regardless. Whoever survives walks into tomorrow as an individual. No more Wardens. No more Floofs. No more Syndicate. Tomorrow is one against one against one — or however many of you there are. The teams were always temporary. Today is where they end."

He gestures toward a corridor that has opened behind him — wide, brightly lit, leading somewhere.

"The hunting ground is through there. You have eleven hours to hide before we come for you. I suggest you use them well." A pause. "Eleven hours starting when you walk through that door. Go whenever you're ready. The clock starts with the first step."

Eight people look at the corridor. At WavyDragons. At the two hunters beside him. At Rabbit, who will not meet their eyes now. At alex_awesomeness, who is meeting all of their eyes, one by one, the way you study things you intend to find later.

"One more thing," WavyDragons says. "I've genuinely enjoyed building this for you. I know that's a strange thing to say to people I'm about to hunt. But it's true. You've been better than I expected. All of you. I mean that." A beat. "Now run."

[[THE HUNTING GROUND]]
They walk through the door.

The space on the other side is enormous.

It takes a moment to understand what they are looking at, because the scale is wrong for an underground facility — the ceiling is high, the floor extends in every direction, and the layout is not corridors or chambers but something domestic, something commercial, something that eight people recognize from a life none of them have mentioned in fifteen days.

It is a furniture store.

Not a small one. A vast one — the kind that occupies the footprint of several buildings, organized into a winding showroom path that leads through staged room after staged room. Living rooms. Bedrooms. Kitchens. Home offices. Each one fully furnished, decorated, lit with the warm domestic lighting of a place designed to make you imagine living in it. The path winds through them in the deliberately disorienting way these stores are designed — no straight lines, no obvious exits, the route folding back on itself so that you pass through every department whether you want to or not.

There are signs. Yellow and blue. Arrows on the floor. Product names in a typeface that none of them can read — the letters are letters but the words are not words, the language of the signs sliding away from comprehension the way the words in space_fan's notebook slid away.

"It's an IKEA," CharliSpirit says. The name arrives from somewhere — a memory, a life before, the specific commercial cathedral that exists in every city. "It's a giant IKEA."

"Why," Rally Mally says.

"Because it's the perfect hunting ground," Duccarian says. He is already analyzing it — the philosophical mind turned tactical by necessity. "Maximum hiding places. Maximum disorientation. The layout is designed to keep you moving through it, to prevent you from finding the exit, to make you pass every display. For shopping, that's marketing. For hunting, it's a kill box."

"There have to be exits," JIKOSU says. "Fire codes. Loading docks. Staff areas."

"There are no fire codes here," Duccarian says. "This is a replica of an IKEA. It is not an IKEA. The exits exist where WavyDragons decided they exist, which may be nowhere."

Eight people stand at the entrance to a furniture store that goes on forever, lit warm and domestic and wrong, and the clock has started.

[[THE ELEVEN HOURS]]
They split.

There is no discussion about whether to split — the logic is immediate and shared. A group is easier to find than individuals. A group moves slower, makes more noise, presents a larger target. Eight people in one place is eight people the hunters find at once. Eight people in eight places is eight separate problems for three hunters to solve.

The teams dissolve in the splitting. Not by announcement — by physics. The Wardens scatter into the showroom. The Floofs scatter. JIKOSU, already alone, goes his own way. By the end of the first hour, eight people are eight points distributed across an enormous fake furniture store, and the team structure that has defined them for fifteen days has quietly stopped existing.

JIKOSU goes high.

His tactical instinct says: the hunters will search at eye level, at human level, at the level where people hide. So he goes up — into the warehouse section, the towering shelves of flat-packed furniture, the industrial racking that rises toward the high ceiling. He climbs into a gap between stacked boxes on a high shelf, three meters up, and arranges boxes around himself until he is invisible from below. He has water — there are display kitchens with functioning taps. He has positioned himself near enough to a display kitchen to reach water during the eleven hours and high enough to be hidden during the final one.

It is a good position. He knows it is a good position. He has spent fifteen days being tactical and the position is the product of all of it.

space_fan goes low, and she goes with Orchid.

The cat came with her through the door — the Drake said the rules might not exclude her, and they did not, Orchid trotting through the entrance alongside space_fan as if a giant IKEA were a perfectly normal place for a cat to be. space_fan has decided that Orchid is an advantage. The cat's senses work where human senses fail. In the dark maze on Day 12, Orchid found people by smell. In a hunting ground, the same sense that finds people can avoid them.

space_fan finds a children's bedroom display — a small room staged with a child's bed, a wardrobe, a toy box. She gets into the wardrobe. It is large enough for a person who folds herself correctly. Orchid gets in with her. space_fan leaves the wardrobe door open a crack — enough to see, enough to breathe, enough for Orchid to monitor the air.

The plan is simple: when the hunt begins, Orchid will tell her when a hunter is near. The cat's body language — the flattened ears, the puffed tail, the hiss she gave the corridor on Day 12 — is a warning system more sensitive than anything space_fan's own body can produce. space_fan will read the cat and move accordingly.

Polaris cannot go high. Her ankle forbids it.

She cannot run far either — the ankle limits her range, her speed, her ability to relocate quickly if a hiding spot is compromised. So Polaris does the thing her limitation forces her to do: she finds the best single hiding place she can and commits to it completely, because she will not get a second one. If she is found, she cannot flee. The first hiding place is the only hiding place.

She chooses a bedroom display with a bed that has storage drawers underneath — the large kind, deep enough for a person. She gets into the largest drawer. She closes it most of the way. She arranges a folded blanket from the bed display over the gap. She lies in the dark in a drawer under a bed in a fake bedroom in a fake IKEA, her ankle finally elevated and resting, and she waits.

The eleven hours pass slowly.

Duccarian spends them thinking. He has found a home office display — a desk, a chair, a bookshelf — and he sits in the chair and considers the situation philosophically, because that is how Duccarian metabolizes fear. He concludes several things: that the hunt is unwinnable through hiding alone, that the hunters will eventually find every static position given enough time, that survival therefore requires not staying hidden but staying mobile, anticipating the hunters' search pattern and moving counter to it. It is a good theory. It requires the ability to move quickly and read the hunters in real time. He is not sure he has either. But the theory is sound.

Rally Mally and Orphan Annihilator break the splitting rule.

They stay together. They know they shouldn't — a pair is more findable than two individuals — but Orphan Annihilator's ankle is nearly as bad as Polaris's, and Rally Mally has been helping him since Day 6, and the habit of helping is stronger than the logic of splitting. They find a kitchen display with a large pantry cabinet and they both fit inside it, pressed together in the dark, and they decide that whatever happens, it happens to both of them, because that is what the last fifteen days have made them.

CharliSpirit maps.

The mapping instinct, broken in the altered rooms, has been recovering, and a giant IKEA is exactly the kind of space the mapping instinct loves — complex, structured, full of patterns. She spends the eleven hours walking the showroom carefully, building the complete map in her head, every winding turn and every display and every shortcut between departments. She does not pick a hiding place. Her plan is to use the map — to know the space so completely that she can stay ahead of the hunters, always moving to the section they have already cleared, using her knowledge of the layout as a weapon against people who do not know it as well as she does.

Ninnginni waits.

He is the last leader of the Floofs and he spends the eleven hours thinking about the nine people who used to be his team. He finds a quiet corner — a storage area behind a display, a gap the showroom path doesn't reach — and he sits with Loyal2Schwab's junction mark in his hand and he does the thing leaders do at the end, which is account for the people they led. Loyal2Schwab. Golden. lumallie. Galaxy. Godly_Aura. Revenant. Elv. CharliSpirit and Polaris still alive, somewhere in the store. He holds the mark and he waits for the hour.

[[THE HUNT BEGINS]]
The lights change.

The warm domestic lighting of the showroom shifts — not to darkness, but to something colder, dimmer, the emergency-register lighting the facility uses when it wants people afraid. The fake windows in the fake room displays go dark. The product signs dim. The vast space becomes a shadowed labyrinth of furniture, the staged rooms now reading less like homes and more like the abandoned sets of homes, places where people used to pretend to live.

WavyDragons's voice, everywhere:

"The eleventh hour is over. The hunt begins now. You have one hour. Good luck."

Then silence.

The hunters enter.

They do not announce themselves. They come in through the entrance — three figures spreading into the showroom, each taking a different vector, the coordinated movement of people who planned this. WavyDragons. alex_awesomeness. Rabbit. They move quietly. They move with purpose. They move like people who know the space and know the people hiding in it do not.

alex_awesomeness is carrying the knife. He said he would. It is in his right hand as he walks, a long fixed-blade thing that catches the cold light, and he carries it the way his brother carried the responsibility of going first — as a tool, as a fact, as a thing that exists to be used.

[[DUCCARIAN]]
Duccarian's theory is correct and it kills him.

He has decided that survival requires mobility — that static hiding fails and counter-movement succeeds. So when the hunt begins, he leaves his home office display and begins to move, tracking the hunters' likely search pattern, trying to stay in the sections they have already cleared.

The theory works for twenty minutes. He moves through the showroom ahead of the search, reading the hunters' positions from the sounds they make, staying one department behind them, occupying the cleared space the way his theory predicted.

The flaw in the theory is that there are three hunters and they do not search in a single line.

WavyDragons does not follow a pattern. He moves through the showroom the way the architect of a space moves through it — taking shortcuts that don't exist on the showroom path, passing through displays rather than around them, appearing in sections the search pattern said were clear. Duccarian moves into a cleared living room display and WavyDragons is already there, sitting in an armchair, waiting, because WavyDragons knew that a clever person would try exactly what Duccarian is trying and positioned himself accordingly.

Duccarian turns to run.

He is not fast. He has never been fast — the body of a thinker, not a runner — and the twenty minutes of careful movement have not prepared him for the sudden need for speed. He gets three steps before WavyDragons is on him, and WavyDragons is fast, unnaturally fast, the speed of someone who built the rules and is not entirely bound by them.

The thing WavyDragons does is quick. There is a pressure at the base of Duccarian's skull — a hand, finding a specific point, the practiced precision of someone who has done this before and knows exactly where to apply force. Duccarian's vision whites out. His legs stop receiving instructions. He goes down onto the showroom carpet of a fake living room, and the last thing his philosophical mind produces, as the white spreads and the room recedes, is the completed thought that his theory was right and his body was wrong, and that the gap between a correct idea and an inadequate vessel is the gap that kills you.

He stops moving. WavyDragons crouches beside him for a moment, checks something at his throat, confirms it, and stands.

"You reasoned well," WavyDragons says, to the body. He sounds like he means it. "You just didn't account for me not following my own rules."

The Wardens are three.

[[CHARLISPIRIT]]
CharliSpirit's map is perfect and it is not enough.

She has the entire showroom in her head — every turn, every display, every shortcut. When the hunt begins she moves through it like water through a channel she has memorized, always ahead, always in the section that has been cleared, the map allowing her to predict where the hunters will be and route around them.

She evades for thirty minutes. It is the longest evasion of any of the eight. The map works exactly as she designed it to work.

What the map cannot account for is that one of the hunters has a map too.

Rabbit spent fifteen days as the Syndicate's navigator-by-proxy, the translator of the Bunny's six-second-ahead perception. He does not have CharliSpirit's cartographic instinct, but he has spent fifteen days thinking about how spaces work and how people move through them, and when WavyDragons assigned the hunters their roles, Rabbit asked for the one that suited him: anticipate the clever one.

He finds CharliSpirit by thinking the way she thinks.

He does not chase her. He predicts her. He identifies the section she will route to next — the cleared department that her map will send her toward — and he goes there first, and he waits, and CharliSpirit comes around a corner into a bedroom display and Rabbit is standing in it.

She stops. She turns. She runs — and she is faster than Duccarian, and the chase is real, CharliSpirit sprinting through the showroom with the map guiding her toward the routes she knows, Rabbit behind her, the gap between them holding.

The map betrays her at a dead end.

She has routed into a corner department — a children's section, walled on three sides, a single entrance — believing it connects to the next aisle. It does not. The map was built during the eleven hours of stable lighting; the emergency lighting has changed which display partitions are walls and which are passages, and the partition CharliSpirit expected to be a gap is a wall. She hits it at a run. She turns. Rabbit is in the entrance.

"The map was your strength," he says. He is breathing hard from the chase. "It was also predictable. Anything predictable can be hunted."

"You found me by guessing where the map would send me."

"Yes."

CharliSpirit puts her back against the wall. There is nowhere to go. The map, the thing that defined her, the instinct that survived the altered rooms and rebuilt itself over six days, has delivered her to a corner with one exit and a hunter in it.

"Is it true you didn't really die?" she asks. "Yesterday?"

"It's true."

"Then maybe I won't either."

Rabbit's face does the thing it does when it is carrying something heavy. He crosses the small space. CharliSpirit does not fight — there is no fighting that helps, and she knows it, the strategic mind calculating the absence of options. Rabbit's hands find her throat. He is not cruel about it. He is fast, and he is firm, and he holds the pressure with the steady focus of someone doing a hard thing as mercifully as a hard thing can be done. CharliSpirit's hands come up to his wrists — reflex, not resistance — and then the reflex fades, and her hands fall, and the mapping mind that held the whole store goes dark.

He lowers her to the floor of the children's section. He arranges her so she is not crumpled. Then he stands and walks back into the showroom, and the store, which she mapped completely, is unmapped now, just a building again.

The Floofs are two.

[[RALLY MALLY AND ORPHAN ANNIHILATOR]]
They are found together because they hid together.

alex_awesomeness finds them. He has been moving through the kitchen displays with a methodical thoroughness that is different from the other hunters' styles — not WavyDragons's architectural shortcuts, not Rabbit's prediction. alex_awesomeness searches the way his brother searched. First to scan, thorough, systematic, opening every cabinet, every pantry, every space large enough to hold a person. The family resemblance extends past the face into the method.

He opens the pantry cabinet and Rally Mally and Orphan Annihilator are inside it, pressed together in the dark.

For a moment nobody moves. The knife is in alex_awesomeness's hand.

"You're Wardens," he says. It is not a question. He can tell — the way they hold themselves, the formation-habit visible even crammed into a cabinet. "My brother's team."

"Schwalbe was our friend," Rally Mally says. Carefully. Watching the knife. "He died well. He took too many shifts on the wheel because he was trying to cover for the people who couldn't push. He died because he was the best of us."

Something moves in alex_awesomeness's face. The grief that was given a task meets the information about how the task's target actually died — not as an enemy, as a hero, the brother dying for his team the way the brother apparently always would have.

"He did that," alex_awesomeness says. "He would do that."

"He did."

For a moment it seems like the information might change something. It does not. alex_awesomeness is here because grief was given a task, and the task is not negotiable, and learning his brother died well does not bring his brother back — it only makes the grief sharper, more specific, more in need of the task's completion.

"I'm sorry," he says. And he means it.

Orphan Annihilator moves first — tries to, the bad ankle and the cramped cabinet making the movement slow, a lunge that is more intention than execution. alex_awesomeness is ready for it. He has been holding the knife the whole time. The blade goes in below the ribs, once, precise, the thoroughness extending to efficiency, and Orphan Annihilator folds back into the cabinet with a sound that is mostly breath. Rally Mally catches him — reflex, the habit of helping, his hands going to his friend the way they have gone to his friend since Day 6 — and that is the position alex_awesomeness finds him in, holding Orphan Annihilator, his back half-turned, and alex_awesomeness does the same thing again, once, clean.

Rally Mally goes down beside Orphan Annihilator. The pantry cabinet holds both of them in the dark, side by side, the way they hid, the way they have done everything since Day 6. Neither of them is alone. The blood is dark on the cabinet floor and alex_awesomeness closes the cabinet door on it, gently, the way you close a door on people who are sleeping.

He wipes the knife on a display dishtowel and moves on.

The Wardens are one.

[[NINNGINNI]]
WavyDragons finds Ninnginni.

Ninnginni has not moved from his quiet corner — the storage gap behind a display the showroom path doesn't reach. He has spent the hunt the way he spent the eleven hours: sitting, holding Loyal2Schwab's junction mark, accounting for the people he led. He hears the hunt happening around him — the distant chase of CharliSpirit, the silences, the soft sounds of things ending — and he does not move, because moving is what gets you found, and because some part of him has decided that the last leader of the Floofs should meet the end the way the Floofs met everything: deliberately, on his own terms.

WavyDragons finds him not by accident but because WavyDragons built the storage gap and knows it exists. The architect knows every space the showroom path doesn't reach, because the architect drew the spaces the showroom path doesn't reach.

He crouches at the entrance to the gap.

"Hello, Ninnginni," WavyDragons says.

Ninnginni looks up. He does not get up. "You built this place."

"I did."

"Then you know what this is." Ninnginni holds up the junction mark.

WavyDragons looks at it. "Loyal2Schwab's first mark. Day One. The labyrinth." A pause. "I kept it. I put it in the altered rooms for you to find on Day Eight. I knew you'd carry it."

"Why?"

"Because I wanted there to be something. Because watching ten people become one is the kind of thing that should leave a mark, literally, a physical mark, something a person can hold." WavyDragons's voice is gentle. "You led them well. I watched the whole time. You led them as well as anyone could have led them through what I built. I'm sorry it ends here."

Ninnginni sets the junction mark down — places it on the floor of the storage gap, the way you set down something precious when you will not be able to hold it anymore.

"Make it quick," he says. He closes his eyes.

WavyDragons makes it quick. The same hand, the same point at the base of the skull, the same practiced precision. Ninnginni slumps against the wall of the storage gap, and the last leader of the Floofs goes with his eyes closed and the first mark of his first lost teammate resting beside him on the floor.

WavyDragons picks up the junction mark. He looks at it for a moment. Then he sets it back down, beside Ninnginni's hand, and leaves it there. It is the only thing in the storage gap the hunt does not take.

The Floofs are one.

[[JIKOSU — LUCK]]
JIKOSU's position is perfect and the hunters walk past it three times.

He is high — three meters up, hidden in the gap between stacked boxes on a warehouse shelf, invisible from below. WavyDragons passes beneath him in the first ten minutes and does not look up. alex_awesomeness passes beneath him twenty minutes later, knife in hand, and checks the lower shelves and the floor-level gaps and does not check three meters up because three meters up requires climbing and the search does not include climbing.

His tactical instinct was correct. The hunters search at human level. He went above human level. The position is, as he assessed at the start, a good position.

For forty minutes, JIKOSU is the safest person in the IKEA.

Then his luck inverts.

A box shifts. Not because of anything he does — because of the box itself, the flat-packed furniture inside settling, the weight redistributing, the cardboard giving way at a seam that was always going to give way and chose this moment. The box slides. JIKOSU grabs for it — reflex, the instinct to prevent the noise — and his grab destabilizes the stack further, and the boxes around him begin a slow cascade, and his perfect hiding position becomes a collapsing pile of furniture three meters up with him inside it.

He falls with the boxes.

He falls three meters onto a concrete warehouse floor surrounded by tumbling flat-packed furniture, and the fall should break something. Luck again — the inverse of the box's bad luck — JIKOSU landing in a way that distributes the impact, the boxes cushioning rather than crushing, a heavy carton catching the corner that would have caved his ribs and taking the force instead. His shoulder wrenches. His head strikes a box edge and opens a cut above his eyebrow that immediately runs blood into his eye. But the body that should have shattered is merely hurt.

He lands. He is alive. He is bleeding from the head and his shoulder screams and he is half-buried in collapsed boxes, having made the loudest noise anyone has made in the entire hunt, and the noise has been heard.

Rabbit arrives first.

JIKOSU sees him coming — through the blood in one eye, the hunter who used to be his teammate, walking through the warehouse toward the source of the noise. JIKOSU is on the floor, half-buried, his tactical position destroyed by the worst luck at the worst moment, his shoulder useless, blood sheeting down half his face.

But the luck inverts again.

The same collapsed boxes that revealed him now hide him — the pile is large, chaotic, and JIKOSU, lying still in the middle of it, is just another shape among the fallen furniture. Rabbit arrives at the pile and sees a collapse, a stack that gave way. He does not immediately see JIKOSU, because JIKOSU has gone perfectly still in the wreckage, breathing shallow through the blood, and the wreckage is exactly the right shape to hide a still person.

Rabbit looks at the pile for a long moment.

"Is someone there?" he says.

JIKOSU does not breathe. The blood from his eyebrow pools against the cardboard beneath his cheek. His shoulder is a white bar of pain. He does not move.

Rabbit waits. The tactical part of JIKOSU's mind, even in this moment, notes that Rabbit is good at this — that Rabbit is not fooled, exactly, that Rabbit suspects. But suspecting is not seeing, and the hour is finite, and Rabbit cannot dig through an entire collapsed furniture stack on suspicion alone.

"If you're there," Rabbit says, quietly, "stay there. Stay still. Don't make another sound."

He leaves.

JIKOSU lies in the wreckage and understands that Rabbit knew — that Rabbit chose not to find him, that the message stay there was not a hunter's taunt but a teammate's last gift, the leader who died for his team extending one final protection to the last member of it. JIKOSU does not move for the rest of the hour. He bleeds quietly into the cardboard. His luck, having nearly killed him, has saved him, and the saving came wearing the face of the man who used to lead him.

[[SPACE_FAN — ORCHID]]
space_fan survives because the cat is better at this than any of them.

When the hunt begins, Orchid changes. The calm purring cat of the morning becomes something else — alert, low, ears tracking, the predator-prey awareness that lives in every cat surfacing fully. space_fan, folded in the wardrobe of the children's bedroom display, watches the cat's body the way she has learned to, reading the warnings in the ears and the tail and the angle of the head.

The first hunter passes — Rabbit, moving through the bedroom displays — and Orchid goes flat. Ears back, body low, no sound. space_fan reads it and stays frozen, and Rabbit passes the wardrobe without opening it, because there is no sound, no movement, no reason to check this particular wardrobe among the hundreds in a furniture store.

The second approach is worse. alex_awesomeness — the one with the knife, the one who checks everything — comes through the children's section, and Orchid does something new. The cat presses against the back wall of the wardrobe and goes absolutely still, and space_fan understands the instruction: this one checks. This one will open the wardrobe. We cannot be here when he does.

space_fan moves.

She has been watching the showroom through the wardrobe's cracked door for the entire hunt, building her own working sense of the immediate area — not CharliSpirit's perfect cartography, but enough. When Orchid signals that alex_awesomeness is coming and will check, space_fan slips out of the wardrobe in the gap between his search sweeps, carries Orchid in her arms, and relocates to a display she scouted earlier — a living room with a large sectional sofa, the kind with a hollow base, a gap underneath deep enough for a person and a cat.

She is under the sofa when alex_awesomeness opens the wardrobe she just left.

She watches his boots from beneath the sofa, ten meters away across the showroom. He opens the wardrobe. He finds it empty. He runs the knife-point along the inside once, checking the back panel for a false wall, and then he closes it and moves on, boots passing within three meters of the sofa, the blade hanging at his side dripping faintly with something that is not space_fan's and that she chooses not to think about.

space_fan and Orchid stay under the sofa for the rest of the hour. Every time a hunter approaches, Orchid signals — the ears, the tail, the stillness — and space_fan reads the signal and responds. When a hunter will pass, they stay. When a hunter will check, they have already moved, because Orchid knows before space_fan can know and space_fan has learned to trust the cat completely.

The cat that was a birthday gift on Day 11. The cat that disappeared for two days. The cat that came back under a blanket. The cat is the reason space_fan is alive at the end of the hour, and space_fan holds her under a sofa in a fake IKEA and does not let go.

[[POLARIS — RABBIT]]
Polaris cannot move and so Polaris is found.

She is in the drawer under the bed, the blanket over the gap, her ankle finally resting. She has been there for the entire hour. She has heard the hunt happening — the distant sounds, the silences, the one wet sound from the kitchen section that she has decided not to interpret. She cannot relocate. The ankle that has limited her for nine days has limited her to a single hiding place, and when that hiding place is searched, she has no second option.

Rabbit finds her.

He opens the drawer. The blanket falls away. Polaris is curled in the drawer, looking up at him, the hunter who used to be a pillar, the man who died yesterday and stood back up on the wrong side.

Neither of them moves.

"Polaris," Rabbit says.

"Rabbit."

"Your ankle."

"I couldn't run. I couldn't climb. I picked one spot." She says it without self-pity, the flat statement of someone describing a limitation she has lived with so long it is simply a fact. "You found it."

"I found it."

He does not reach for her throat. He does not have a weapon — Rabbit took no knife, the killing he has done today done with his hands, and now his hands are at his sides and they stay there. He stands at the open drawer and looks at her and does not do the thing the other hunters would do, and the not-doing extends, and Polaris realizes something is happening in Rabbit that is not happening in the other hunters.

"You're not going to do it," she says. Quietly. Testing.

"I should. It's the job. It's the thing I came back to do." Rabbit's voice is the level voice, the unchanging voice, except now it is changing. "I died yesterday so Rally Mally wouldn't die. I pressed the button. And then WavyDragons offered me this — a way to keep existing, in exchange for hunting. And I took it. Because I wasn't ready to be dead." He looks at Polaris in the drawer. "But I keep finding people I knew. CharliSpirit. I put my hands on CharliSpirit's throat an hour after she asked me if she'd survive. And now you. And every time I do the job it gets harder to remember why existing was worth this."

Polaris does not say anything. She has learned, over fifteen days, when silence is the right move.

"alex found Rally Mally," Rabbit says. "I heard. Knife. In a cabinet. The person I died for is dead anyway — made it through the elevator and then died in a pantry one day later, with a blade in him, holding Orphan Annihilator." Rabbit's face does something complicated, something that has been building since the hunt began. "So what did I trade for? I put my hands around a girl's throat tonight. I'm going to be the thing that does that, forever, so I can keep existing. And the thing I originally died to protect is dead anyway. What is the existing for?"

"I don't know," Polaris says. Honestly.

"Neither do I."

The hour is running down. Somewhere in the IKEA, the hunt is concluding. Rabbit stands at the open drawer with Polaris inside it, and the decision he is making is visible in his face, the slow arrival of a person at the edge of what they are willing to do.

"Stay in the drawer," Rabbit says. "Don't come out until the hour ends. If anyone asks, I searched this section and it was empty."

"Rabbit—"

"I died for a teammate yesterday and it didn't matter," Rabbit says. "Maybe tonight I can not-kill one and have that matter instead." He pushes the blanket back over the gap, half-closing the drawer, restoring her hiding place. "You were never my teammate. Different team. But you're the closest thing to one I've got left to protect. So stay down."

He closes the drawer.

Polaris lies in the dark and listens to Rabbit walk away, and her chest does something that is not fear and is not relief but is the specific ache of being spared by someone who is paying a price to spare her — someone whose hands, an hour ago, ended CharliSpirit, and who is choosing not to end her. The pillar that fell and stood back up on the wrong side has, in this one moment, faced the right direction again.

[[THE HOUR ENDS]]
WavyDragons's voice, everywhere:

"The hour is over. The hunt is complete."

The cold emergency lighting shifts back to warm. The IKEA returns to its domestic glow — the fake windows lighting up, the product signs brightening, the staged rooms looking like homes again instead of the sets of homes. The warm light reaches the kitchen section, and the pantry cabinet, and the children's section where CharliSpirit lies arranged-not-crumpled, and it makes all of it look ordinary, which is the worst thing the light can do.

"Three survivors," WavyDragons says. "As I expected. JIKOSU. space_fan. Polaris. Please make your way to the entrance."

Three people emerge from three hiding places.

JIKOSU climbs out of the collapsed furniture pile in the warehouse, bleeding from the eyebrow, one shoulder hanging wrong, alive by the worst luck and the best luck arriving in sequence. He wipes the blood out of his eye with the back of his good hand and walks.

space_fan crawls out from under the sofa with Orchid in her arms, the cat calm again now that the hunt is over, the warning-system powering down.

Polaris pushes open the drawer under the bed and stands — slowly, the ankle protesting, the underdog who survived not by running or climbing but by being spared.

They converge at the entrance.

They do not speak at first. Three people who entered as members of three teams and who are now, by attrition and by WavyDragons's design, three individuals. The Wardens are space_fan. The Floofs are Polaris. The Syndicate is JIKOSU. Each team reduced to a single person. Each person now alone.

WavyDragons is at the entrance. The two hunters are behind him — alex_awesomeness, the knife now clean and sheathed at his hip, and Rabbit, whose hands are in his pockets. Rabbit does not look at Polaris. Polaris does not look at Rabbit. The thing that passed between them in the drawer is not for the others to see.

"You survived," WavyDragons says. "All three of you earned it differently. JIKOSU — the worst luck and the best luck, in the same hour. You're bleeding; that'll be seen to before tomorrow. space_fan — the cat. I should have excluded the cat. I didn't think she'd matter. I was wrong, and being wrong is rare for me, so well done. And Polaris—" He pauses. He looks at Polaris. He looks at Rabbit. Something passes across his face — he knows, or suspects, what Rabbit did. "Polaris survived. The how is between her and one of my hunters. I'll allow it. Even I let mercy happen sometimes."

Rabbit's expression does not change. WavyDragons does not press it.

[[THE ANIMALS]]
The animals return.

They come from the corridor — the Drake, Soup, the Bunny — moving into the IKEA entrance toward the three survivors, and the movement is different from any movement they have made before. They are not leading. They are not warning. They are arriving, the way something arrives to a person it has chosen.

The Drake goes to space_fan. It settles beside her, electric veins at a steady glow, and it does not communicate anything — it simply positions itself at her side, the bonded animal of a team that is now one person, staying with the one person.

Soup goes to Polaris. The luminescent sphere hovers beside her at face height, glow steady, and Polaris — who was never the Floofs' leader, who was just a member, the underdog with the ankle — understands that she is the Floofs now, the last one, and Soup stays with the Floofs, which means Soup stays with her.

The Bunny goes to JIKOSU. It was already with him this morning. Now it sits beside him fully, the red eyes at the six-second-ahead distance, the bonded animal of the Syndicate staying with the last of the Syndicate. It looks at the blood on his face and does not react, because the Bunny has seen worse and the Bunny is not built to flinch.

"The animals stay with the survivors," WavyDragons says. "You're each the leader of your team now — what's left of your team. The animals recognize that. They'll be with you for the finale."

space_fan is holding Orchid. The Drake is beside her. Two animals — one bonded, one a cat that was a gift. She does not know if she is allowed both. She does not ask. She holds the cat and stands beside the Drake and waits to find out what happens next.

"Tomorrow is the finale," WavyDragons says. "One against one against one. Three of you. Three animals. The end of everything we've built over fifteen days." He smiles — the genuine one, the Easter one. "But tonight, you don't go back to your dorms. Tonight is different. Follow the lights."

[[THE COLISEUM]]
The corridor takes them down.

Not to the hub. Not to the sectors. Down — stairs, then more stairs, the descent steeper and longer than any path the facility has used before. The floor-lights are dim. The air grows cooler. The architecture changes from the smooth manufactured surfaces of the upper facility to something older-feeling, rougher, stone where there was metal.

The stairs end at an opening, and the opening is the edge of an enormous space.

A coliseum.

It is vast — circular, tiered, the architecture of an ancient arena, stone seats rising in rings around a central floor of packed earth and sand. The ceiling is so high it disappears into darkness. The whole structure has the quality of something built long before the facility, or built to look that way — the specific grandeur of a place designed for spectacle, for the watching of people doing terrible things to each other while others watch.

The central floor is empty.

At the edge of the central floor: cages.

Three of them. Iron-barred, low, the kind of cage that held the people who were kept before they were used — not the holding cells of prisoners awaiting trial, but the pens of the owned, the bare iron boxes where slaves were kept between the work and the rest. Each cage is large enough for a person and an animal. Each cage has a name — JIKOSU, space_fan, Polaris — on a plate above the door.

"Get in," WavyDragons says. Not unkindly. "You'll be kept here tonight."

"Kept," Polaris says. Quietly. "Like animals."

"Like property," WavyDragons says, and he does not soften it, because softening it would be a lie and he has been honest all day. "Tonight you're not contestants. Tonight you're just things I own, waiting in the dark until I have a use for you. That's what tomorrow is — the use." A pause. "I'm not going to pretend it's dignified. It isn't. Get in."

They get in.

JIKOSU enters his cage and the Bunny enters with him. The door closes — no ceremony, no lock that announces itself, just iron settling into iron, the boundary established. He sits with his back against the bars, his bad shoulder cradled in his good arm, the cut over his eye still weeping slowly, and the Bunny presses against his side.

Polaris enters hers and Soup floats in beside her. The luminescent glow fills the small caged space with soft light. She sits on the cage floor, her ankle finally able to rest, and Soup hovers above her like a small moon.

space_fan enters hers. The Drake comes with her — folding its wings to fit through the cage door, settling beside her in the confined space. She is holding Orchid.

The cage door begins to close.

Orchid moves.

The cat, who has been calm since the hunt ended, suddenly twists in space_fan's arms — not afraid, not fighting, but with the specific intention of a cat that has decided to be somewhere else. She slips free. She is through the closing cage door before space_fan can grab her, out onto the coliseum's central floor, a small striped shape moving across the sand.

"Orchid—"

The cage door closes.

space_fan is at the bars, her hands through them, watching the cat she got back yesterday move away from her across the arena floor. Orchid stops at the center of the coliseum. She looks back at space_fan. The three black stripes are visible even in the dim light. She sits, briefly, looking at space_fan through the bars.

Then she walks into the shadows at the edge of the arena and is gone.

"No," space_fan says. "Not again. Orchid—"

"The cat isn't part of the finale," WavyDragons says. His voice is gentle now. Almost apologetic. "She came into the hunt because I let her. But the coliseum is for the bonded animals — the Drake, Soup, the Bunny. Orchid isn't bonded. She can't stay for what comes tomorrow." A pause. "I'm sorry. I know what she means to you. But the rules of the finale are the rules of the finale, even for me."

space_fan grips the bars. The Drake presses against her side — the bonded animal that is allowed to stay, the consolation that is not the thing she wants. She got Orchid back yesterday. She had the cat for one night. Now the cat is gone again, into the shadows of an arena, on the night before the finale.

"Will she come back?" space_fan asks.

WavyDragons does not answer immediately.

"I don't know," he says, finally. And it has the ring of truth — the figure who built everything, who knows everything, admitting he does not know whether the cat will return. "Some things in here are mine. The cat — I'm not sure the cat is mine. She does what she does. She came back to you once when I didn't expect it. Maybe she will again. Maybe she won't. I genuinely don't know."

[[THE HUNTERS DEPART]]
WavyDragons turns to leave.

The two hunters are behind him — alex_awesomeness, Rabbit. They have been silent through the descent and the caging. Now they move to follow WavyDragons toward a far corridor, an exit from the coliseum that the survivors cannot see clearly in the dim light.

Rabbit pauses.

He looks back at the cages. At JIKOSU, who he protected in the warehouse. At Polaris, who he spared in the drawer. At space_fan, gripping the bars of her cage. The three survivors of a hunt he participated in — the three people he could have killed and, in two cases, chose not to. He thinks of CharliSpirit, who he did not choose to spare, whose throat was under his hands an hour ago, and his face carries that weight openly now, the level mask finally cracked.

He does not say anything. There is nothing to say that would help. He looks at them — at Polaris specifically, for a moment longer than the others — and then he turns and follows WavyDragons into the corridor.

alex_awesomeness does not look back at first. The grief that was given a task has completed part of it — Rally Mally and Orphan Annihilator, two Wardens, his brother's team, accounted for, the knife having done what grief sent it to do. Whatever the grief needed, it has not finished needing it. At the corridor's mouth he pauses, and he does look back — once, at the Wardens' cage, at space_fan, the last living member of the team his brother belonged to. His expression is unreadable. Then he follows WavyDragons.

WavyDragons is the last to leave.

He stops at the corridor entrance. He looks back at the coliseum — at the three caged survivors, at the three bonded animals, at the vast arena where tomorrow's finale will happen. The architect surveying the thing he built, one day before its conclusion.

"Sleep," he says. To the three of them. "Tomorrow is the last day. Whatever happens, it ends tomorrow. You've come further than anyone I've watched. All three of you. I mean that." A pause. "Goodnight."

He walks into the corridor. The hunters are ahead of him. The three of them move into a darkness the survivors cannot follow with their eyes, going somewhere — back to wherever WavyDragons exists when he is not watching, into the unknown architecture of the facility's deepest level, into the place the figure goes.

The corridor light extinguishes behind them.

[[THE CAGES]]
Three people in three cages in a coliseum.

JIKOSU sits in his cage with the Bunny. The tactical mind, for once, has nothing to plan — tomorrow is one against one against one, and there is no strategy he can build tonight that survives contact with whatever the finale is. He sits with the Bunny and does the thing he does every night: the inventory, the systems check. The inventory returns: alive, shoulder wrenched, eyebrow split and crusting, last of the Syndicate, caged. He closes his eyes. The Bunny's red eyes stay open, watching the dark for the threat that is always six seconds away.

Polaris sits in her cage with Soup. Her ankle is grateful for the rest. She thinks about Rabbit — about the drawer, about the choice he made, about the price he is paying to keep existing and the way he spent some of that price to spare her. She thinks about CharliSpirit, who Rabbit did not spare, and the unfairness of being the one he chose when CharliSpirit was not, and the guilt of being glad anyway. She lies down on the cage floor and Soup's glow dims to its lowest register, a small light in a vast dark arena, and she sleeps with the strange ache of having been saved by someone who shouldn't have, in place of someone who should have lived.

space_fan sits in her cage with the Drake. Orchid is gone again — the second time. The first was the time-skip, the sedation, the cat aging while she didn't. This time was a choice — the cat slipping free, walking into the shadows, choosing to leave. She does not understand it. She holds the bars for a long time after the hunters depart, looking at the spot where Orchid disappeared, waiting for a small striped shape to come back across the sand.

It does not come back.

The Drake presses against her side. *I'm here,* it communicates. *I can't be the cat. But I'm here.*

"I know," space_fan says.

She lies down. The Drake settles beside her, electric veins dimming to a low steady glow. The coliseum is enormous and dark and cold and the cage is small and the finale is tomorrow and Orchid is gone and space_fan is afraid — genuinely afraid, the fear she has held at the edge of formations for fifteen days finally arriving in full now that there is no team, no cat, nothing between her and the end except a bonded animal and a set of iron bars.

Three people. Three cages. Three animals. One arena.

The finale is tomorrow.

They sleep — scared, caged, alone-together in the way of people who are in the same terrible place but separated by bars. The coliseum holds them in its vast stone dark. Somewhere far above, the dorms they did not return to hold their orchid aquariums, still growing. Somewhere in the shadows of the arena, a cat is doing whatever the cat does. Somewhere far below, WavyDragons and his two hunters have gone into the unknown.

The dead are wherever the dead go — Duccarian on a fake living-room floor, CharliSpirit arranged in a children's section, Rally Mally and Orphan Annihilator folded together in a pantry, Ninnginni against a wall with a junction mark beside his hand.

Tomorrow, three become one.

Tonight, three sleep in cages, and are afraid.
