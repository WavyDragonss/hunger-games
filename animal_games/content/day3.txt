[[NARRATIVE]]
They do not wake where they fell asleep.

There is no transition. No memory of movement — not even the vague, liquid quality of being carried, the brief awareness of a changed surface, the small physical evidence that a body was relocated. Nothing. The previous day ends, and then, without interval, something else begins. Whatever happens in the space between is not available to them. It has been edited out cleanly, the way a word is removed from a sentence — not crossed out, not left as a gap, simply not there.

Stone is gone. Sky is gone. The labyrinth is gone. The collapsing terrain is gone. The overhang, the junction marks, the shelf earned through running — gone. The last things they remember are: the circular room. The figure. The word elimination deposited into twenty-two seconds of silence. And then nothing, and then this.

This is a room.

Not a room in the way that a cave is a room, or the way that a space defined by three walls and an overhang is a room. A room in the deliberate sense — in the sense of something that was made to be a room, that has no ambiguity about its function, that arrived at its form through intention and not through geology. The walls are smooth and contain no seams. The ceiling is high enough to create a specific kind of atmospheric space — not cavernous, not intimate, something calculated between. The floor is slightly reflective, showing each person a faint and imprecise version of themselves looking up. The light is sourceless and even, the same quality from every direction, casting no shadows, and this is the detail that several people will later identify as the first thing that told them this place was different from everything before it: not the walls, not the ceiling, not the absence of exits, but the light that came from nowhere and illuminated everything equally, as if the room itself was the source.

It does not feel indifferent.

Every environment before this one — the fractured plateaus, the labyrinth's logical stone, the collapsing paths — had the quality of a place that did not care about the people in it. Not hostile. Not welcoming. Indifferent, the way weather is indifferent, the way terrain is indifferent. This room is not indifferent. This room, in some quality that precedes language and sits in the body rather than the mind, feels like it was waiting.

They will not all articulate this. Most of them will feel it and not say it, adding it to the growing collection of things this place has produced that do not have comfortable homes in existing categories.

Thirty people. One room. And they are already, in the first seconds of awareness, reaching for each other.

Schwalbe was one of the first conscious, which was consistent with a general pattern that nobody had formally noted but that everybody had observed: Schwalbe's eyes opened ready, the gap between sleep and full function shorter in him than in most people. He was upright before the room had resolved fully into sensory data. His first action was environmental scan. His second was inventory.

Dark blue. His clothing was dark blue — fitted, clean, different from what he'd been wearing, and he had not consented to the change, and his hand went immediately to where his notation sketches had been and found the fabric of a pocket that was the wrong shape for the purpose. The sketches were not there. He checked twice before accepting this and filing it.

He looked for his team. Found Duccarian three meters away, sitting up with the particular expression of someone who had been awake for slightly longer than their eyes were open — already building a picture, already running the inputs. Found Ashborn helping CrystalWolf orient, one hand briefly on her arm and then removed once she was clearly stable. Found Rally Mally on her feet, already counting — her mouth moving silently through numbers. Found Shroud crouched, examining the floor's reflective quality with the same methodical attention he applied to fungal clusters. Found Toad, who was looking at his own wrist.

"Schwalbe," Toad said, and held up his arm.

The band floated approximately a centimeter and a half above the skin of Toad's wrist. Metallic, thin, not in contact with anything. A number displayed on its face in the faint blue that matched their clothing: 10. Toad turned his wrist. The band followed the movement precisely, maintaining its fixed distance from the skin, tracking his anatomy like a satellite tracking a surface.

"Mine too," CrystalWolf said, looking at her own: 3.

Schwalbe looked at his. 7. He looked at the number for a moment, then at the band, then tried pressing it toward his skin. It offered no resistance and no progress — simply maintained its distance, the gap between metal and wrist staying constant regardless of external pressure, as though the space itself was structural. He released it. It returned to center.

"Who's 1?" Duccarian asked.

Across the room, space_fan was already on their feet, looking at their band with the expression of someone who has received a piece of data and is in the process of deciding what it implies. The band at their wrist glowed violet-blue, consistent with the clothing, and the number on it was 1. Space_fan looked up from it and found Duccarian watching them.

"Alphabetical for the others," space_fan said quietly. "Leaders are one."

"Who decided we were leaders?" Duccarian said.

"The same thing that decided we were here," space_fan said.

Orphan Annihilator, sitting against the wall with their ankle held at an angle that communicated ongoing but manageable pain, looked at their band: 5. "At least I'm not last," they said, to no one in particular.

"I'm last," Toad said. "Ten."

"You're last alphabetically," Rally Mally said, looking at her own band: 6. "That's different from last."

"Is it?"

Rally Mally opened her mouth to explain the distinction and then stopped, because the honest answer was that she didn't know, and saying I don't know in a room like this with rules that hadn't been explained yet required a different kind of honesty than usual.

The Reddening Day stood near the room's center, having completed their own inventory without announcement: The Reddening Day, 9. Clean clothing — deep violet, fitted, functional. The fit was precise in a way that was unsettling because it was precise without any measurement having been taken. They turned once, slowly, surveying the room with the comprehensive attention they brought to any space that contained people in unknown condition. The room was large enough. The floor was clean. The light was adequate. The absence of exits was a fact that required no emotional response yet, because exits were secondary to the question of who in this room needed immediate assessment.

The answer was: nobody. Which was itself interesting.

"We all look the same," Ashborn said. He said it not with discomfort but with observation — the specific quality of someone noting a variable. "Same palette. Same style. We're marked as a group."

"Look at the others," Shroud said.

The others.

[[UNIFIED ROOM — INITIAL STATE]]

[[SPIRIT ANIMAL BEHAVIOR]]

They came from the walls.

Not through a door or an opening — the walls simply admitted them, the smooth surface parting without mechanism and closing after without seam. The Drake came first, folding through the northeast wall and landing on the floor with the controlled weight of something that had decided how much presence to occupy. The Killer Bunny followed through the south wall, white fur carrying its faint iridescent edge, already moving, covering ground without urgency but without pause. Soup descended through the ceiling — or appeared to, the boundary between inside and outside not registering as meaningful — and settled to hover at the room's middle height, rotating.

Thirty people and three animals.

The animals did not go to their teams. They went to each other.

The Drake crossed to the room's center at a pace that was not hurried and not casual but deliberate — the pace of something doing something intentional. The Killer Bunny arrived from the other direction and stopped three meters away. Soup descended until it was roughly at the Drake's eye level, which involved a considerable drop in elevation, and the rotation slowed.

Then they spoke to each other.

Or produced something that occupied the function of speech. It arrived in the room as sound — layered, rhythmic, the kind of complexity that a human voice cannot produce because a human voice has one source and this had several simultaneously, harmonics stacking in intervals that felt mathematical rather than musical. The Drake's contribution had texture and depth. The Bunny's was faster, higher, precise — it repeated phrases in a way that suggested grammar rather than emphasis. Soup's contribution was the most unsettling: it seemed to come from between the other two rather than alongside them, filling gaps in the rhythm as if it were not adding to a conversation but completing an equation.

It went on for approximately ninety seconds.

The thirty people in the room were entirely quiet.

Nobody spoke. Nobody moved toward the animals or away from them. The communication — whatever it was — had a quality that produced not alarm but the specific stillness of people in the presence of something that is not for them. Not exclusive in a way that was hostile. Exclusive in the way that a conversation in a language you don't know is exclusive: you are not unwelcome, you simply cannot enter.

Rabbit stood at the edge of the Syndicate's cluster and listened with the full attention of someone straining toward comprehension and arriving at none. The Killer Bunny — who could communicate, who had communicated directly and clearly and in terms that Rabbit could receive and process and use — was producing something that Rabbit could not follow at all. Not even the edges of it. It was like trying to read a language that used an alphabet you'd never encountered, written in a medium you couldn't see clearly: you could recognize that it was language, recognize that it was organized, recognize that the organization had internal logic. And nothing else.

"It's not talking to us," Rabbit said, quietly.

"No," hollow Camillia agreed. She had come to stand near Rabbit, watching the three animals in the room's center. "Is that the first time it's done something you couldn't follow?"

Space_fan, across the room, was watching the Drake and feeling a version of the same thing — the specific disorientation of an established relationship suddenly producing a dimension you hadn't known existed. The Drake had spoken to them. Had communicated with precision and clarity and specific calibration to space_fan's understanding. Whatever it was doing now was neither of those things. It was operating on a register that excluded them entirely, and the exclusion felt significant in a way that space_fan could not yet interpret.

Each in their own space. [[RULE EXPLANATION]]

"A few things," the figure said.

The thirty people at three tables made the adjustment that people make when a voice that has authority speaks — not compliance, not immediately, but attention. The orientation of thirty bodies toward a single point.

"This facility operates by rules," the figure said. "I'll tell you the rules. I'll tell them to you once, clearly, and after that you'll be expected to have understood them. Questions at the end."

Nobody interrupted. It was the specific silence of people who have decided that information comes first.

"Your dormitories are accessed by bracelet scan. The numbers projected at your dorm entrance are the numbers authorized to enter. If a person enters a dorm that does not display their number, the team associated with that dorm receives a penalty. What that penalty is depends on the nature of the violation."
