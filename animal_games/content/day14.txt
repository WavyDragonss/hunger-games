[[DAY]]
DAY 14 — THE ELEVATOR

[[SYSTEM_STATE]]
Day index: 14.
Participant count: 10.
Challenge type: perception-based identification — anomaly detection across sixty sequential rooms.
Lethality: active. Three-life system, individual.

[[NARRATIVE]]
The room is black.

Not dark. Not dim. The specific kind of black that the eye cannot adjust to because there is nothing for the eye to adjust to. space_fan stands in it — or sits, or floats, the body's relationship to gravity uncertain — and the black is total, the kind of black that her body remembers from the dark maze on Day 12, the kind of black that has texture even though it is the absence of texture.

She is not in the dorms.

She knows this without being able to see it. The air is different. The acoustics are different. The smell is different — there is no smell, which is its own kind of smell, the absence of every odor that defines a place. She is somewhere that the facility has not shown her. She is somewhere that is not on any of the maps she has built in her head over fourteen days.

Then — Orchid.

The cat brushes against her ankle. The small warm body, the soft fur, the three black stripes she cannot see but can feel through the contact. Orchid is here. Orchid has been gone since yesterday morning — sixteen days ago by the body's count, since yesterday morning by space_fan's count — and Orchid is here, brushing against her ankle, purring softly, the same purr that space_fan has known since the morning of Day 11.

"Orchid," space_fan says. Quiet. Not wanting to startle the cat.

The cat moves.

She walks away from space_fan. Not far. Three meters. Then stops. Then walks further. Five meters. Then stops. Then she breaks into a run — the specific patter of cat-feet on a surface that space_fan cannot see, the sound of a small animal moving fast, the sound of Orchid leaving.

"Orchid!"

space_fan tries to follow. Her feet do not move. Her body is in the black room and her body is not capable of motion, the way bodies are not capable of motion in the specific dream-physics that the brain produces when it is processing something it cannot accept. She lifts her foot. The foot does not lift. She shouts.

"ORCHID!"

The shout has weight. It travels through the black room and does not come back. Orchid's footsteps are getting smaller, further, dimmer, the cat running through a darkness that space_fan cannot enter, going somewhere that space_fan cannot follow.

"ORCHID—"

[[WAKING]]
The shouting is in her sleeping space.

space_fan jerks upright. The dorm's altered geometry is around her. The dim emergency lighting that came up during the dark hours is providing enough light to see by. Her own voice is hanging in the air — the last syllable of the cat's name still ringing — and the body that was paralyzed in the dream is now sitting up in bed, sweating, breathing fast, the heart rate of someone who has been running in their sleep.

Across the sector, Rally Mally and Orphan Annihilator are in space_fan's doorway. Duccarian is behind them. The three remaining Wardens, woken by space_fan's shout, having come to her sleeping space to find out what is happening.

"space_fan," Rally Mally says. "space_fan."

"Sorry," space_fan says. She is still breathing fast. "Dream. Bad dream. I'm sorry."

"You were calling for Orchid," Duccarian says.

"Yes."

The three Wardens are quiet. Orchid has been missing for a day — sixteen days, by the body — and space_fan has not mentioned the cat much since yesterday. The grief has been kept private. The shouting in sleep is the first time the grief has become loud enough for the team to hear.

"Lights are on," Rally Mally says. He is looking past space_fan, into the corridor. "The lights came on when you woke up. They were off before. The whole sector was dark."

space_fan looks. He's right. The dim emergency lighting has been replaced by the dorm's full daylight register. The change happened at the moment she sat up, or shouted, or her dream ended — the facility's timing precise as always.

"The hub is probably ready," Duccarian says.

space_fan gets out of bed. Her legs are unsteady. She walks anyway.

[[THE FEAST]]
The hub is dark when they enter.

Not the total black of the dream. The same dim emergency lighting that was in space_fan's sleeping space — present but minimal, the room visible but indistinct. The food tables are up. The smell — not rot. The smell of cooking. The smell of food, actual food, food that has been prepared rather than just produced.

The lights come up the moment ten people are in the hub.

The food is not the canteen breakfast. The food is not yesterday's rotting display. The food is German. space_fan recognizes the dishes without having tasted them, the specific visual vocabulary of a cuisine from somewhere far from anywhere she's been. There is a roast pork knuckle the size of a small football, its skin crackling and dark gold, sitting on a wooden board with a knife beside it. There is a platter of breaded veal cutlets, thin and crispy, golden brown. There is a tureen of dark, fragrant pot roast in some kind of vinegar-spice gravy. Sausages — multiple kinds, the Bratwurst grilled with sweet mustard beside them, the Currywurst in its red-orange sauce, the white Weisswurst with a pretzel curled beside it on a separate plate.

There are sides. A bowl of soft egg-noodle dumplings glistening with cheese and onions — Käsespätzle. A crock of pale fermented cabbage. A pyramid of bread dumplings, round and pale. There are pretzels, glossy and dark and salt-flecked. A large tart in the center of one table — chocolate cake with dark cherries between layers, whipped cream, the specific architecture of a Black Forest preparation. Beside it, apple strudel, layers of flaky pastry visible at the cut edge, dusted with powdered sugar.

The portions do not have tags.

There are no team-colored numbers. No assignment markers. The food is on the tables in serving dishes, with serving utensils. This is the shared meal protocol. This is what Day 6 was and what Easter was and what the cake was. This is generosity.

The figure's voice arrives.

"Good morning. The facility's systems are fully restored. The food situation has been resolved. Today is — in German, the language of this morning's meal — ein guter Tag. A good day. Eat. Enjoy. The challenge will be announced after breakfast."

Ten people stand in the hub looking at a table that contains more food than they have seen since they arrived.

Rally Mally moves first. He gets a plate. He puts a sausage on it. He puts dumplings beside the sausage. He puts sauerkraut beside the dumplings. He sits down at the central table and begins to eat.

The others follow.

space_fan does not get a plate.

She sits at the edge of the table with her hands in her lap. The food smells extraordinary. Her body wants it — she has not eaten in sixteen days plus yesterday, has been operating on sedation-maintenance nutrients for over two weeks, the body's protein stores depleted, the muscles still in catabolism. She should eat. She knows she should eat.

She is looking for Orchid.

Her eyes have been scanning the hub since she walked in — the corners, the edges, the spaces behind the tables, the area near the corridors. The cat is not visible. The cat is not in the hub. The cat has not appeared because the lights came on, the way she half-expected, the way the dream half-promised. The cat is gone the way the cat was gone yesterday.

"You need to eat," Drug says. She has sat down beside space_fan with two plates. One is hers — sparse, precise. The other is space_fan's — full, the way a friend's plate would be full when they are watching out for a friend. The plate has bread, some of the pot roast, dumplings, a sausage. It is balanced. It is what space_fan would have made for herself if she had been able to make a plate.

"Thank you," space_fan says. She does not pick up a fork.

"She's not here," Drug says.

"I know."

"Eat."

"I will."

Drug eats her own food. space_fan looks at her plate. The figure's voice arrives — not in her head, in the room, but it feels like in her head, the specific timing of the figure speaking when space_fan has been thinking and the thinking has been getting darker.

"Five minutes," the figure says. To everyone. "Then the challenge begins. Please finish what you can."

space_fan picks up the fork.

She eats. The food is good. Her body responds the way bodies respond to actual nutrition after a long absence — with relief and with a kind of greed that the will cannot fully suppress. She eats fast for the first minute. Then she remembers that fast eating after starvation is a bad idea and slows down. She manages about half the plate before the figure's voice says "Time."

The plates descend. The food descends. The hub returns to its normal state.

[[THE WALK]]
The Drake leads them. The Bunny follows. Soup hovers at ceiling height.

Three animals, ten humans, one absent cat. The corridor opens to a path they have not used before — narrower than most, lit in a register that is somewhere between white and gold, the specific lighting of a hallway that is leading somewhere important. The walk is five minutes. Nobody talks. The food is in their stomachs and producing the slow heaviness of bodies that are using nutrition for the first time in too long, and the conversation that might have happened is being suppressed by the simple work of digesting.

The corridor ends at a metal door. The door is heavy, industrial, the kind of door that exists in basements and mechanical rooms. There is a control panel beside it. On the wall, a sign in printed letters:

ELEVATOR

[[THE RULES]]
"The challenge today is simple," the figure says.

The tone is warm-controlled. The exhaustion of yesterday is gone — the figure has been restored along with the food and the lights, the system fully operational again.

"You will enter the elevator. The elevator will take you to a room. You have two hundred forty seconds — four minutes — to inspect the room. After the four minutes, you will return to the elevator. The elevator will prompt you to vote: did the room contain an anomaly, or did it not?"

A pause.

"An anomaly is anything that does not belong. Something out of place, miscolored, mis-shaped, mis-numbered, mis-arranged. The room will appear ordinary. Most anomalies will be small. Some rooms will contain no anomaly at all. You will not always be sure. Trust your perception."

Another pause.

"Each round, one person votes. That person bears the consequence of the vote. The voter rotates each round, in an order I will provide. You will each vote six times across the course of the challenge. Each person has three lives. Vote correctly: no life lost. Vote incorrectly: one life lost. Lose all three lives, and you are eliminated."

The math is being done in ten heads.

"Sixty rounds total," JIKOSU says. "Ten people, six rounds each."

"Sixty rounds," the figure confirms. "Voting order is: Rabbit. Duccarian. space_fan. Drug. CharliSpirit. JIKOSU. Polaris. Ninnginni. Rally Mally. Orphan Annihilator. Then the order repeats. Six full cycles."

"Good luck."

The elevator door opens.

She walks forward slowly. She pulls the blanket back gently — the way you pull back a blanket when you do not want to startle whatever is under it, the way you do not pull back blankets when you have spent two days believing the thing you wanted is gone forever.

Orchid is under the blanket.

She is grooming her paw. She looks up at space_fan with the specific calm of a cat who has been doing exactly what she meant to be doing and has no explanation to offer. Her three black stripes are visible on her back. She is alive. She is here. She is purring before space_fan touches her, the small motor starting up at the sight of her person, the specific sound that has been absent for two days.

space_fan sits down on the platform.

She picks up Orchid. The cat goes limp in her arms — the boneless acceptance of a cat being held by a person it has decided is its person — and resumes purring against space_fan's chest.

space_fan does not cry. She has not cried for any of the deaths today and she does not cry now. But her face does something — the muscles that have been holding for fourteen days release, just slightly, the specific easing of someone who has been bracing against a loss that did not, in fact, happen.

"Where did you go?" she whispers.

Orchid purrs.

"I don't care where you went. You're back."

She lies down on her platform with Orchid against her chest. The aquarium beside her — the impossible aquarium, two weeks of growth in a single sleep — continues to glow softly. The orchids are alive. The moss is thick. The cat is here.

Across the room, Schwalbe's empty platform is still empty. Across the dorm, the gaps from yesterday and from the days before yesterday have not changed. But on space_fan's platform, against her chest, a small living thing is breathing and purring.

space_fan closes her eyes.

The day has cost Drug. The day has cost Rabbit. The day has given back the cat. The math of the facility is the math of the facility, and tonight it has handed her a small impossible mercy on a night that should have ended in despair.

Orchid is here.

space_fan sleeps.
