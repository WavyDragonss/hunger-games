[[DAY]]
DAY 16 — THE FINAL DAY

[[SYSTEM_STATE]]
Day index: 16.
Participant count: 3.
Challenge type: the crossing — single combat in name, threshold in truth, one to pass through last.
Lethality: the word no longer means what it meant. Something ends here. It is not what they think.
Animal presence: full — each contestant accompanied by the thing that has carried them since Day 1.

[[NARRATIVE]]
They wake in the cages.

The coliseum is dim — the same vast stone dark they fell asleep in, the high ceiling lost above them, the tiered seats rising empty into shadow. Three iron cages at the edge of a fifty-meter circle of packed sand. Three people who slept badly, if at all, in the bare pens where the place kept them overnight.

Polaris wakes first. Her ankle should be the worst it has ever been — the cold of the stone seeping into the joint overnight — but when she tests it, the pain is strangely distant, a thing she remembers more than feels, as though the ankle belongs to someone she used to be. She sits up against the bars and Soup is beside her, the luminescent sphere hovering low, its glow the only warmth in the cage.

"Morning," she says to it.

*Morning,* Soup says. The communication is gentle, and gentler than gentle, the way a voice softens when it is close to the end of what it has to say. *Today is the last day. The final one. After today there is no Day 17. There is no facility. There is only what is on the other side of today.*

"The finale," Polaris says. "The three of us. One survives."

*That is what you have been told,* Soup says. *Hold the word survives loosely. It may not fit what happens.* A pause. *I have something to tell you. Not yet. When the moment comes, you'll know, and I'll confirm it. I told Ninnginni the same. His moment never came. Yours will.*

"You're sure this time."

*I'm sure. Today everything that was held in reserve gets spent. There is no eleventh day to save it for.*

JIKOSU wakes second. The cut over his eyebrow has scabbed overnight, and his wrenched shoulder has stiffened, but when he runs his morning inventory the results come back wrong in a way the tactical mind cannot file: the injuries are there and they do not hurt the way injuries hurt. The pain has the quality of a rumor. He sits up and takes inventory anyway, because taking inventory is the last thing he has, and the inventory returns: alive, caged, the last of the Syndicate, and standing at the edge of something his tactics cannot map.

The Bunny is pressed against him. Its red eyes are open, but the six-second-ahead distance in them is gone — the gaze that always looked slightly into the future now looks at nothing, or at everything, the foresight spent or no longer needed.

*I can't see ahead anymore,* the Bunny communicates. *Today there is no ahead to see. Today is the edge of the map.*

"That's new."

*Everything today is new. Nothing today has happened before.*

space_fan wakes last. The Drake is beside her, electric veins at a low steady glow, wings folded in the cramped cage. Her first thought, before the arena, before the finale, before anything — is Orchid. The cat slipped away last night, out of the closing cage, into the shadows of the coliseum. space_fan looks across the sand for a small striped shape and does not find one.

"Is she out there?" she asks the Drake.

*She's everywhere out there,* the Drake says, which is not an answer, or is a different kind of answer. *I can't perceive her the way I perceive things. She's gone past where my senses reach. She crossed something. I don't have a word for what she crossed.*

space_fan grips the bars and looks at the vast dark arena and somewhere in it, past the reach of a creature made to perceive, is a cat that came back to her once and left again — and today she has to face Polaris and JIKOSU with the cat unaccounted for and the final day waiting.

[[THE MORNING]]
There is no food.

The realization arrives slowly — no tables rise, no smell of cooking, no meal delivered to the cages. For fifteen days the place fed them, with cruelty or generosity or indifference, but it fed them. Today there is nothing. And the absence does not read, this morning, like the message it would have read on any other day — not *the bodies are tools that no longer need maintaining*. It reads like something quieter. Like a house on the morning everyone leaves it. Like a place that has stopped needing to keep them, because keeping is almost over.

"No breakfast," JIKOSU says, across the arena.

"No breakfast," Polaris agrees.

"That's how you know it's the last one."

The three of them sit in three cages and do not say much else. There is not much to say. They have come through fifteen days together — through the platforms and the altered rooms and the mirror rooms and the wheel and the dark maze and the elevator and the hunt — and the coming-through has built something between them that does not have a name, the specific bond of people who watched the same dark and remained inside it. And today, they have been told, they have to end each other.

Nobody pretends otherwise. Nobody offers a speech about how they wish it were different. They are past that. They have been past that for days.

space_fan breaks the silence once. "If it's me," she says. To both of them. To the arena. "If I'm the one who — whatever the word is for what happens at the end of today. I'll carry all of you. Everyone. Toad and Loyal2Schwab and Kiwi and Mainthemainstar and Drug and all of them. Wherever I end up, I'll bring them with me."

"If it's me, the same," Polaris says.

"The same," JIKOSU says.

It is the closest thing to a goodbye that three people standing at the edge of something can give each other.

The figure's voice arrives.

"Good morning."

WavyDragons. The warm-controlled tone, but today it carries the specific weight of an ending — not exhaustion, not anticipation, but the quiet of someone who has shepherded many people to this exact place and is standing at it again, as he always stands at it, with the patience of a thing that does this and has always done this.

"Today is the final day. You three are the last. You came through everything I set in your path — every challenge, every loss, every impossible thing. I want you to know, before this begins, that I am proud of you. All three. What you did over the last sixteen days was more than I ask of most who pass this way."

A pause. *Most who pass this way.* The phrasing is strange and none of them catch it, or all of them catch it and none of them say so.

"But only one passes through last. That was always the shape of it. The final day is single combat — that is what the cages and the sand and the arena tell you. Three of you enter the circle. One remains in it. Your animals will be with you; they earned the right to stand beside you at the threshold. There are no other rules. There is no time limit. The final day ends when one of you is the last one standing on the sand."

The cages open.

Three iron doors swing outward at once, the sound echoing across the empty coliseum. Three people step out onto the sand. Three animals follow.

"Begin when you're ready," WavyDragons says. "Or don't begin at all, and see how long the arena lets you wait. Either way — this is the end. Make the crossing worth the sixteen days."

The voice goes quiet. *The crossing.* The word hangs in the dark where the voice was, and dissolves before anyone can hold it up to the light.

[[THE STANDOFF]]
For a long time, nobody moves.

Three people stand at three points of the fifty-meter circle, their animals beside them, the sand between them empty. The reluctance is total and shared — none of them wants to take the first step, because the first step is the beginning of the end of two of them.

"We could wait," Polaris says. Her voice carries across the sand. "He said the arena might not let us. But we could try. Just — not do it."

"He built the arena," JIKOSU says. "If he wants us to fight, the arena makes us fight. Waiting is a strategy he's already accounted for."

He is right. The sand begins to change.

But it does not change the way they expect. They brace for heat at the edges, for a burning perimeter, for the arena herding them inward with pain. That is not what comes. Instead the outer ring of the sand begins, very faintly, to *glow* — a pale light rising up through the grains from underneath, as though the floor of the coliseum is thinning, as though something luminous is pressing up against the underside of the world. The light is not hot. It is cold and clean and it widens inward, a ring of brightness eating the dark sand, contracting the circle of ordinary ground toward the center, toward each other.

"There it is," JIKOSU says. "No waiting." But his voice is uncertain, because the light is not a thing his tactics have a category for, and uncertainty is the one state JIKOSU has spent sixteen days refusing.

Three people move toward the center as the edges brighten. The animals move with them — the Drake at space_fan's shoulder, the Bunny at JIKOSU's side, Soup hovering above Polaris. The ordinary ground shrinks to thirty meters. Twenty. The three of them are close now, close enough to see each other's faces, the injuries, the exhaustion, the reluctance that none of them have managed to put down.

"I don't want to do this," space_fan says.

"None of us do," Polaris says.

"Then maybe we make it gentle," JIKOSU says. "For each other. Whatever happens — gentle. No suffering. We owe each other that much."

The ground hits fifteen meters and stops shrinking. The ring of cold light holds, a luminous wall around a small circle of three people and three animals, and the light is bright enough now to see by, and in it their faces look pale and strange, lit from below, lit from underneath the world.

[[THE FIGHT — OPENING]]
JIKOSU moves first.

Not at a person — at the space between them, the tactical instinct choosing position over aggression, JIKOSU moving to a spot that gives him sightlines on both space_fan and Polaris simultaneously. The Bunny flows with him, low and fast, but the Bunny's flow is different now — it moves perfectly, beautifully, but without the predictive edge it always carried, like a dancer who has stopped reading the music and is simply moving to it.

space_fan responds by moving with the Drake. The Drake's electric veins flare — and space_fan understands, suddenly, that the Drake is a weapon, that the light running through its body is not decorative. But even as the veins brighten she feels the wrongness of it: the Drake's electricity, in the cold rising light of the arena, looks small. A spark held up against a dawn. The animals are weapons and the arena is making the weapons feel like gestures.

Polaris moves slowest. Her ankle should forbid speed, and she favors it from habit, but the pain that should anchor her is far away, and she finds she could move faster than she does. She doesn't. She and Soup retreat to the edge of the lit ground, and Soup positions itself in front of her, a shield of light against a fight that has not started.

For a moment the three of them circle, animals and humans, a slow rotation around the center of the shrunken arena. Reading each other. Waiting for someone to commit.

JIKOSU commits.

He goes at space_fan — the assessment concluding that she and the Drake are the larger threat, that Polaris with her ankle is the lesser problem to be dealt with second. He closes the distance, and the Bunny streaks ahead of him, and space_fan barely gets the Drake between herself and the charge.

The Drake meets the Bunny.

It is the first time any of them have seen the animals fight, and it is fast and strange and somehow already grieving — the Drake's light against the Bunny's speed, a clash that should be brutal and instead looks like two old things going through a motion they were built for and no longer believe in. White arcs crack across the sand. The Bunny blurs. But the clash has no malice in it. The animals fight the way the humans are about to fight — because the arena says to, and not because anything in them wants to.

While the animals clash, JIKOSU reaches space_fan.

[[THE FIGHT — THE MIDDLE]]
They grapple.

space_fan is not a fighter. JIKOSU is — or has the instinct that approximates one — but his shoulder is wrenched and space_fan fights with the specific desperation of someone who does not want to die and does not want to kill, the two refusals making her wild and hard to predict. They go down onto the sand together, a tangle of limbs, each trying to gain the position that ends the fight without quite being willing to take it.

"I'm sorry," JIKOSU says, into the grapple. "I'm sorry, space_fan—"

"Don't apologize while you're doing it," space_fan says, and gets a knee up, and creates space, and rolls free.

The Drake and the Bunny are still fighting. The Drake has caught the Bunny with a glancing arc — the Bunny favors one side now — and the Bunny retaliates with a strike too fast to see, and the Drake's glow flickers, dims, recovers. The animals are wounding each other the way the humans cannot quite bring themselves to. But where the wounds open, the light from underneath the world shows through — as though the animals are not bleeding so much as thinning, becoming translucent at the edges, the way fog thins when the sun comes up behind it.

Polaris watches from the edge.

This is the thing about Polaris — she has always watched. The ankle that kept her from the front of every formation put her at the edge, observing, and sixteen days of observing have made her good at it. She watches JIKOSU and space_fan grapple. She watches the Drake and the Bunny clash. And she watches the thing the others, caught in the fight, cannot watch: she watches the *light*. The way it rises through the sand. The way the wounded animals go translucent. The way the whole arena feels less like a place where people are about to die and more like a place that is quietly preparing to open.

And she understands something — not a tactic, a different kind of understanding. *This isn't a fight. The fight is the cover story. Something else is happening underneath it, and the fight is what we're being shown so we don't look at the something else.*

*Polaris,* Soup says.

"Not yet," Polaris says. She is still watching. "You said I'd know. I don't know yet. But I'm starting to feel the edge of it."

*Soon,* Soup says. *Keep watching. You always see it before the others. That's why it's you.*

[[THE FIGHT — ORCHID]]
The cat appears.

Nobody sees where she comes from — she is simply there, on the sand, at the edge of the fight, a small striped shape that should not be in the arena because the arena is for the three contestants and their three bonded animals, and a cat is none of those things. Orchid walks onto the sand with the specific unhurried confidence of an animal that has decided to be somewhere, and she walks toward space_fan.

But she is not quite the cat she was.

She is lit, too — like the sand, like the wounded animals, lit from within and from underneath, the three black stripes on her back glowing faintly along their edges. She moves with a smoothness that is not a cat's smoothness. She has been somewhere, in the shadows of the coliseum, in the place past where the Drake's senses reach, and she has come back changed by it, carrying some of its light back with her into the circle.

space_fan, mid-fight, sees her. "Orchid—no, stay back—"

The Bunny, wounded and fast, breaks off from the Drake and goes at space_fan — JIKOSU's opening, the Bunny exploiting the moment space_fan is distracted. The strike is coming fast, faster than space_fan can react.

Orchid moves.

The cat crosses the space between herself and space_fan in a motion that is not a cat's motion — too fast, too precise, the kind of speed that belongs to the things that walk in the light under the world. She intercepts the Bunny's strike. The two animals collide on the sand, the cat and the impossible rabbit, and for an instant the arena holds something that should not be possible: a small striped cat moving like something far older than a cat, doing a thing it was never made to do.

The collision should end Orchid. The Bunny is a force that came through sixteen days. Orchid is a birthday gift. The Bunny strikes and Orchid takes it and the impact throws her across the sand—

—and where she lands, the sand goes bright.

She gets up. She should not get up — but she gets up, and she is not even hurt, because the strike that should have ended her passed *through* her, through the part of her that is becoming light, and did not find anything solid enough to break. Orchid shakes herself, and the brightness clings to her like water, and she looks back at the Bunny with an expression that no cat has ever worn, and the Bunny — wounded, fast, fearless for sixteen days — backs away from her.

space_fan does not understand it. She does not have time to. But she sees the cat do a thing the cat should not do, and somewhere underneath the fight a small impossible fact registers and is filed for later: *Orchid is more than she was. Orchid found something in the shadows. Orchid crossed something and came back to show me the way.*

WavyDragons's voice does not comment. But the quality of the arena's silence shifts — the specific stillness of a keeper who has watched many crossings and is watching one he did not arrange, a thread in the pattern he did not weave, and is choosing, with something almost like wonder, not to cut it.

[[THE FIGHT — THE TURN]]
JIKOSU and space_fan finish their war.

It happens the way Polaris saw it would — the two of them grinding each other down, the Drake and the Bunny both thinned to translucence, the humans both exhausted, until a moment arrives where one of them gains the position that ends it. It is JIKOSU. His instinct, even depleted, even one-shouldered, finds the opening that space_fan's desperation cannot close. He gets behind her. His arm goes around her throat — the practiced hold, the position that wins.

"I'm sorry," he says again. He means it again. "Gentle. I promised gentle."

space_fan's hands come up to his arm. The Drake tries to reach her — thinned, slow, its light guttering — and the Bunny moves to block it, and the Drake cannot get there in time. Orchid sits on the bright sand and watches, and does not intervene, the way something watches when it knows that what is about to happen is not what it looks like.

"It's okay," space_fan says. Her voice is tight against the hold. But there is something underneath the tightness — not fear. Something closer to recognition. As though some part of her, looking at the lit sand and the translucent Drake and the impossible cat, has understood what Polaris understood at the edge. "JIKOSU. It's okay. I think — I think it's not what we were told. I think it's okay. Let go of being sorry."

JIKOSU makes it gentle.

space_fan goes still. The hold does its work — clean, quiet, the promise kept — and space_fan's body settles onto the sand, and the Drake makes a sound that no one has heard it make, an electric keening. And then the white comes for her — but it does not take her the way the white took the dead all sixteen days, dissolving them into nothing. It rises up through the sand beneath her and *lifts* her, gently, the brightness gathering her up the way light gathers around a thing it is welcoming, not erasing. For an instant space_fan is outlined in it, lit from every side, and her face — slack a moment ago — is not the face of the dead. It is the face of someone who has just stepped through a door into a brighter room.

Then she is gone from the sand. And the Drake, standing over the empty bright place where its human was, does not fade into nothing. It folds its wings, and the light takes it too, lifting it after her, the bonded thing following its person through the door it could not see until it opened.

space_fan and the Drake are gone — but *gone* is the wrong word, and even JIKOSU, kneeling on the sand, feels that it is the wrong word.

JIKOSU is on his knees, breathing hard, having just done the thing he promised to do gently and well, and the tactical mind that has carried him through sixteen days is finally, completely quiet — because there is no tactics for what he just watched, which was not a death. It had none of the finality of the deaths he has witnessed. It looked like a beginning wearing the shape of an ending.

He looks up. Across the sand, Polaris stands at the edge of the lit ground, Soup hovering before her, her ankle whole-feeling and her eyes on JIKOSU.

"Just us," JIKOSU says.

"Just us," Polaris says. "JIKOSU — did that look like dying to you? Because it didn't look like dying to me."

JIKOSU does not answer. The honest answer is no, and the honest answer frightens the tactical mind more than death would have, because death he understands and this he does not.

[[THE FIGHT — THE MOMENT]]
JIKOSU stands.

It costs him, or it should cost him — the shoulder, the depletion, the war he just fought — and he stands anyway, because the final day is not over and he is the last of the Syndicate and the Syndicate does not stop. The Bunny moves to his side, thinned, half-translucent, but present, the bonded thing staying with its human to the threshold.

Polaris does not move toward him. She stays at the edge of the lit ground and watches JIKOSU gather himself, and she does the thing she has done for sixteen days: she reads the situation. But the situation she reads is no longer the situation she was given. The fight is the cover story. The light rising through the sand is the truth. And the truth is about to ask something of her.

*Now,* Soup says.

Polaris feels it. The thing Soup told her she would feel. The moment Ninnginni was promised and never got, the gift the Floofs won on Day 5 and held for eleven days, the ability that has waited in reserve since the night Loyal2Schwab's absence was established. It arrives in her not as a thought but as a certainty — but it is not the certainty she expected. She expected a weapon. What arrives feels less like a weapon and more like a *key*.

"Now," Polaris says.

*Now,* Soup confirms. Its voice is full of light. *I told you that you'd know. You know. The freeze is yours. But hear me, Polaris — it was never only a way to win a fight. It is a way to step outside the moment. To stand in the still place between one instant and the next, where the rules of the arena cannot reach you. What you do in that still place is yours to choose. The Floofs won the right to choose. I'm giving it to you. Use it as the last of us would have wanted.*

Soup's luminescence flares — brighter than it has ever been, brighter than the light rising through the sand, a sphere of pure white expanding outward — and time stops.

Not metaphorically. The arena freezes. JIKOSU, mid-stride in his approach, stops between one step and the next, his weight forward, his body committed to a motion that no longer continues. The Bunny freezes beside him. The cold light rising through the sand stills mid-rise, caught halfway through the floor of the world. The entire arena holds, suspended — and in the suspension, the light that was rising no longer hides anything. With everything stopped, Polaris can see clearly what the moving fight kept blurred: the sand is not sand. The arena is not stone. Underneath the appearance of a coliseum is a vast brightness, and the coliseum is the thin dark skin stretched over it, and the skin is wearing thin.

Polaris moves through the stillness.

Her ankle does not hurt — not because the freeze numbs it, but because in the still place between instants, she understands, the ankle was never the real thing about her. The limitation was the cover story too. She walks across the frozen sand toward JIKOSU, who hangs motionless in the middle of his final assault, and she reaches him, and she stops.

Because this is the part nobody prepared her for. The freeze gives her the win — the easy, terrible win, the clean finish returned to JIKOSU the way he returned it to space_fan, the arena delivered into her hands. All she has to do is the thing that ends it.

JIKOSU's face is frozen mid-effort — the exhaustion, the pain, the grief of a person who just had to do a terrible thing and is now doing another. It is not the face of an enemy. It is the face of someone she came through sixteen days beside.

And in the still place, with all the rules of the arena stopped around her, Polaris understands what the key is for.

"He told us the rules," she says, to the frozen JIKOSU. To the arena. To Soup. "Single combat. One stands. He never said the one standing has to be the one who put the others down." She looks at the frozen brightness pressing up through the sand. "And I don't think this place is what he says it is. I don't think any of us died here. I think we crossed. And I'm not going to be the last one to cross by being the one who pushed the other two through."

*Then what will you do?* Soup asks. It already knows. It is asking so that she will say it.

"I'm going to cross *with* him," Polaris says. "Not over him."

She does not do the thing the freeze was supposed to let her do. Instead she reaches out, in the still place, and she puts her hand on JIKOSU's frozen shoulder — the wrenched one, the one that has screamed since the warehouse — and she holds it the way you hold a person you are about to walk somewhere with.

"Resume it," she tells Soup. "But not into the fight. Into the other thing. The thing under the sand."

*That is the choice,* Soup says, and its light is overwhelming now, joyful, the joy of a thing that held a secret for eleven days and finally gets to spend it the right way. *That was always the choice. The freeze stops the moment. What restarts is up to the one who holds it. You could restart the fight. You're choosing to restart the crossing. Polaris — the underdog who shouldn't have made it — of course it's you. Of course it was always going to be you.*

Time resumes.

[[THE WHITE]]
JIKOSU completes his frozen step — but there is no opponent in front of him, and there is no sand under him, because the thin dark skin of the arena, with the freeze lifted into the crossing instead of the fight, finally gives way.

The coliseum comes apart. Not violently — the way ice comes apart in spring, the way a held breath comes apart into a laugh. The sand, the stone, the tiered seats, the high lost ceiling — all of it loses its hold on being a coliseum, the dark skin peeling back from the brightness underneath, and the brightness is not a weapon and not an ending. It is warm. It is the warmest thing any of them have felt in sixteen days.

JIKOSU staggers as the floor of the world opens under him, and Polaris's hand is on his shoulder, and she does not let go.

"Polaris—" JIKOSU says. "What did you—the fight—"

"I didn't win it," Polaris says. "I ended it a different way. Come on. We're not finishing this one apart."

The Bunny and Soup rise into the brightness alongside them, no longer translucent but whole, made solid again by the light they were thinning toward. And the white takes everything — but it takes them together, the two of them and the two animals, lifted the way space_fan was lifted, gathered up rather than erased, carried through the door rather than dissolved at it.

The last thing the arena does, as it comes apart, is fall silent in a way that is not empty. It is the silence of a keeper watching a crossing go differently than every crossing before it, and finding, against all his patience, that he is glad.

The white takes everything. *Open your eyes when it fades,* Soup says, from inside the light, its voice already part of the brightness. *The other side is real. It was always the realest thing here.*

[[THE OTHER SIDE]]
Polaris opens her eyes.

She is not in the arena. She is not in a chair, not in a room, not anywhere that has walls in the way she understands walls. She is standing — her ankle whole, truly whole now, the injury not numbed but simply *not there*, the way a thing is not there when it was never the real thing about you — on a wide bright plain of soft light that has the quality of early morning and goes on in every direction without ending.

JIKOSU is beside her. His shoulder is whole. The cut over his eyebrow is gone. The tactical inventory, when he runs it — and he runs it, because it is the last reflex to leave him — comes back with results it has never returned: *whole. unhurt. unafraid. and not alone.*

"This isn't a recovery room," JIKOSU says, slowly. The tactical mind, presented with the impossible, does the only thing it can do, which is describe it. "There's no transition. No waking. We didn't come back from somewhere. We arrived somewhere."

"Yeah," Polaris says.

And then she sees them.

They are all here.

Toad. Loyal2Schwab. Kiwi. Golden. Mainthemainstar. Ashborn. CrystalWolf. lumallie. hollow Camillia. The Reddening Day. Revenant. Akarimmu. Elv. NinjaFlout. Galaxy. Godly_Aura. Shroud. Schwalbe. janhk. Lyrix. Drug. Duccarian. Rally Mally. Orphan Annihilator. Ninnginni. CharliSpirit. And near them, not apart this time, not held at a distance — Rabbit. And space_fan, with the Drake at her shoulder.

Everyone. Every person who fell across sixteen days, standing on the bright plain, whole, unhurt, lit by the morning that has no source. They are not crowded into a clinical room watching screens. They are *waiting* — the way people wait at the far end of a journey for the last travelers to arrive, the way a family waits on a platform for the last train.

"They're here," Polaris says. Her voice does not work properly. "They're all—"

"They crossed first," a voice says.

It is WavyDragons. But not from the ceiling, not from everywhere — from a person, standing on the bright plain a little apart from the others, the young man with the kind face, the same person from the hub, the same person who hunted them, the same voice. But here, in the light, he is harder to look at directly, the way the brightest part of a morning is hard to look at, as though the body is a courtesy he extends so the new arrivals have something to focus on.

"Welcome to the other side," he says. "You crossed last, Polaris. And you crossed carrying someone, which almost no one does. That's rare. That's the rarest thing I see."

[[WAVYDRAGONS EXPLAINS]]
Polaris turns on him. The grief and the confusion of sixteen days arriving all at once, with nowhere left to put it.

"What is this. What was the facility. The challenges, the deaths — were they real? Did they die? Did *I* die? What are you?"

"Sit, if you like," WavyDragons says. "Or stand. Distance and tiredness don't mean much here. I'll tell you what I tell everyone who crosses, which is the truth, which is all I've ever told you even when it sounded like cruelty."

He looks at the gathered roster — the living and the fallen, which here is no distinction at all, because all of them are standing in the same light.

"The facility was a threshold. A passage. Not a place where people die — a place people pass *through*. Everyone who came here was already at the edge of an ending, in the life on the other side of the dark. I don't decide who comes. I only keep the crossing. I have kept it a long time. And what I do — what the challenges were, what the platforms and the rooms and the wheel and the maze and the hunt were — was never a test of whether you deserved to live. It was the shape your crossing took. The crossing always takes a shape. For you, it took sixteen days and a set of teams and a coliseum, because that is the shape your particular passage needed in order to teach you the thing you needed to learn before you arrived."

"And dying?" Drug says. She is standing near the front, the same flat calm she carried through the passage, but it is softer now. "We felt people die. I grieved Mainthemainstar. For days."

"When someone 'fell' in the passage," WavyDragons says, "they finished crossing. They came here. To this side. Ahead of you. And then they waited." He gestures to the bright plain, to all of them. "Nobody ended. Crossing isn't ending. It only looks like ending from the side you start on. The fear was real. The grief was real. The bonds were real — they were the *most* real thing, they're the only thing you carried across with you. But the fall was never the end you thought it was. It was a door, and the door opened here, and here everyone is."

Mainthemainstar speaks. Quietly. He is standing close to Drug. "I crossed on Day Eight. And I had to wait, and watch, while she grieved me — for days, knowing I was fine, unable to tell her."

"I know," WavyDragons says. "That's the cruelest part of every crossing, and I won't pretend it away. The ones who cross early wait for the ones they love. The waiting is hard and the waiting is part of it. But the waiting ends. Look — it ended. She's right here."

Drug looks at Mainthemainstar. Mainthemainstar looks at Drug. Neither says anything, but Drug, who held her arms crossed through sixteen days of dark, uncrosses them, and on the bright plain that is its own kind of sentence.

[[RABBIT]]
Polaris finds Rabbit in the crowd.

He is not held apart here — the gap the others left around him in every imagining of this moment is gone, because here the thing he carried is visible to everyone, and visible, it is lighter than it was. But he steps forward when she looks at him, because the accounting is still his to give.

"You hunted us," Polaris says. Not angry. Needing to understand. "You crossed early, on Day Fourteen. And you came back into the passage to hunt the people you'd come through it with."

"I wasn't ready to cross," Rabbit says. "I know how that sounds. I finished the elevator and I arrived here and I felt — robbed. Like my passage wasn't finished. And WavyDragons offered me a way back into it. To go back through as a hunter. And I took it, because I wasn't ready for my crossing to be over." He looks at CharliSpirit, who is watching him from the bright plain. "And then I was back in, and I had to do the thing the hunters do — I had to help people cross before they were ready, with my own hands. I helped CharliSpirit cross. It felt like killing, because in the passage it *was* like killing. And I have to carry that I chose to be the one who did it."

"You spared me," Polaris says.

"By the time I found you, I understood what I'd done," Rabbit says. "And I couldn't help one more person across against the clock. Sparing you didn't undo CharliSpirit. But it was the only thing I had left to give." He looks at the gathered roster. "I went back. I don't expect the kind of welcome the others get. I just need you to know I know what I did."

CharliSpirit crosses the bright plain to him — the woman whose crossing he hurried in a children's section of a furniture store that did not exist.

"It felt real when you helped me across," she says. "I want you to know that. I felt myself go. And then I arrived here, and I had to sit with the fact that someone I trusted did that to me." A pause. "But I've had a while, on this side, to watch the rest of it. I watched you spare Polaris. I watched it cost you. And I think the passage broke something open in all of us, a little. You just broke open where everyone could see it. I'm not all the way to forgiving you. But I understand you. And on this side, understanding is most of the way there."

Rabbit nods. It is not absolution. It is more than he came back expecting.

And beside the crowd, alex_awesomeness stands with Schwalbe — the brother who walked into the passage to understand the place his brother crossed in, the observer who took up the knife. Schwalbe has an arm around him.

"My brother came in from the outside," Schwalbe says, to the bright plain. "He wasn't crossing. He was grieving — he watched me go on Day Thirteen, from the near side, and he couldn't bear it, and when WavyDragons offered him a way in, he took it, to be close to the thing that took me. He carried a knife through a crossing because grief gave him a knife. I've already forgiven him. He was avenging an ending that wasn't an ending. He just didn't know yet. None of us knew yet. That's the whole point of the near side — you don't know yet."

[[THE FINALISTS]]
WavyDragons turns to the three who came through last — Polaris, space_fan, JIKOSU.

"You three crossed last. And you each crossed differently, and the differences are the truest thing I learned about each of you."

He looks at JIKOSU.

"JIKOSU. You crossed on tactics until tactics ran out, and then you crossed on something you couldn't control and had to learn to bear — the boxes that nearly crushed you and then hid you, the luck that wasn't yours to command. A tactician humbled by the part of the crossing that no plan touches. You spent sixteen days learning that you can't strategize your way through an ending. You can only meet it. And at the very end you met it gently, which is more than most tacticians manage."

He looks at space_fan.

"space_fan. You crossed because you trusted something other than yourself. You read the cat. You trusted her senses over your fear, all the way through the dark." He pauses, and something moves across the part of him that is hard to look at — the wonder from the arena. "We'll come to the cat. But you arrived here a moment before the others because, at the very end, you stopped being afraid of the crossing. You felt what it was, in JIKOSU's arms, and you told him to let go of being sorry. You crossed willingly. The willing ones arrive lightest."

He looks at Polaris.

"And Polaris. The underdog. The one with the ankle I gave you on Day Six — and yes, I gave it to you, the limitation was part of the shape of your crossing, the thing that pushed you to the edge of every formation where you could *watch*. You couldn't run, couldn't climb, couldn't fight at the front. So you watched, and you learned to see the thing under the thing. And when the last moment came, and the freeze the Floofs won was finally yours, you saw what it was actually for. Everyone before you who held a freeze used it to win the fight. You used it to refuse the fight. You stepped into the still place between instants, and instead of pushing JIKOSU through alone, you took his shoulder and crossed *with* him." The bright, hard-to-look-at face does something that is unmistakably a smile. "Nobody has ever done that. In a long time of keeping this crossing, nobody has ever spent the last gift to bring someone across beside them instead of going alone. You didn't win the final day, Polaris. You ended it. There's a difference, and the difference is the whole point."

"I thought I had to be the last one standing," Polaris says.

"You were the last one standing," WavyDragons says. "You just refused to be the last one standing *over* anyone. Standing beside is also standing. The rule never specified. I built it that way on purpose. Almost no one finds the gap. You found it."

space_fan crosses to Polaris. JIKOSU follows. The three who came through last stand together, whole, in the light.

"You brought him across," space_fan says. "You could've gone alone and you brought him."

"I'm not mad about how it ended," JIKOSU says, and the tactical face has eased into something it has not worn in sixteen days. "Tactically, refusing the fight was a move I didn't even know was on the board. I respect it." A pause. "I'm glad it was you. If one of us had to find the gap, I'm glad it was you."

[[ORCHID]]
And then space_fan feels something against her ankle.

A pressure. Soft, warm, accompanied by a sound she knows better than almost any sound — a purr, loud and rumbling, the purr of a small animal that has decided a person is its person.

space_fan looks down.

Orchid is at her feet. On the bright plain, on the other side, purring, the three black stripes catching the light that has no source. She is whole and real and *here* — present in a place that even the keeper of the crossing did not bring her to.

space_fan drops to her knees and gathers the cat into her arms, and Orchid goes limp in the boneless way she always goes limp, and presses her head under space_fan's chin.

WavyDragons is looking at the cat the way he looked at her in the arena — the wonder, but deeper now, because here he cannot pretend not to see her.

"That one," he says, slowly, "I can't account for."

"What?" space_fan says, holding her.

"I keep the crossing. I know who comes through it, and how, and why. I know the shape of every passage I've ever kept." He looks at Orchid. "She was never at an ending. She wasn't crossing. She was a small comfort I let into your passage on Day Eleven, because you needed one — she was part of the shape, not a soul making the journey. She shouldn't be able to be *here*. There's no door she was supposed to come through." He pauses, and the wonder in him is the wonder of something very old encountering something it has never seen. "And yet she crossed. She went into the shadows of the coliseum, past where any of the bonded ones could perceive, and she found the door herself, and she came through it on her own, because she decided she belonged on whatever side you ended up on. I don't have a category for that. I'm not sure there is one."

"Is she—" space_fan starts.

"She's yours," WavyDragons says. "Whatever she is. However she got here. Some things cross by love alone, and they don't ask me for a door. I think that's the only kind of crossing I don't keep. I think that one keeps itself." He looks at the cat for a long moment, the keeper of all the doors looking at the one creature that opened its own. "Keep her. She was always yours. She'll always find you. That seems to be the rule she made, and it's a better rule than any of mine."

space_fan holds Orchid and does not ask any more questions. The cat is here. The cat crossed a threshold a cat should not be able to cross, following space_fan by nothing but the decision to. The secret of how stays a secret. Even WavyDragons does not know it. Maybe no one ever will. Maybe that is the point — that on the other side of everything, there is still one small bright mystery that even the keeper of crossings cannot explain, and her name is Orchid, and she is purring.

[[CLOSING]]
The bright plain does not empty, because there is nowhere to empty to. Instead it opens — the morning-light going wider, and the thirty of them, whole, beginning to move into it together, the ones who crossed early and the ones who crossed last no longer separated by the order of their going.

Toad finds the Wardens he started with. Loyal2Schwab and Golden and the Floofs who crossed before them. Drug and Mainthemainstar walk into the light side by side, the grief finally resolved into the thing on the far side of grief.

Polaris stands at the edge of the opening light with her whole ankle and the strange weight of having ended the final day without ending anyone.

JIKOSU finds her there. "You going to be okay?" he asks.

"I don't know," Polaris says. Honestly. "I spent sixteen days thinking the only way through was to be the last one standing over everyone else. And it turns out the way through was beside. I don't know how long it takes to stop bracing for a fight that's over."

"A while, probably," JIKOSU says. "For all of us." He looks across the light at space_fan, who is holding Orchid, the cat that opened her own door. "Some of us came through with stranger company than others."

Polaris almost laughs. "Yeah."

WavyDragons approaches the three of them one last time.

"Thank you," he says. "All three of you. For sixteen days I watched you cross things I shaped to be uncrossable, and you crossed them with more grace than the shape required. I keep this passage for a great many people. Most cross alone, and arrive alone, and find the others waiting. You—" he looks at Polaris "—arrived carrying someone. And you—" at space_fan "—arrived followed by something that crossed for no reason but love. And you—" at JIKOSU "—arrived having finally put the strategy down and just walked through. Those are the three best ways I know to make the crossing. You found all three, between you."

"Were you really proud of us?" space_fan asks. "You said it. In the passage. More than once."

"Every time," WavyDragons says. "I keep a hard door. I make it look like a nightmare, because the near side has to look like something a person fights, or the crossing teaches nothing. But on this side I'm not the voice that told you that you'd die. I'm the one who's glad you didn't, in the only way that counts — you didn't end. None of you ended. Go on. The light goes somewhere, and it's good. You earned the going."

He steps back. He smiles — the genuine smile, the Easter smile, the smile of a keeper who has watched a great many crossings and is, despite all of them, still glad of a good one.

"Goodbye," he says. "And thank you. Truly. Open your eyes, on the far side of this light too, when it fades. It keeps going. It's real all the way down."

The thirty of them walk into the widening morning — Polaris with her whole ankle, JIKOSU with the strategy finally laid down, space_fan with a cat in her arms that crossed a threshold by love and that no keeper of any door can explain.

Behind them there is no coliseum, no facility, no screens, no chairs. There is only the light, and the going, and the company of everyone they thought they'd lost.

Thirty people crossed.

None of them ended.

One of them refused to be the last one standing alone, and so all three of the last ones arrived together — and the keeper of the crossing, who has kept it a long time, marked it as the rarest passage he has seen.

space_fan holds Orchid up to the light. The cat blinks in it — slow, content, the three black stripes catching a morning with no edge and no source.

"Come on," space_fan says. "Whatever's next. Let's go together."

And they do.

[[THE END]]

[[FINAL_OVERVIEW]]
Outcome: The crossing complete. Zero true endings across sixteen days — every fall was a passage through, not an end.

The last three to cross (each having found a different true way through):
- Polaris — crossed last, and crossed carrying JIKOSU rather than going over him. Refused to be the last one standing alone; spent the inherited Day 5 freeze to step into the still place and cross beside another instead of winning the fight. The keeper's rarest passage.
- space_fan — crossed willingly, unafraid at the end, trusting the crossing the way she trusted the cat.
- JIKOSU — crossed having finally laid down the tactics and simply walked through.

All 30 who entered the passage are whole and gathered on the other side.

Special mystery: Orchid (a comfort given on Day 11, never a soul making the journey) — present and whole on the other side regardless. She found her own door, in the shadows of the coliseum, and crossed by love alone. Mechanism unknown even to the keeper. Status: with space_fan, and apparently always will be.

The Hunters (Day 15, all revealed as those who crossed early or came from the near side):
- WavyDragons — keeper of the crossing.
- Rabbit — crossed Day 14, re-entered the passage as a hunter rather than finish his own crossing.
- alex_awesomeness — Schwalbe's brother, came from the near side in grief and entered as a hunter.

Note: Throughout the passage, "death" was never an end. Every one who fell finished crossing to the other side and waited there for the rest. The fear was real, the grief was real, the bonds were real and were the only things carried across. The ending was not an ending. It only looked like one from the side where you begin.
