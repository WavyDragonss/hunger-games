[[DAY]]
DAY 16 — THE FINALE

[[SYSTEM_STATE]]
Day index: 16.
Participant count: 3.
Challenge type: final fight — single combat, three contestants, one survivor.
Lethality: absolute. Only one walks out.
Animal presence: full — each contestant accompanied by their bonded animal.

[[NARRATIVE]]
They wake in the cages.

The coliseum is dim — the same vast stone dark they fell asleep in, the high ceiling lost above them, the tiered seats rising empty into shadow. Three iron cages at the edge of a fifty-meter circle of packed sand. Three people who slept badly, if at all, in the bare pens where the facility kept them overnight.

Polaris wakes first. Her ankle is the worst it has been — the cold of the stone floor seeped into the joint overnight, the swelling tight against the skin, the pain a constant now rather than a thing that comes and goes. She sits up against the bars and Soup is beside her, the luminescent sphere hovering low, its glow the only warmth in the cage.

"Morning," she says to it.

*Morning,* Soup says. The communication is gentle. *Today is the last day.*

"I know."

*Do you know what today is?*

"The finale. The three of us. One survives." Polaris looks across the arena at the other two cages — JIKOSU in one, space_fan in the other, both still asleep or pretending to be. "I have to fight them. The two people who survived with me. That's the test."

*Yes,* Soup says. And then, after a pause: *I have something to tell you. Not yet. When the moment comes, you'll know. I'll confirm it.*

"That's the same thing you told Ninnginni," Polaris says. She remembers — the Floofs talking, the time freeze, Soup's gift held in reserve since Day 5, the ability the team won and never used. Ninnginni carried the knowledge of it. Ninnginni is gone. "You told him you'd tell him when. You never did."

*Ninnginni's moment never came,* Soup says. *Yours might.*

JIKOSU wakes second. The cut over his eyebrow has scabbed overnight, dark and tight. His shoulder — wrenched in yesterday's fall — has stiffened into a limited, painful range of motion. He sits up, takes inventory the way he takes inventory every morning, and the inventory returns the worst results it has ever returned: injured, depleted, caged, and scheduled to fight two people he would, under any other circumstance, stand beside.

The Bunny is pressed against him. Its red eyes are open. *You're hurt,* it communicates.

"I'm aware."

*The finale does not care that you're hurt.*

"I'm aware of that too."

space_fan wakes last. The Drake is beside her, electric veins at a low steady glow, wings folded in the cramped cage. Her first thought, before the arena, before the finale, before anything — is Orchid. The cat slipped away last night, out of the closing cage, into the shadows of the arena. space_fan looks across the sand for a small striped shape and does not find one.

"Is she out there?" she asks the Drake.

*I don't know,* the Drake says. *I can't perceive her. She's not where I can reach.*

space_fan grips the bars and looks at the vast dark arena and somewhere in it, maybe, is a cat that came back to her once and left again, and today she has to fight Polaris and JIKOSU with the cat unaccounted for and the finale waiting.

[[THE MORNING]]
There is no food.

The realization arrives slowly — no tables rise, no smell of cooking, no meal delivered to the cages. For sixteen days the facility has fed them, with cruelty or generosity or indifference, but it has fed them. Today there is nothing. The absence is its own message: today is not a day that requires feeding. Today the bodies are tools that do not need maintaining past the end of their use.

"No breakfast," JIKOSU says, across the arena.

"No breakfast," Polaris agrees.

"That's how you know it's the last one."

The three of them sit in three cages and do not say much else. There is not much to say. They have survived fifteen days together — through the platforms and the altered rooms and the mirror rooms and the wheel and the dark maze and the elevator and the hunt — and the survival has built something between them that does not have a name, the specific bond of people who watched the same horrors and remained. And today they have to end each other.

Nobody pretends otherwise. Nobody offers a speech about how they wish it were different. They are past that. They have been past that for days.

space_fan breaks the silence once. "If it's me," she says. To both of them. To the arena. "If I'm the one who — if I win. I'll remember all of you. Everyone. Toad and Loyal2Schwab and Kiwi and Mainthemainstar and Drug and all of them. I'll remember."

"If it's me, the same," Polaris says.

"The same," JIKOSU says.

It is the closest thing to a goodbye that three people who have to fight can give each other.

The figure's voice arrives.

"Good morning."

WavyDragons. The warm-controlled tone, but today it carries the specific weight of an ending — not exhaustion, not anticipation, but the quiet of someone who has reached the last page of something he has been writing for a long time.

"Today is the finale. You three are the last. You've survived everything I built — every challenge, every loss, every impossible thing. I want you to know, before this starts, that I'm proud of you. All three of you. What you did over the last sixteen days was extraordinary."

A pause.

"But there can only be one. That was always the rule. The finale is single combat. Three of you enter the arena. One walks out. The cages will open. Your animals will be with you — they've earned the right to stand beside you at the end. There are no other rules. There is no time limit. The finale ends when one of you is the last one standing."

The cages open.

Three iron doors swing outward at once, the sound echoing across the empty coliseum. Three people step out onto the sand. Three animals follow.

"Begin when you're ready," WavyDragons says. "Or don't begin at all, and see how long the arena lets you wait. Either way — this is the end. Make it worth the sixteen days."

The voice goes quiet.

[[THE STANDOFF]]
For a long time, nobody moves.

Three people stand at three points of the fifty-meter circle, their animals beside them, the sand between them empty. The reluctance is total and shared — none of them wants to take the first step, because the first step is the beginning of the end of two of them.

"We could wait," Polaris says. Her voice carries across the sand. "He said the arena might not let us. But we could try. Just — not do it."

"He built the arena," JIKOSU says. "If he wanted us to fight, the arena will make us fight. Waiting is a strategy he's already accounted for."

He is right. The sand begins to change.

It starts at the edges — the outer ring of the arena floor growing warm, then hot, the temperature visibly rising, the air above it beginning to shimmer. The circle of safe ground is contracting. The arena is herding them toward the center, toward each other, the heat at the perimeter a wall that pushes inward.

"There it is," JIKOSU says. "No waiting."

Three people move toward the center as the edges burn. The animals move with them — the Drake at space_fan's shoulder, the Bunny at JIKOSU's side, Soup hovering above Polaris. The safe ground shrinks to thirty meters. Twenty. The three of them are close now, close enough to see each other's faces, the injuries, the exhaustion, the reluctance that none of them have managed to put down.

"I don't want to do this," space_fan says.

"None of us do," Polaris says.

"Then maybe we make it fast," JIKOSU says. "For each other. Whatever happens — fast. No suffering. We owe each other that much."

The safe ground hits fifteen meters and stops shrinking. The arena has its combatants where it wants them. The heat at the edges holds, a burning wall around a small circle of three people and three animals who have run out of room to avoid what comes next.

[[THE FIGHT — OPENING]]
JIKOSU moves first.

Not at a person — at the space between them, the tactical instinct choosing position over aggression, JIKOSU moving to a spot that gives him sightlines on both space_fan and Polaris simultaneously, refusing to turn his back on either. The Bunny flows with him, low and fast, its six-second-ahead perception reading the arena.

space_fan responds by moving with the Drake. The Drake's electric veins flare — and space_fan understands, suddenly, that the Drake is a weapon, that the electricity running through its body is not decorative, that the bonded animal she has carried since Day 1 can do more than communicate and navigate. The Drake's body crackles, the veins brightening to a white-hot intensity, and the air around it begins to smell of ozone.

Polaris moves slowest. Her ankle forbids speed. She and Soup retreat to the edge of the safe ground — as far from the other two as the burning perimeter allows — and Soup positions itself in front of her, the luminescent sphere interposing between Polaris and the fight, a shield of light.

For a moment the three of them circle, animals and humans, a slow rotation around the center of the shrunken arena. Reading each other. Waiting for someone to commit.

JIKOSU commits.

He goes at space_fan — the tactical assessment having concluded that space_fan and the Drake are the larger threat, that Polaris with her ankle is the lesser problem to be dealt with second. He closes the distance fast despite the shoulder, and the Bunny streaks ahead of him, and space_fan barely gets the Drake between herself and the charge.

The Drake meets the Bunny.

It is the first time any of them have seen the animals fight, and it is fast and brutal and strange — the Drake's electric body against the Bunny's impossible speed, a clash of light and motion that the human eye can barely track. The Bunny moves at the speed it showed on the Day 6 ramp, a blur, here and then there, and the Drake's electricity arcs after it, white bolts cracking across the sand, each one missing the Bunny by the fraction of a second that the Bunny's foresight provides.

While the animals clash, JIKOSU reaches space_fan.

[[THE FIGHT — THE MIDDLE]]
They grapple.

space_fan is not a fighter. JIKOSU is — or has the tactical instinct that approximates one — but his shoulder is wrenched and his body is depleted and space_fan is fighting with the specific desperation of someone who does not want to die and does not want to kill, the two refusals making her movements wild and hard to predict. They go down onto the sand together, a tangle of limbs, each trying to gain the position that ends the fight without quite being willing to take it.

"I'm sorry," JIKOSU says, into the grapple. "I'm sorry, space_fan—"

"Don't apologize while you're doing it," space_fan says, and gets a knee up, and creates space, and rolls free.

The Drake and the Bunny are still fighting. The Drake has landed a glancing arc — the Bunny is favoring one side now, its speed slightly reduced, the electricity having caught it at last. The Bunny retaliates with a strike that the human eye cannot follow, and the Drake's glow flickers, dims, recovers. The animals are wounding each other the way the humans cannot quite bring themselves to.

Polaris watches from the edge.

This is the thing about Polaris — she has always watched. The ankle that kept her from the front of every formation put her at the edge, observing, and sixteen days of observing have made her good at it. She watches JIKOSU and space_fan grapple. She watches the Drake and the Bunny clash. She watches the shape of the fight, the way it is developing, the two strong contenders wearing each other down while she stands at the perimeter with her bad ankle and her sphere of light.

And she understands something. The fight will come down to whoever is left after JIKOSU and space_fan finish with each other. The two of them are evenly matched in their exhaustion — JIKOSU's tactics against space_fan's desperation, the Drake against the Bunny — and they will grind each other down to the point where one of them barely wins, and then that one will turn to Polaris, depleted, and Polaris will have to fight someone who has just survived a war.

She does not like her odds. She has never liked her odds. She has survived sixteen days without ever once liking her odds.

*Polaris,* Soup says.

"Not yet," Polaris says. She is still watching. "You said I'd know. I don't know yet."

*Soon,* Soup says.

[[THE FIGHT — ORCHID]]
The cat appears.

Nobody sees where she comes from — she is simply there, on the sand, at the edge of the fight, a small striped shape that should not be in the arena because the arena is for the three contestants and their three bonded animals and a cat is none of those things. Orchid walks onto the sand with the specific unhurried confidence of an animal that has decided to be somewhere, and she walks toward space_fan.

space_fan, mid-fight, sees her. "Orchid—no, stay back—"

The Bunny, wounded and fast, breaks off from the Drake and goes at space_fan — JIKOSU's tactical opening, the Bunny exploiting the moment space_fan is distracted by the cat. The strike is coming fast, faster than space_fan can react, the Bunny's reduced speed still far beyond human reaction time.

Orchid moves.

The cat crosses the space between herself and space_fan in a motion that is not a cat's motion — too fast, too precise, the kind of speed that belongs to the spirit animals and not to a tabby cat that was a birthday gift. She intercepts the Bunny's strike. The two animals collide on the sand, the cat and the impossible rabbit, and for an instant the arena holds something that should not be possible: a simulated cat moving like a bonded animal, a creature out of its category, doing a thing it was never built to do.

The collision should end Orchid. The Bunny is a force that has survived sixteen days of challenges, and Orchid is a house cat. The Bunny strikes and Orchid takes it and the impact throws her across the sand—

—and she gets up.

She should not get up. The strike that caught her was a strike that has felled larger things. But Orchid rolls across the sand and comes to her feet and shakes herself, and she is hurt, visibly hurt, but she is up, and the getting-up is the wrongest thing that has happened in the arena, because the cat is doing something that the rules of the simulation do not permit. Something has changed in her. Something that was never supposed to change.

space_fan does not understand it. She does not have time to understand it. But she sees the cat survive a thing the cat should not survive, and somewhere underneath the fight a small impossible fact registers and is filed for later: Orchid is different now. Orchid is more than she was.

WavyDragons's voice does not comment. But something in the quality of the arena's silence shifts — the specific stillness of a system that has noticed an anomaly it did not author and is choosing, for now, not to address.

[[THE FIGHT — THE TURN]]
JIKOSU and space_fan finish their war.

It happens the way Polaris predicted — the two of them grinding each other down, the Drake and the Bunny both wounded, the humans both exhausted, until a moment arrives where one of them gains the position that ends it. It is JIKOSU. His tactics, even depleted, even one-shouldered, find the opening that space_fan's desperation cannot close. He gets behind her. His arm goes around her throat — the practiced hold, the position that wins.

"I'm sorry," he says again. He means it again. "Fast. I promised fast."

space_fan's hands come up to his arm. The Drake tries to reach her — wounded, slow, the electricity guttering — and the Bunny blocks it, and the Drake cannot get there in time.

"It's okay," space_fan says. Her voice is tight against the hold. "JIKOSU. It's okay. Make it—"

JIKOSU makes it fast.

space_fan goes still. The hold does its work — clean, quick, the promise kept — and space_fan's body settles onto the sand, and the Drake makes a sound that no one has heard it make, an electric keening, and then space_fan is gone the way the dead in this place go: there, and then not, the white light taking her, her body dissolving into the arena's light the way every death in the facility has dissolved.

The Drake stands over the empty sand where its bonded human was. Its veins dim to almost nothing. Then it, too, fades — the bonded animal following its human out of the arena, the way the animals have always been tied to the people they chose.

space_fan and the Drake are gone.

JIKOSU is on his knees on the sand, breathing hard, his shoulder screaming, having just done the thing he promised to do quickly and well. He does not get up immediately. The tactical mind, which has carried him through sixteen days, is quiet. There is no tactics for this. There is only the fact of what he just did and the two of them that remain.

He looks up. Across the sand, Polaris stands at the edge of the safe ground, Soup hovering before her, her ankle bad and her body whole and her eyes on JIKOSU.

"Just us," JIKOSU says.

"Just us," Polaris says.

[[THE FIGHT — THE MOMENT]]
JIKOSU stands.

It costs him. The shoulder, the depletion, the war he just fought with space_fan — standing costs him all of it, and he stands anyway, because the finale is not over and he is the last of the Syndicate and the Syndicate does not stop. The Bunny moves to his side — wounded, slowed, but present, the bonded animal staying with its human to the end.

Polaris does not move toward him. She cannot — the ankle, the speed she does not have. She stays at the edge of the safe ground and watches JIKOSU gather himself for the final assault, and she does the thing she has done for sixteen days: she calculates her odds, and her odds are bad.

JIKOSU is depleted but he is a fighter. Polaris is fresh but she cannot run, cannot close distance, cannot escape if the fight goes wrong. If JIKOSU reaches her, he wins — the same hold, the same clean finish, the tactical instinct that just beat space_fan turned on the last opponent. Polaris has watched the whole fight from the edge and learned everything about how JIKOSU fights, and the learning tells her she loses.

Unless.

*Now,* Soup says.

Polaris feels it. The thing Soup told her she would feel. The moment Ninnginni was promised and never got, the gift the Floofs won on Day 5 and held for eleven days, the ability that has been waiting in reserve since the night Loyal2Schwab's absence was established. It arrives in Polaris not as a thought but as a certainty, a sudden knowledge of what she can do.

"Now," Polaris says.

*Now,* Soup confirms. *I told you that you'd know. You know. The freeze is yours. Use it well.*

Soup's luminescence flares — brighter than it has ever been, a sphere of pure white light expanding outward across the arena — and time stops.

Not metaphorically. The arena freezes. JIKOSU, mid-stride in his approach, stops between one step and the next, his weight forward, his body committed to a motion that no longer continues. The Bunny freezes beside him, mid-flow. The dust kicked up by JIKOSU's step hangs in the air, motionless. The heat-shimmer at the arena's edge stills. The entire world holds, suspended, a single frozen instant stretched into something Polaris can move through.

Polaris moves through it.

Her ankle does not hurt in the frozen time. Or it hurts the same, but the hurting does not matter, because she does not have to hurry, does not have to close distance against a moving opponent, does not have to win a race her body cannot win. She walks — slowly, carefully, favoring the ankle out of habit even though the habit no longer matters — across the sand toward JIKOSU, who hangs motionless in the middle of his final assault.

She reaches him.

And she stops.

Because this is the part nobody prepared her for. The freeze gives her the win — JIKOSU cannot move, cannot defend, cannot do anything but hang in the frozen air while Polaris does whatever she chooses to do. She has won. The ability the Floofs held for eleven days has delivered the finale into her hands. All she has to do is the thing that ends it.

JIKOSU's face is frozen mid-effort — the exhaustion, the pain, the grief of a person who just had to kill space_fan and is now trying to kill Polaris because the arena demands one survivor. It is not the face of an enemy. It is the face of someone she survived sixteen days beside.

"I'm sorry," Polaris says. To the frozen JIKOSU. To the arena. To the sixteen days. "You'd do it to me. You'd have to. So I have to. But I'm sorry."

She does the thing.

It is not graphic and it is not cruel. In the frozen instant, with JIKOSU unable to move, Polaris does the thing that ends his participation — the same kind of clean finish JIKOSU gave space_fan, returned now to him, the mercy of speed extended to the person who extended it to others. She does it with her eyes open and her hands steady and her heart breaking, the underdog who survived sixteen days finishing the last opponent not through strength but through the one gift her lost team left her.

Time resumes.

JIKOSU completes his frozen step — onto sand that no longer has an opponent in front of him, because the thing happened in the instant he could not perceive — and then JIKOSU is gone, the white light taking him, his body dissolving into the arena's light. The Bunny stands over the empty sand for a moment, its red eyes at zero seconds, and then it fades too, following its human.

JIKOSU and the Bunny are gone.

Polaris is the last one standing.

[[THE WHITE]]
The arena goes silent.

Polaris stands alone on the sand. Soup hovers beside her, its luminescence settling back to its normal glow, the freeze spent. The heat at the edges fades. The empty coliseum holds one person and one sphere of light and the sand where two other people stood a moment ago.

"I won," Polaris says. She does not sound like she won. She sounds like someone who has done a terrible thing and survived it. "I'm the last one."

*You are,* Soup says.

"They're gone. space_fan and JIKOSU. I killed them. I killed everyone — no. I'm the only one who didn't—" Her voice breaks. Sixteen days of holding finally giving way, the grief arriving all at once, the weight of being the survivor crashing down. "Everyone else is dead and I'm the one who's left and I had to kill the last two myself and—"

The world begins to dissolve.

It starts with the arena — the sand, the stone, the tiered seats, the high lost ceiling — all of it beginning to lose definition, to soften, to come apart at the edges the way an image comes apart when you look at it too closely. The coliseum is dissolving. The dim light is brightening. The vast stone dark is giving way to something white, something clean, something that is not the facility at all.

"Soup," Polaris says. "What's happening—"

*The end,* Soup says. Its voice is changing — gentler, and somehow further away, the way a voice sounds when the thing producing it is also dissolving. *You won, Polaris. And winning means it's over. All of it. Everything.*

"What's over? What do you—"

*You'll understand in a moment. Thank you for carrying me. Thank you for being the last. I'm proud of you — the underdog who shouldn't have made it, who made it anyway.* Soup's glow expands, fills Polaris's vision, becomes everything. *Goodbye, Polaris. Open your eyes when the white fades. The world on the other side is real.*

The white takes everything.

[[WAKING]]
Polaris opens her eyes.

She is not in the arena.

She is in a chair — a reclined chair, comfortable, the kind of chair that exists in medical offices or expensive lounges. There is a device on her head, light and cool, being lifted away by hands she cannot see. The light is bright and clean and ordinary — fluorescent, real-world, the specific light of a room that is just a room.

Her ankle does not hurt.

That is the first thing she notices, before anything else, before she understands where she is or what has happened. Her ankle — the ankle that has hurt since Day 6, that was the worst it had ever been this morning, that limited her for sixteen days — does not hurt. She moves it. It moves freely. It is fine. It was always fine. The injury was in the simulation. Her real body never had it.

"Welcome back," a voice says.

It is WavyDragons's voice. But not from the ceiling, not from everywhere — from a person, standing beside her chair, a young man with a kind face and an expression of genuine warmth. The same person from the hub. The same person who hunted them. WavyDragons, in the real world, smiling down at her.

"You did it," he says. "You won. Take a moment. The disorientation passes."

Polaris sits up. The chair is one of many — a room full of reclined chairs, dozens of them, arranged in rows, and most of them are empty now, their headset devices lifted and set aside. The room is large and clean and bright and full of people.

The people.

Polaris looks at them and her mind refuses, for a moment, to process what she is seeing, because what she is seeing is impossible.

They are all here.

Toad. Loyal2Schwab. Kiwi. Golden. Mainthemainstar. Ashborn. CrystalWolf. lumallie. hollow Camillia. The Reddening Day. Revenant. Akarimmu. Elv. NinjaFlout. Galaxy. Godly_Aura. Shroud. Schwalbe. janhk. Lyrix. Drug. Duccarian. Rally Mally. Orphan Annihilator. Ninnginni. CharliSpirit.

Everyone. Every person who died over sixteen days. All of them, standing in a bright clean room, alive, watching her wake up. Some of them are smiling. Some of them have been crying. All of them are looking at Polaris with the specific expression of people who have been watching something for a long time and are relieved it is over.

"They're alive," Polaris says. Her voice does not work properly. "They're — everyone's—"

"Nobody died," WavyDragons says gently. "Nobody ever died. It was a simulation, Polaris. All sixteen days of it. The facility, the challenges, the animals — all of it was simulated. And when someone 'died' in the simulation, they didn't die. They woke up. Right here, in this room, in a chair like the one you're in. And then they watched."

He gestures to the far wall. It is covered in screens — a massive array, dozens of feeds, and on the largest of them is the arena, frozen on the final image: Polaris, alone on the sand, the last one standing.

"They watched the whole thing," WavyDragons says. "Everyone who woke up early got to watch the people who were still in. They watched you survive. They watched you win. They've been cheering for you — for all three of you finalists — for hours."

[[THE OTHERS WAKE]]
There is movement in two of the chairs.

space_fan sits up first. The headset lifts away from her. She blinks in the bright light, her hand going to her throat — to the place where JIKOSU's arm was, the hold that ended her in the simulation — and finding nothing there, no injury, no mark, just her own neck and her own breath and the dawning understanding that she is alive.

"I'm—" she says. "I died. JIKOSU—"

"You woke up," WavyDragons says. "The same as everyone. JIKOSU didn't kill you. He couldn't. There was nothing to kill. You're fine."

space_fan looks around the room. At the people. At everyone. Her eyes are doing the same impossible math Polaris's eyes did, refusing and then accepting and then refusing again. "They're all—"

"Alive," Polaris says, from her chair. "Everyone's alive, space_fan. It wasn't real. None of it was real."

JIKOSU sits up in the third chair.

He comes up the way he does everything — all at once, the tactical mind snapping online, the inventory running. And the inventory returns results it has never returned: no cut over his eyebrow, no wrenched shoulder, no depletion, no injury. His body is whole. He touches his shoulder — the shoulder that screamed through the entire finale — and it is fine.

"It was a simulation," JIKOSU says. Not a question. The tactical mind has already assembled it from the available evidence — the impossible recovery of his body, the room, the people, the screens. "The whole thing. Death was — what. Extraction?"

"Extraction," WavyDragons confirms. "When the simulation registered a 'death,' it woke the participant. Cleanly, instantly, painlessly. The death you experienced — that anyone experienced — was the last sensation before waking, and even that was dampened. Nobody suffered. Nobody felt the thing they thought they felt. The simulation pulled you out at the threshold."

JIKOSU absorbs this. Then he looks at space_fan, and at Polaris, and something in his tactical face eases — the specific relief of a man who killed his friend and just learned he didn't.

"space_fan," he says. "I'm—"

"Don't," space_fan says. But she is smiling — barely, shakily, but smiling. "You don't have to apologize for something that didn't happen. We're okay. We're all okay."

[[WAVYDRAGONS EXPLAINS]]
WavyDragons stands at the front of the room, and the thirty people — all of them, the living and the formerly-dead, which is everyone, because everyone is living — turn to listen.

"I owe you an explanation," he says. "All of you. So here it is."

"This was a study. A psychological experiment — the largest and most immersive of its kind. Thirty participants, sixteen simulated days, a controlled environment designed to test how people behave under conditions of perceived mortal stakes. How you cooperate. How you sacrifice. How you lead, how you follow, how you break and how you hold. The simulation was total — you believed it was real, because belief was the entire point. An experiment where the participants know the stakes are fake measures nothing. You had to believe you could die. So we made you believe it."

He looks around the room.

"Every one of you volunteered. You signed up for an immersive psychological study — you knew that going in. What you didn't know was the content, because knowing the content would have invalidated the results. You went under, and you woke up believing you were in a facility that would kill you, and for sixteen days you lived as if death were real. And you were extraordinary. All of you. Every single person in this room did things under simulated pressure that I will be studying for years."

"The deaths," Drug says. She is standing near the front, her arms crossed, her expression the same flat calm she carried through the simulation. "We felt them. We felt people die. Mainthemainstar—" She glances at Mainthemainstar, who is standing a few feet away, alive, watching her. "I grieved him. For days. That grief was real even if the death wasn't."

"Yes," WavyDragons says. "That's the part I won't pretend away. The deaths weren't real, but the grief was. The fear was. The bonds you formed were. The experience was psychologically real even though the stakes were simulated. That's why the study works — and it's also why every one of you is going to be offered counseling, support, whatever you need, for as long as you need it. What you went through was not nothing. It was simulated, but it was not nothing."

Mainthemainstar speaks. Quietly. "Drug grieved me for six days. I woke up on Day Eight and I had to watch her grieve me for the rest of the simulation, on the screens, and I couldn't tell her I was okay."

"I know," WavyDragons says. "That was one of the cruelest parts of the design, and I won't defend it except to say that it produced the most valuable data. The people who woke early and watched their friends grieve them — that was its own experiment. I'm sorry. Genuinely. It was necessary and it was cruel and both things are true."

Drug looks at Mainthemainstar. Mainthemainstar looks at Drug. Neither of them says anything, but Drug uncrosses her arms, and that is its own kind of sentence.

[[RABBIT]]
There is one person standing slightly apart from the others.

Rabbit.

He is at the edge of the group, and the space around him is not accidental — the others have left a gap, the specific distance that people leave around someone they have not decided how to feel about. Because Rabbit's situation is different from everyone else's. Rabbit did not just die and wake up and watch. Rabbit died, woke up, and then went back in — as a hunter. To hunt the people he had survived beside.

"Rabbit," WavyDragons says. "You should explain this yourself. It's yours to explain."

Rabbit steps forward. The level face, the unchanging face, except now — in the real world, with everyone watching — it is not unchanging. It is the face of someone who did something and has to account for it.

"I died on Day Fourteen," Rabbit says. "In the elevator. I pressed the button so Rally Mally wouldn't have to risk his last life. I woke up here. And I watched — I watched the screens, the same as everyone. And then WavyDragons offered me something."

"I offered him re-entry," WavyDragons says. "As a hunter. The Day Fifteen hunt needed antagonists who knew the participants — who could hunt them intelligently, personally, in a way that produced real fear. I offered the role to people who had already woken up. Rabbit took it. So did alex."

A young man near the back raises a hand slightly — alex_awesomeness, and beside him stands Schwalbe, the two of them clearly together, clearly brothers, Schwalbe alive and whole and standing next to the brother who hunted his teammates.

"alex is my brother," Schwalbe says, to the room. "He wasn't a participant — he was an observer. He watched me 'die' on Day Thirteen, on these screens, and when WavyDragons offered the hunter role, alex took it. To get inside the thing that he watched kill me. I've already forgiven him. He was avenging a death that didn't happen, but he didn't know that — not really, not in his gut. The screens make it feel real even when you're on the outside."

The room's attention returns to Rabbit. Because alex is explained — an outsider, a brother, caught in the simulation's emotional pull. But Rabbit was one of them. Rabbit chose to hunt his own.

"Why," Polaris says. She is out of her chair now, standing, facing Rabbit across the room. She — who Rabbit spared in the drawer, who lived because Rabbit chose not to kill her — has the most standing to ask. "You hunted us. You killed CharliSpirit. You put your hands on her throat. Why would you go back in and do that?"

"Because I wasn't ready to leave," Rabbit says. "I know how that sounds. But I died on Day Fourteen and I woke up here and I felt — robbed. Sixteen days of being someone, of leading the Syndicate, of being part of something, and it ended for me on Day Fourteen with a button. And WavyDragons offered me a way back in. And I took it, because I wasn't ready for my story to be over." He looks at CharliSpirit, who is watching him from across the room. "And then I was back in, and I was a hunter, and I had to actually do it. I had to hunt people I cared about. I killed CharliSpirit and it felt real, because in the simulation it was real, and I have to live with the fact that I chose to be the person who did that."

"You spared me," Polaris says.

"I spared you because by the time I found you, I understood what I'd done to CharliSpirit," Rabbit says. "And I couldn't do it again. Sparing you didn't undo killing her. But it was the only thing I had left to give." He looks at the room. "I betrayed you. All of you. I was one of you and I chose to hunt you. I don't expect to be forgiven for it. I just want you to understand that I know what I did."

The room is quiet.

CharliSpirit crosses it.

She walks to Rabbit — the woman whose throat he held in a fake IKEA, who asked him if she'd survive and then didn't — and she stops in front of him, and she studies his face for a long moment.

"It felt real when you killed me," she says. "I want you to know that. I felt my throat close. I felt myself go. And then I woke up here and I had to sit with the fact that someone I trusted did that to me." A pause. "But I've had hours to watch the rest of it. I watched you spare Polaris. I watched it cost you. And I think—" She stops. Starts again. "I think the simulation broke something in all of us, a little. You just broke in a more visible way. I'm not ready to forgive you. But I understand you. And that's a start."

Rabbit nods. It is not absolution. It is more than he expected.

[[THE FINALISTS]]
WavyDragons turns to the three finalists — Polaris, space_fan, JIKOSU — standing now near their chairs.

"You three," he says. "The last three. You survived sixteen simulated days and a hunt and a finale. And I want to talk about each of you, because each of you survived differently, and the differences are the most interesting data I have."

He looks at JIKOSU.

"JIKOSU. You survived on tactics, until tactics weren't enough, and then you survived on luck. The warehouse — the boxes that collapsed and nearly killed you and then hid you — that was the simulation's randomness, not your skill, and you'd be the first to admit it. You're a tactician who got humbled by chance and survived anyway. You made it to the final three on a combination of skill and fortune that you didn't fully control, and watching you adapt to that loss of control was fascinating. You did terribly and you did wonderfully and both were real."

JIKOSU nods. It is an accurate assessment. The tactical mind cannot argue with it.

He looks at space_fan.

"space_fan. You survived because of the cat." He pauses. Something crosses his face — the same anomaly-noticing stillness from the arena. "We'll come back to the cat. But you survived because you trusted something other than yourself — you read Orchid, you trusted her senses over your own, you let an animal lead you through the dark maze and the hunt. Most people trust themselves first. You trusted the cat. And the cat kept you alive. That's a kind of wisdom I didn't expect and can't fully explain."

space_fan does not respond. She is looking around the room — for the cat, Polaris realizes, space_fan is looking for Orchid, and Orchid is not here, because Orchid was simulated and the simulation is over.

He looks at Polaris.

"And Polaris. You won. The underdog. The one with the ankle that I designed to limit you — I gave you that injury on Day Six specifically to handicap you, to see how someone performs under a persistent physical disadvantage. And you performed better than anyone. You couldn't run, couldn't climb, couldn't fight at the front. So you watched. You learned. You positioned. And when the finale came down to the moment, you used the one thing your lost team left you — the time-freeze, the ability the Floofs won on Day Five and never spent. You inherited it as the last Floof, and you held it until the exact right instant, and you won with it." WavyDragons smiles. "You won the way underdogs win. Not by becoming strong. By using what you have better than anyone expected. It was the most satisfying outcome I could have hoped for."

"I killed them," Polaris says. Quietly. "space_fan and JIKOSU. In the freeze. I had them frozen and I—"

"You extracted them," WavyDragons says. "They woke up here. Look — they're standing right there. You didn't kill anyone, Polaris. You won a simulation. The two people you 'killed' are alive and they don't blame you and neither should you."

space_fan crosses to Polaris. JIKOSU follows. The three finalists stand together — the three who survived to the end, who fought each other in the arena, who are all alive.

"You won fair," space_fan says. "The freeze was yours. You earned it eleven days ago and you saved it for when it mattered. I'd have done the same if Soup had been mine."

"I'm not mad about losing to a frozen moment," JIKOSU says. "Tactically, it was the correct use of a held resource. I respect it." A pause, and then something that is almost a smile on the tactical face. "I'm glad it was you. If it couldn't be me, I'm glad it was you."

[[THE MONEY]]
"There's the matter of compensation," WavyDragons says.

"Every participant is compensated for the study — that was in the agreement you signed. But the three finalists receive something more. For surviving to the end, each of you receives one hundred thousand dollars."

The number lands in the room.

"Polaris, space_fan, JIKOSU — one hundred thousand each. The winner doesn't receive more than the other finalists; making it to the final three was the achievement, and all three of you achieved it. The money is real. It's already been transferred. When you leave here, it's in your accounts."

JIKOSU processes the number with the same tactical evenness he processes everything. space_fan's eyes widen slightly. Polaris — Polaris, who has thought of herself as someone who hasn't died yet rather than someone who survives — does not seem to know what to do with the information that she has won a hundred thousand dollars by being the last one standing in a simulation.

"That's—" Polaris says.

"Life-changing money, for most people," WavyDragons says. "Yes. You earned it. All three of you. Spend it well."

[[ORCHID]]
And then Polaris feels something at her feet.

No — not Polaris. space_fan.

space_fan feels it first — a pressure against her ankle, soft, warm, accompanied by a sound she knows better than almost any sound in the world. A purr. A specific purr, loud and rumbling, the purr of a small animal that has decided a person is its person.

space_fan looks down.

Orchid is at her feet.

The cat is sitting on the clean floor of the real-world facility, looking up at space_fan with the specific calm of a cat who has been doing exactly what she meant to be doing. The three black stripes are visible on her back. She is real — physically, undeniably real, present in a room she should not be able to be present in, because Orchid was simulated, Orchid existed only in the facility that does not exist, Orchid was data and not flesh.

And here she is. Purring. At space_fan's feet. In the real world.

space_fan drops to her knees and gathers the cat into her arms, and Orchid goes limp in the boneless way she always goes limp, and presses her head under space_fan's chin, and purrs.

"Orchid," space_fan says. Her voice cracks completely now — the thing that held through sixteen days and a finale and a resurrection finally breaking. "How are you—you were—"

WavyDragons is staring at the cat.

His expression is the one from the arena — the anomaly-noticing stillness, but deeper now, because in the arena he could choose not to address it and here it is sitting on the floor of his facility purring and he cannot pretend it away. He looks at Orchid the way a person looks at something that violates a rule they wrote themselves.

"That's not possible," he says. Quietly. Mostly to himself.

"What?" space_fan says, holding the cat.

WavyDragons does not answer immediately. He is looking at Orchid, and the look is the look of someone running through every explanation he has and finding that none of them fit. The cat was simulated. The cat was code. The cat was a gift he programmed into the birthday on Day Eleven, a comfort object, a variable. The cat cannot be in this room because the cat was never a physical thing.

And yet.

"The cat was part of the simulation," WavyDragons says, slowly. "She was — she shouldn't be here. There's no mechanism by which she could be here. A simulated entity cannot—" He stops. He looks at space_fan holding the purring cat, and he makes a decision, the specific decision of someone who has encountered something he cannot explain and is choosing not to explain it. "I don't know how she's here. I genuinely don't. There was a — there were some irregularities in the simulation near the end. Anomalies I didn't author. I'll be looking into them for a long time."

He does not say more. He does not say the thing that the irregularities suggest — that somewhere in the simulation, something happened that should not be able to happen, that a creature made of code became a creature made of flesh, that the glitch in the arena was the moment it changed. He does not say it because he does not understand it and because some part of him, looking at space_fan holding the cat, decides that some things are better left as they are.

"Keep her," he says, finally. "Whatever she is. However she got here. She's yours. She was always yours."

space_fan holds Orchid and does not ask any more questions. The cat is real. The cat is here. The cat survived a thing in the arena that a cat should not survive, and then followed space_fan out of a simulation that a simulated thing should not be able to leave, and is now purring in her arms in the real world. space_fan does not need to understand it. She got the cat back. Again. For real, this time. For good.

The secret of how stays a secret. Even WavyDragons does not know it. Maybe no one ever will.

[[CLOSING]]
The room slowly empties.

Thirty people — alive, all of them, the entire roster that started the simulation sixteen days ago — begin to leave the bright clean facility in ones and twos and small groups, reuniting, reconnecting, the formerly-dead and the survivors mixing freely now that death is revealed to have been a door rather than an end. Toad finds the Wardens he started with. Loyal2Schwab and Golden and the Floofs who woke before them. The Syndicate — Drug and Mainthemainstar standing close, finally, the grief resolved into something else.

Polaris stands near the door with her hundred thousand dollars and her healed ankle and the strange weight of having won.

JIKOSU finds her there. "You going to be okay?" he asks.

"I don't know," Polaris says. Honestly. "I killed two people last night. They're standing right over there, alive, and I still feel like I killed them. The simulation was — it was real while it was real. I don't know how long it takes for that to stop being real."

"A while, probably," JIKOSU says. "For all of us." He looks across the room at space_fan, who is still holding Orchid, the cat that should not exist. "Some of us got stranger souvenirs than others."

Polaris almost laughs. "Yeah."

WavyDragons approaches the three of them one last time — Polaris, JIKOSU, and space_fan with the cat, the three finalists, near the door.

"Thank you," he says. "All three of you. For sixteen days I watched you survive things I designed to be unsurvivable, and you survived them with more grace and more humanity than I had any right to expect. The study will change how we understand a lot of things. But more than the data — I'm just glad you're all okay. I mean that. I always meant that, even when I was the voice telling you that you were going to die. Especially then."

"Were you really proud of us?" space_fan asks. "You said that. In the simulation. More than once."

"Every time I said it," WavyDragons says. "I built a nightmare and put thirty people in it and watched them be brave inside it. Of course I was proud. I'm still proud. Go home. Live your lives. You earned them — twice, now. Once by being born and once by surviving the thing I made."

He steps back. He smiles — the genuine smile, the Easter smile, the smile of someone who built something and is, despite everything, glad of what it revealed.

"Goodbye," he says. "And thank you. Truly."

The three finalists walk out of the facility into a world that is real — Polaris with her healed ankle, JIKOSU with his whole shoulder, space_fan with a cat in her arms that walked out of a simulation and into flesh and that no one, not even the man who built the simulation, can explain.

Behind them, the screens have gone dark. The chairs are empty. The simulation is over. Sixteen days of a facility that does not exist, of challenges that killed no one, of animals made of light and code, of a hunt and a wheel and a maze and a birthday — all of it concluded, all of it filed, all of it survived.

Thirty people are alive.

Three of them are richer.

One of them is carrying a small impossible cat that purrs against her chest as she steps into the daylight, real and warm and entirely, against every rule that should govern it, alive.

The daylight is bright. The air is real. The world on the other side of the white was real, exactly as Soup promised.

space_fan holds Orchid up to the light. The cat blinks in it — slow, content, the three black stripes catching the sun.

"Come on," space_fan says. "Let's go home."

And they do.
