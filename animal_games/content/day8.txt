[[DAY]]
DAY 8 — THE ALTERED ROOMS

[[SYSTEM_STATE]]
Day index: 8.
Participant count: 26.
Challenge type: interior horror — altered geometry, fear-responsive environment.
Animal presence: none inside sectors.
Lethality: elevated. System selection active.

[[NARRATIVE]]
Nobody sleeps well.

The corridors are not designed for sleeping. The floor is the same hard-smooth surface it has always been — functional, cleanable, indifferent to the human body's preference for give. Twenty-six people lie on it in configurations that prioritize proximity to their teams over proximity to comfort, and the night passes the way nights pass when you're sleeping in a hallway because the rooms you've lived in for a week decided to become something else while you were out looking for Easter eggs.

The animals don't sleep either.

The Drake is at the far end of the Wardens' corridor stretch, settled but awake, electric veins running at the low dim pulse of something that is conserving energy while maintaining full awareness. It has not moved closer to the Wardens' sector entrance. It has not moved further from it. It has chosen a distance and is holding it with the precision of something that has calculated exactly how close to the wrongness it is willing to be.

Soup hovers at the Floofs' corridor ceiling, rotation nearly stopped, luminescence at its lowest register — not dark, just quiet, the glow of something that is present without insisting on it. Ninnginni wakes twice during the night and both times Soup is in the same position, which is either comforting or unsettling depending on whether you think it means Soup is watching over them or watching for something.

The Bunny sits precisely three meters from the Syndicate's sector entrance. Facing it. Ears forward. It has been in this position since the teams returned from the Easter hunt and it has not moved — not the micro-adjustments of an animal at rest, not the ear-tracking that usually accompanies its awareness of ambient sound. Complete stillness, focused entirely on the doorway, the posture of something monitoring a boundary it has chosen not to cross.

Rabbit wakes at some point in the deep middle of the night and sees the Bunny and understands, with the clarity that arrives at 3 AM when everything else is stripped away, that the Bunny is afraid.

He has never seen the Bunny afraid.

He doesn't go back to sleep.

[[MORNING]]
The facility's light shifts from its night register to its day register at the usual time, which means the usual time still exists, which means whatever happened to the rooms didn't happen to the schedule. Small comfort. Measurable comfort. The kind of comfort that matters because the alternative is no comfort at all.

Twenty-six people sit up in a corridor and do the things that bodies do in the first minutes of a day — stretch, breathe, orient. The hierarchy of needs asserts itself in the usual order: water first, then food, then situation. Water is available from corridor panels. Food is not available from anywhere. The tables in the hub have not risen. The morning meal is not coming.

"Two days without a proper meal call," CrystalWolf says. She's at the corridor water panel, filling a container. "Yesterday was the eggs. Today is nothing."

"The eggs were a lot of food," Ashborn says.

"The eggs were challenge food," CrystalWolf says. "It's different."

She's right. It is different. Food earned by competition carries a different nutritional quality than food given by routine — not chemically, psychologically. The body processes them differently. One is sustenance. The other is a prize that happens to be edible.

Nobody moves toward the sector entrances. Twenty-six people in a corridor with three open doorways leading to three altered sectors, and nobody approaches them, the way nobody approaches a sound they heard in the night until the morning is bright enough to make the investigation feel like a choice rather than a compulsion.

But the morning is here. And the rooms are still there. And at some point, someone has to look.

[[THE LOOKING]]
Drug goes first.

She doesn't announce it. She stands, walks to the Syndicate's sector entrance, and looks through the doorway with the specific attention of someone who catalogues what she sees before she reacts to it. Behind her, Mainthemainstar stands, walks to the same doorway, and stands beside her with the soft-focus attention that covers everything without fixing on anything.

They look.

The central room is recognizable the way a face is recognizable after significant injury — the structure is there, the features are there, but the arrangement has been altered by something that understood the original layout and chose to disagree with it. The sleeping platforms are on the ceiling. Not collapsed, not displaced — mounted cleanly on the ceiling surface, bedding still in place, the orientation correct in every way except the most fundamental one. They are upside down relative to gravity and right-side up relative to themselves.

The water panel is on the wrong wall. Three meters left of where it has been for seven days, on a wall that didn't exist yesterday — a new partition that bisects the room at an angle that doesn't correspond to any structural line, creating two spaces where there was one, neither of them the right shape for anything.

Beyond the central room, a corridor that used to be straight now curves, and beyond the curve is a doorway that shouldn't exist, and beyond the doorway is darkness that the facility's light doesn't reach, which is new — the light has always reached everywhere.

"The platforms are upside down," hollow Camillia says. She has joined them at the threshold.

"The platforms are on the ceiling," Drug says. "They're right-side up. The ceiling is wrong."

"That's worse," hollow Camillia says.

"Yes," Drug says.

In the Wardens' sector, Shroud has stepped one foot across the threshold — not entering, testing — and is looking at the corridor beyond the central room. It curves. Their corridor has always been straight. Now it bends to the right, gently, and where it bends a doorway has appeared that leads to a room none of them have seen before. Through that doorway: another doorway. Through that one: a wall where the corridor should continue.

"There are new rooms," Shroud reports, stepping back to the corridor. "At least two spaces that weren't there. And the corridor geometry is different — it turns right but the room beyond it is to the left."

"That's not possible in this footprint," Duccarian says. He is looking at the ceiling through the doorway. "And the ceiling height is lower in the second visible room. By at least forty centimeters."

"So the space is physically inconsistent," Schwalbe says. Not a question. He is at the threshold beside Shroud, looking at the rooms with the forward-focused assessment of someone who will be entering first and is already planning how.

"Physically impossible," Duccarian says. "Which is different from inconsistent and more interesting."

In the Floofs' sector, Ninnginni has not stepped across the threshold. He's standing at it, looking, the way you look at water before you know if it's deep. The central room is rearranged — sleeping platforms relocated to a configuration that suggests a different room's layout imposed on this room's dimensions. Two beds where four were. None where five were. The remaining platforms clustered against the far wall in a formation that looks deliberate but follows no logic.

And there's a smell. Not unpleasant — architectural, the smell of new materials, surfaces that haven't been breathed on yet, the chemical novelty of a space recently built or recently changed.

"It smells new," lumallie says.

"It smells wrong," CharliSpirit says. She is looking at the room with the mapping instinct already active, already trying to build the spatial picture, and the picture isn't building. The room's proportions don't match her memory and the mismatch isn't the kind that resolves with better data. It's the kind that gets worse.

"I can't map it from here," she says. "The angles aren't consistent."

Nobody enters. Not yet. Twenty-six people at three thresholds, the rooms patient behind them, the corridor safe around them, the morning light unchanged and the schedule intact and the food absent and the animals at their chosen distances, watching doorways they won't approach.

The Bunny has not moved from its three-meter position. Its ears are forward. Its red eyes hold the doorway.

Rabbit crouches beside it. "What's in there?"

*Rooms that weren't there before. They move.*

"Move how?"

*They rearrange. The space is alive in a way the other challenges weren't. It responds. It listens.*

"Listens for what?"

*Everything. But it acts on fear.*

Rabbit stands. Looks at Drug. Drug looks back. Between them the information passes without words — Drug heard enough, Drug heard fear, Drug's expression shifts by exactly nothing, which is how Drug looks when she's processing something she intends to use.

The corridor holds twenty-six people and three animals and three open doorways and a silence that is not peaceful and not panicked but something in between — the specific quiet of a group that knows it is about to be asked to do something it does not want to do and is waiting for the asking.

The figure's voice arrives.

[[THE BRIEFING]]
"Good morning."

The warm-controlled tone. No amusement in it today — not the Easter lightness, not the Day 6 weather-report delivery. Just the tone, and underneath it the weight, and the weight is heavier today than it has been.

"You've seen your sectors. What you're looking at is real — the restructuring is physical, permanent for the duration of the challenge, and responsive. The space reads your collective physiological state in real time. Heart rate. Adrenaline. The measurable output of a body that is afraid."

A pause.

"The calmer you are, the more stable the geometry remains. The more afraid you are, the more it shifts. Doorways close. Ceilings lower. Rooms rearrange between visits. The space is not hostile by default — it becomes hostile in proportion to your fear."

Twenty-six people absorb this. The rooms they've been looking at — the inverted platforms, the impossible corridors, the wrong-angled walls — are not just different. They are listening.

"Something is inside each sector that belongs to your team. You'll know it when you find it. Retrieve it and return to your sector entrance. First team to return with their object wins."

"The animals?" space_fan asks. Her voice carries the specific tone of someone who already knows the answer and is asking anyway because the answer needs to be said out loud.

"Your animals will wait here. This challenge is yours alone."

The corridor recalculates. No Drake navigation. No Soup luminescence. No Bunny foresight. Twenty-six people and three altered sectors and whatever is inside them, faced with nothing but each other and the quality of their collective nerve.

"Lethality is active," the figure says. "The system has already run its selection. You don't know the outcome yet."

The weight of this sits in the corridor like a physical object — something that takes up space, something that has to be walked around.

"One more thing." The voice carries something that is not quite warning. "There is a door in each sector that leads somewhere it shouldn't. You'll know it if you find it. Don't go through it."

A beat.

"Begin when you're ready."

The voice is gone. The corridor is quiet. Twenty-six people and a choice that is not a choice — the rooms must be entered, the objects must be found, the challenge must be met. The only real choice is how.

[[THE ENTRIES]]
The Wardens enter first because Schwalbe enters first, and Schwalbe enters first because that is what Schwalbe does.

He steps across the threshold with the specific energy of someone who has decided that the doing is preferable to the deciding, and behind him the Wardens follow in formation — The Reddening Day near the center, space_fan at the edge, Duccarian behind Schwalbe reading the geometry, Shroud already cataloguing environmental data, CrystalWolf watching for the patterns she finds in everything. Ashborn, Rally Mally, Orphan Annihilator filling the formation's remaining positions. Nine people entering a space that is listening to their heartbeats.

"Together," space_fan says from the edge. "Nobody separates."

The Floofs enter second. Ninnginni at the threshold calls a fast input round — two words each, the protocol's pulse-check before action.

"Scared. Ready," CharliSpirit says.

"Alert. Hungry," lumallie says.

"Focused. Cold," Galaxy says.

"Steady. Uncertain," Godly_Aura says.

"Nervous. Determined," Polaris says. Her ankle — Day 6's inheritance — will be louder in there.

"Present. Watchful," Revenant says.

"Calm. Angry," Elv says.

"Afraid. Committed," Ninnginni says. Then: "The protocol hasn't been tested in shifting geometry. If it doesn't hold, we put it down and move as people. Understood?"

Eight nods. They go in.

The Syndicate enters last because Rabbit takes thirty seconds to crouch beside the Bunny one more time.

"Anything else?"

*The rooms have five spaces each that weren't there before. They loop — you'll exit a room and enter one you've already been in, from a different angle. The object is in the furthest room, but furthest is not fixed. The distance depends on you.* A pause. *On how together you are.*

"Together," Rabbit repeats.

*Coherent,* the Bunny says. *The word is coherent.*

Rabbit stands. Looks at his team. Drug is at the entrance, expression level, breathing even. JIKOSU beside her, already reading the room beyond the threshold. janhk, NinjaFlout, hollow Camillia, Lyrix, Akarimmu filling positions. Mainthemainstar at the back, soft-focus attention already inside the sector, already reading the altered space.

"Drug leads," Rabbit says. The team looks at him. "She's the calmest person here and the rooms are listening. Stay near her."

Drug doesn't react to this. She just walks through the doorway, and behind her the Syndicate follows the person whose heartbeat is telling the room: nothing to fear here.

[[INSIDE — THE WARDENS]]
The first room is the central room they knew, rearranged. The sleeping platforms on the ceiling. The water panel on the wrong wall. The proportions compressed in a way that is felt in the chest before it is measured by the eye — the same dimensions, technically, but the experience of standing in them is different, smaller, the room pushing inward without the walls actually moving.

"Breathe," Schwalbe says from the front. He says it to himself as much as to the group.

The second room is through the doorway that shouldn't exist. It opens onto the corridor that curves wrong — right instead of left — and the inner ear rejects the difference before the mind can process it. The walls are closer. The ceiling is lower. Not dangerously — just enough that Ashborn, the tallest, feels it.

"Ceiling dropped," Ashborn says.

"That's us," Duccarian says immediately. "The geometry is reading our fear. Keep your breathing controlled."

The third room is the first one that loops. They enter through a door on the east wall and find themselves in a room they've been in — the second room, viewed from a different angle, the curved corridor visible through a window that wasn't there when they walked through it.

"We're above where we just were," Shroud says.

"That's not possible," CrystalWolf says.

"The rules aren't our rules," Duccarian says. "It's possible here."

The Wardens are getting quieter. This is how their fear works — not noise, not panic, silence. The more disorienting the geometry, the less they speak, and the less they speak, the more the room reads their silence as fear, and the walls shift inward by increments felt in the body rather than seen by the eye.

Schwalbe identifies this.

"Talk," he says. Not loudly — firmly, the instruction of someone who has recognized the feedback loop and is interrupting it. "Everyone talk. About anything. The food from yesterday. The weather outside. Toad's jokes. Anything."

"Toad didn't tell jokes," Rally Mally says.

"He told one," Orphan Annihilator says. "Day Two. It wasn't good."

"What was it?"

"I don't remember. I remember it wasn't good."

"That's the whole review," Rally Mally says.

It isn't much. But the ceiling stabilizes. The walls stop their advance. The room is still wrong, still impossible — but it holds still while nine people talk about a joke nobody remembers from a person who isn't here, and the talking is what keeps the walls from closing.

They move forward. Talking.

[[INSIDE — THE FLOOFS]]
CharliSpirit begins mapping automatically. It's what she does — every new space gets the treatment, the mental cartography building at the pace of her feet, turns and junctions and distances assembling in her head.

The first room is navigable. Altered — sleeping platforms in a wrong configuration — but stable. She maps it in thirty seconds. The doorways are where doorways could be. The logic is strange but followable.

The second room breaks the map.

She enters through the north wall and the room she enters is the room she just left, mirrored. Sleeping platforms on the opposite side. The doorway she came through is on the wrong wall. She turns to check the door behind her and the door behind her isn't there.

"The door is gone," CharliSpirit says.

"There's a door here," lumallie says, from the other side of a wall that shouldn't be between them. "But it goes somewhere else."

"Where?"

"A room I haven't been in."

"I haven't been in any of these rooms," Galaxy says, from a third position, his voice carrying through walls that transmit sound in ways sound shouldn't travel in a space this size.

CharliSpirit tries to update the map. The map won't update. The data contradicts itself — the room she's standing in cannot be where it is and also be what it is, and the cartographic instinct that has been her contribution since Day 1 jams on the contradiction like a gear meeting a gear that turns the wrong way.

She freezes.

Not a collapse, not a visible break. She just stops. Her hands, which have been gesturing the map into existence the way they always do, go still at her sides. Her eyes are open and focused on the wall and they are not seeing the wall. They are seeing the map trying to exist and failing.

"CharliSpirit," Ninnginni says.

Nothing.

"CharliSpirit." He is beside her now — the geometry let him through, or he found a path, or he simply moved toward her with the directness of someone who has identified the priority. "Look at me."

She looks at him. Her eyes have the quality of someone looking at a person and at a broken system at the same time.

"The map doesn't work here," Ninnginni says.

"I know." Her voice is very controlled, which is how CharliSpirit sounds when nothing is controlled.

"Then put it down."

"I can't put it down. It's how I navigate. It's how I—" She stops. "It's the thing I do."

"I know. Put it down anyway."

This is not the protocol. The protocol would have input and synthesis and commitment. The protocol requires data, and the data is broken. Ninnginni — standing in a room that shouldn't exist, beside a person whose primary skill has just been made useless — puts the protocol down the same way he's asking CharliSpirit to put the map down.

He takes her hand.

"We walk," he says. "No map. No system. Just forward. I'll be here."

CharliSpirit's breathing changes. Not calms — changes. The frequency of someone who has been holding a structure and has agreed to release it. The room responds: a doorway appears where a wall was, not aggressively, just present now, the geometry easing the way a grip eases.

"Okay," CharliSpirit says.

They walk. Eight Floofs in a space that can't be mapped, moving forward by feel and trust and the agreement that sometimes the system you built isn't the system you need.

[[INSIDE — THE SYNDICATE]]
Drug leads and the room lets her.

This is the thing — the rooms respond to fear, and Drug is not afraid. Not performing fearlessness, not managing anxiety. Something in her constitution, in whatever the last seven days have built or broken or rearranged in her, has produced a state that the room reads as neutral. The geometry around Drug is different from the geometry around the others. She walks and the walls hold. The ceiling stays. The doors she approaches remain where doors should be.

Her section of the space is almost navigable — the difficulty of an altered room without the hostility of a room working against you.

"Stay near Drug," Rabbit says, and the team contracts, nine people orbiting the person whose body is lying to the room on everyone's behalf.

Mainthemainstar is behind Drug. His attention covers the full space — not the room they're in, the rooms they've been through, the rooms ahead, the whole architecture of the altered sector existing in his perception as a complete shape rather than a sequence of spaces. He navigates by the same instinct that called platform drops and found Easter eggs: knowing where the path is before his eyes confirm it.

"Left," he says, and they go left.

"Down," he says, and there are stairs that weren't there, and they go down.

"Through here," he says, and a doorway exists because he said it would.

Drug and Mainthemainstar at the front of the Syndicate — one holding the room calm, the other reading the path through it. The team moves behind them with the specific trust of people who have decided that the two in front know something the rest of them don't, and the deciding is enough.

The fourth room is where Mainthemainstar stops.

Not at the object — they haven't reached the object yet. At a door. An ordinary door in an extraordinary position, set in a wall that shouldn't have a door, slightly ajar, and through the gap something that is neither inside nor outside the sector. Something that is somewhere else entirely.

The wrong door.

The team stops behind him. They can feel it — not through Mainthemainstar's perception, through their own. The door has a quality. A pull. The air near it moves differently, as if the space on the other side is breathing, not with lungs but with the rhythm of a place that is alive in a way that even the altered rooms aren't.

Rabbit is beside Mainthemainstar. "The figure said don't."

"I know," Mainthemainstar says.

"Do you want to?"

A pause. The door sits slightly ajar. Whatever is on the other side breathes its slow architectural breath.

"No," Mainthemainstar says. "But I think I'm supposed to eventually."

They leave it. But it exists now — seen and acknowledged and deferred rather than avoided. The door stays slightly ajar behind them. It does not close.

The fifth room.

Drug enters first. In the center of the room, on a surface that is neither table nor shelf but something that functions as both, is a small collection of fur. White and brown. Fine-textured. The quality of rabbit fur shed naturally rather than cut.

The Bunny's fur. From Day 1. The physical remnant of the moment the Bunny chose Rabbit — preserved by a system that keeps everything and placed here for nine people to find in a room that only exists because the facility decided it should.

Rabbit picks it up. It is lighter than anything that means this much should be.

"That's it," Drug says.

"That's it," Rabbit says.

The room responds — geometry simplifying, looping rooms unraveling, impossible angles straightening, the corridor back to the entrance assembling itself from confusion into clarity. The way out opens.

Mainthemainstar turns back once. Toward the wrong door. Then forward.

They walk out.

[[THE FLOOFS' OBJECT]]
The Floofs reach their object in the fourth room.

A mark on the wall. Deliberate notation, written in something that functions as ink on a surface that functions as stone, in handwriting that three of them recognize immediately and the rest identify within seconds.

Loyal2Schwab's junction mark. The first one. The original notation from Day 1 — the mark he made in the labyrinth, the systematic mind leaving its first breadcrumb, the moment before anybody knew his name or understood that the person making marks on walls was building the architecture that would save them.

The system kept it.

Ninnginni is still holding CharliSpirit's hand. He lets go gently. Steps forward. Puts his palm flat on the wall beside the mark.

"Hey," he says. To the mark. To the wall. To the person who made it.

He lifts the mark from the surface — it comes away like a decal, the system allowing the retrieval the way a parent allows a child to take something precious from a shelf. He holds it in both hands.

"We have it."

The geometry eases. Doorways align. Eight Floofs walk out — CharliSpirit without the map she's carried in her head for eight days, Ninnginni with a dead teammate's handwriting in his hands.

[[THE WARDENS' OBJECT]]
The Wardens reach their object last. They've been talking the whole way — Schwalbe's instruction holding, the conversation keeping the geometry stable. The Reddening Day has done most of the talking, the patient circuit of their nightly rounds translated into a steady narration that fills the space the way their presence fills it: reliably, without asking for acknowledgment.

The fifth room opens for them not because they found it but because they earned it — collective fear managed enough that the space has decided they can have what they came for.

On the floor is a notebook.

Space_fan sees it and stops.

Her notebook. Not from the facility — from before, from whatever came before this place, from the life on the other side of the door they walked through on Day 1 and haven't walked through since. She doesn't have it here. She has never had it here. It is the specific blue-covered composition book she kept in a drawer in a room in a life the facility should not have access to.

The system pulled it from her memory. Or from somewhere else. The distinction may not exist here.

She picks it up. It weighs what a notebook weighs. She opens it. Inside is handwriting she recognizes — her own — and she can see the shapes of letters and the logic of sentences and the meaning slides away like water off a surface that doesn't want to be wet. She can see the words but she cannot read them.

She closes it. Holds it.

"That's it," she says.

The Wardens walk out.

[[EXIT]]
The Syndicate emerges first.

The corridor light is facility-white, sourceless, ordinary. After the altered rooms it feels like a physical substance — something that can be stood in and leaned against. Drug stands at the corridor wall and breathes. Just breathes. The calm that held the geometry open is still present but thinner now, the effort of it legible for the first time.

The Bunny is exactly where it was. Three meters from the entrance, red eyes tracking the team as they emerge. It doesn't approach Rabbit. It doesn't approach the fur. It looks at the team and counts — one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine — and its ears settle into the position that means: complete.

The Floofs emerge second. Soup descends from the ceiling to hover beside Ninnginni and its luminescence shifts brighter, for a moment, the light of something that waited and is rewarded.

*You came back,* Soup says.

"We came back," Ninnginni says.

The Wardens emerge third. The Drake lifts its head as they come through. Its electric veins pulse — the sharp bright reading, the assessment — and this time the assessment concludes: the team is out. The team is whole.

*Still here,* the Drake communicates. *Still watching.*

"Still here," space_fan says.

Twenty-six people in the corridor. Three objects retrieved. The figure's voice:

"Velocity Syndicate. First retrieval. Challenge winners."

A pause. And then the pause continues. And the pause is too long. And the pause has a texture to it, the specific weight of something that is about to be said and cannot be unsaid, and twenty-six people feel this weight in the corridor the way they felt the cold on Day 6 — not as temperature, as knowledge.

"The system has completed its selection."

[[THE SELECTION]]
Nobody breathes.

"Mainthemainstar."

The word arrives in the corridor and does something to the air. Not a sound — the removal of sound. The specific silence that a name creates when the name is the answer to a question nobody wanted to ask.

Drug's head turns.

The movement is involuntary — not the controlled observation she carried all day, not the calm that held the geometry. Just her head turning fast, the motion of someone whose body has received information before her mind can process it. She looks at Mainthemainstar.

He is at the back of the Syndicate's group. He has been there since they emerged — not the center, not the front, the back, the soft-focus attention facing outward the way it always faces, covering everything without fixing on anything. He hears his name. He doesn't flinch. He doesn't close his eyes.

He looks at Drug.

The look between them is not long — three seconds, four — but it contains seven days. Sitting in rooms together. Staying when staying was what the room needed. Twenty-seven. Twenty-seven. Thanks for staying. I wasn't going anywhere.

"Mainthemainstar," the figure says again, and the repetition is confirmation.

He looks at Rabbit. Rabbit is holding the Bunny's fur — the remnant of a choosing, the first moment made physical. Mainthemainstar looks at the fur and then at Rabbit and the communication is brief:

Take care of it.

He looks at the Syndicate. JIKOSU, whose tactical attention has gone very still. janhk. NinjaFlout. Hollow Camillia, jaw set the way jaws set when setting them is the alternative to something worse. Lyrix. Akarimmu.

He looks at Drug last.

"I wasn't going anywhere," he says.

Drug doesn't speak. The calm — the real calm, the one that held the geometry open, the one that the room read as absence of fear — is present and it is not enough. It was enough for the rooms. It is not enough for this. She looks at him and her face is the face of someone watching something end who cannot look away and would not look away if she could.

The floor beneath Mainthemainstar shifts.

The same mechanism as Golden — a section descending smoothly, without violence, without drama. He goes down. The light stays on him. His face is the last thing visible — the soft-focus attention still active, still covering everything, still perceiving at the distance it always perceived at, seeing now whatever is below the floor and beyond the corridor and past the architecture into whatever comes after.

He does not look away from them.

The floor closes. The corridor is continuous. The light does not remember what it just illuminated.

The Syndicate is eight.

[[AFTER]]
Twenty-five people in the corridor.

The number adjusts itself in the space between the people who remain. Not announced, not calculated. Just present. Twenty-five.

Drug sits down.

Not against a wall — on the floor, in the middle of the corridor, in the space where Mainthemainstar was standing. She sits there and doesn't cry and doesn't speak and doesn't move and the Syndicate forms around her the way the Syndicate forms around whatever needs forming around — the loose configuration that gives space and coverage simultaneously.

Rabbit sits beside her. He places the Bunny's fur on the floor between them. It is very small. It is impossibly light. It is a remnant of a moment that was saved by a system that saves everything and returns it at the moment when saving feels least like a gift.

The Bunny moves.

For the first time since the teams entered the sectors, it crosses the three-meter boundary. It walks past Rabbit — past Rabbit — to Drug, and sits beside her, and the red eyes that are always six seconds ahead look at her at zero seconds, at right now, at this moment and no other.

Drug puts her hand on the Bunny's back. It is the first time anyone other than Rabbit has touched the Bunny. The Bunny allows this. The specific permission of an animal that has decided this person, right now, needs what it can give.

They sit like this for a while.

Across the corridor, the Floofs hold Loyal2Schwab's mark. The Wardens hold space_fan's notebook. The objects sit in their bearers' hands with the weight of things that mean more than they weigh, which is the weight of every important thing.

Ninnginni looks at Soup. Soup's glow is at its lowest — not dark, just quiet.

*The wrong door,* Soup says. Not to Ninnginni. To the corridor. To whatever architecture is listening.

*The wrong door is still inside.*

Space_fan opens her notebook. The handwriting is still there. The words are still unreadable. She closes it.

The Drake communicates, very quiet: *When you're ready, you'll read it.*

"When will I be ready?"

*When the facility decides you are.*

"That's not an answer."

*No. It isn't.*

The corridor is quiet. Twenty-five people and three animals and three objects and the absence of the person who saw the wrong door and chose not to go through it and said I think I'm supposed to eventually and is gone now, the selection running its arithmetic in parallel with his navigation and his perception and his calm and his last words, which were the same words he said to Drug on Day 6 when she thanked him for staying.

I wasn't going anywhere.

The rooms are still altered. The wrong door is still inside.

Twenty-five remain.
