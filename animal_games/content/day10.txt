[[DAY]]
DAY 10 — THE SHAKING ROOM

[[SYSTEM_STATE]]
Day index: 10.
Participant count: 21.
Challenge type: endurance — balance, item preservation under escalating seismic activity.
Lethality: active.

[[NARRATIVE]]
The vibration wakes them.

Not an alarm — nothing sharp, nothing sudden. A low hum through the floor, rising through the sleeping platforms and into the body at the frequency where bone meets awareness. It feels like the facility breathing. Like the architecture itself stretching in its sleep and the stretch traveling through every surface.

Twenty-one people open their eyes in three sectors at roughly the same time, woken not by command but by the same gentle insistence of ground that has decided to move. The vibration holds for about ten seconds and then fades, leaving the floor still and the bodies awake and the morning feeling, for the first time in ten days, almost peaceful.

Almost.

The hub is warm when they arrive. The tables have risen. The food is warm — not elaborate, not calibrated, just warm. Soup and bread and something that steams. The kind of food that exists to fill you and does its job without asking for commentary. After the feast-and-famine cycle of the last three days — the Easter eggs, then nothing, then the good-not-great breakfast of Day 9 — warm food that simply exists is its own small kindness.

Nobody talks about it much. They eat. The eating is the talking.

The plates have their numbered tags. Violet, silver, red. Seven and seven and seven, the equalized count sitting at the tables with them like a fact that hasn't finished being a fact yet.

And then — nothing.

The meal ends. The tables descend. The hub is quiet. And the facility does not produce a corridor. Does not activate floor-lights. Does not deliver the figure's voice or an alarm or a direction. The hub simply exists, with twenty-one people in it, and waits.

"Is something supposed to happen?" Galaxy asks, after five minutes.

"Something always happens," Duccarian says.

"But when?"

Duccarian looks at the room. The room does not answer.

Ten minutes pass. Fifteen. Twenty-one people in a hub that has never given them free time discovering that free time in a facility designed to kill them is its own kind of stress. The body doesn't know what to do with the absence of a demand. The mind runs ahead, building scenarios, populating the blank space with everything it might contain.

Schwalbe sits with the Wardens — seven now, the two absences from Day 9 present in the formation's gaps. Rally Mally is beside Orphan Annihilator, the persistent configuration that has outlasted Ashborn's loss. The Reddening Day makes their rounds — not because there are rounds to make, but because the pattern of checking on people is The Reddening Day's way of existing in a space, and the pattern doesn't require a reason.

Ninnginni sits with the Floofs and the hour — because it is an hour, though nobody has a clock and nobody can confirm it — passes in the specific rhythm of a group that has learned to rest when resting is offered. Elv sits in their corner, watchful. Polaris stretches her ankle through a sequence that has become ritual. CharliSpirit sits and does not map the room.

The Syndicate spreads. JIKOSU stretches — the post-challenge inventory, the body's systems reporting in, though there has been no challenge yet. Drug sits near the Bunny, which has maintained its position beside her since Day 8. Rabbit is across from them. The Bunny's ears track the room with the six-second-ahead distance, but the tracking is slower today, the ambient surveillance of something that is not detecting an imminent threat.

An hour passes.

The voice arrives.

[[THE WALL]]
"Good morning," the figure says. The warm-controlled tone, and today the warmth is genuine — not performed, not calibrated, just present. "I hope the hour was useful."

Before anyone can respond, three things happen at once.

The Drake moves. Not toward the Wardens, not toward a corridor — toward the north wall of the hub, the section of smooth featureless surface that has never had anything behind it. It moves at speed, wings half-spread, electric veins at full brightness, and it hits the wall and the wall opens and the Drake goes through in one continuous motion, no pause, no hesitation, no communication.

Soup drops from the ceiling. The luminescent sphere descends at a speed none of them have seen — not the gentle drift of its usual movements, a plummet — and at floor level it changes direction horizontally and shoots toward the same wall, which is still open, and vanishes through it. No glow left behind. No message. Nothing.

The Bunny launches from Drug's side. Four seconds from sitting to gone — the full-sprint speed that it showed on the ramp of Day 6, the speed that left the Bunny-shaped impression of something that was there and then profoundly was not. It goes through the wall. The wall closes behind it.

The hub holds twenty-one people and no animals.

"Your animals have gone ahead," the figure says. The warmth is still there. "You have ten minutes to find the way through."

Twenty-one people look at the wall. The wall is smooth. Featureless. The same surface it has been for ten days. Whatever opening the animals used has closed completely, leaving no seam, no mark, no evidence.

"Ten minutes," the figure repeats. "Starting now."

The room moves.

Not the way the altered rooms moved — not fear-responsive, not geometrically impossible. The hub simply rearranges with twenty-one people in it, the walls shifting to reveal surfaces that were hidden and hide surfaces that were visible, the room becoming a puzzle while they stand in it.

"Split up," Schwalbe says immediately. "Cover every wall. Look for anything — seams, panels, levers, changes in texture."

Twenty-one people spread across the hub's perimeter, hands on walls, eyes scanning, the full-body search of a group that has ten minutes and doesn't know how fast ten minutes goes when you're inside them.

"Nothing on the south wall," Shroud reports, thirty seconds in.

"East is clear," NinjaFlout says.

"West — wait." janhk is using the same technique that found Easter eggs — running his palms along the surface, feeling for texture changes. "There's a variation here. Slight. Like the wall is thinner."

"Where?" Rabbit asks.

"Midway. About chest height. It doesn't feel like a door. It feels like the wall is different behind this section."

"Mark it. Keep looking."

Two minutes. Three. The hub's rearrangement has produced corners that weren't there before, sections of wall that angle inward, a geometry that is not impossible but is deliberately confusing — enough variation to make the search exponentially harder, enough sameness to make every section look like every other section.

"Five minutes," the figure says.

Half the time gone. No door found. The warmth in the figure's voice has not changed, which is somehow worse — the consistency of it, the figure maintaining its tone regardless of whether they live or die.

Space_fan is at the north wall — the wall the animals went through. She runs her hand across it systematically, the same section the Drake disappeared into. Nothing. She moves left. Nothing. Right. Nothing.

"It's not in the wall," Duccarian says. He is standing in the center of the hub, not searching, thinking. "The animals went through the wall. But the figure said find a way to enter the door. The door isn't in the wall."

"Then where?" Galaxy asks.

"The floor. The ceiling. Under something. The wall is where the animals went because the animals are not us. Our way in is different."

"Three minutes," the figure says.

Drug is at the hub's floor level, crouching, running her palms across the base of the walls where they meet the floor. "Duccarian's right. The floor has something — there's a ridge here. A line in the floor that goes under the wall."

Polaris drops beside her. Follows the ridge. "It goes to — there's a panel. Under the bench section. You'd have to move the bench."

"Move it," Rabbit says.

Four people converge on the bench section — a low seating structure that has been part of the hub since Day 1, unremarkable, functional, ignored. They push it aside. Beneath it: a panel in the floor with a recessed handle.

"Two minutes," the figure says.

Rabbit pulls the handle. The panel lifts. Beneath it: not a passage but a mechanical system — gears, levers, connections, the visible architecture of something that operates the wall. And somewhere in this system, a lever that opens the door.

"Which one?" NinjaFlout says, looking at a dozen mechanical components.

"One minute," the figure says.

Twenty-one people around a floor panel looking at a mechanism nobody understands, and the figure's voice begins counting.

"Ten."

Hands reach for levers. Rally Mally pulls one — nothing. Schwalbe tries another — nothing.

"Nine."

janhk feels the mechanism the way he feels everything — by texture. "This one's different. The metal is warmer."

"Eight."

He pulls it. A sound from the wall — not the opening sound, something preliminary, a lock disengaging.

"Seven."

"There's a second one," Drug says. She can see it — lower, deeper in the mechanism, partially hidden by a gear assembly.

"Six."

She reaches for it. Her arm isn't long enough. The lever is recessed too deep.

"Five."

"I can't reach it."

"Four."

Orphan Annihilator is beside her. He doesn't have longer arms. He has a different angle — the specific angle of someone who has been compensating for a bad ankle for five days and has learned to position his body in configurations that other people's bodies don't naturally find. He drops to the floor, extends his arm past the gear assembly, his shoulder at the angle that his ankle has taught him.

"Three."

His fingers find the lever. He pulls.

The wall opens.

The sound is enormous — the full mechanical release of a system that has been locked since the facility was built, the wall splitting along its center seam and swinging outward into a corridor beyond. Light spills through. Warm, directionless, the quality of a large space.

"Two," the figure says, and the word arrives into the opened doorway with the specific tone of someone who was counting down to something terrible and has just been denied the ending.

"Well done," the figure says. "Proceed."

Twenty-one people go through the wall.

[[THE ROOM]]
The corridor is short — ten meters — and it opens into something large.

The room is circular, like the mirror room, but wider. Much wider — fifty meters across, the ceiling domed high above, the lighting warm and even. The animals are here — the Drake at the room's edge, Soup hovering at mid-height, the Bunny at the center — and they look at the entering humans with the specific attention of animals that ran ahead and have been waiting.

The floor is wrong.

Not wrong like the altered rooms — wrong in a new way. Drug steps onto it and the surface gives beneath her weight. Not a lot — maybe a centimeter — but the give is unmistakable, the sensation of stepping onto something that is not solid in the way that floors are solid. It feels like dense foam. Like a mattress made of something heavier. Like the room is holding them in its palm and the palm is slightly soft.

"What is this?" NinjaFlout says, testing the surface with his foot, pressing down experimentally. The floor depresses, yields, accepts the weight with a cushioned response.

Galaxy stamps his foot. Hard.

The floor goes rigid. Instantly, completely — the soft surface becoming stone-hard under impact, the transition from give to resistance so fast that Galaxy's foot bounces. He stumbles slightly.

"Did you feel that?" Galaxy says.

"Non-Newtonian," Duccarian says immediately. He stamps his own foot — same result, the floor snapping to rigidity under force. He steps gently — soft again, yielding, the surface accepting his weight like something alive. "It responds to pressure. Gentle contact — it's soft. Impact — it solidifies."

"Like a liquid," Elv says. They step carefully, testing. Soft. They stamp. Hard. Soft again.

"Exactly like a liquid," Duccarian says. "One you can walk on."

Twenty-one people cross the soft floor toward the far wall, where a section is illuminated — the warm light concentrated into a strip that runs along the wall at chest height, and mounted on the wall within the lit section, arranged in neat rows, are tags. Small, numbered, team-colored. One for each person.

Below each tag: an item.

Three types. Glass bottles — clear, fragile, about thirty centimeters tall, the kind of bottle that communicates its breakability through every visual cue. Balloons — inflated, medium-sized, the kind that pulls against your grip with the specific buoyancy of trapped air. And books — thick, heavy, the dimensions of something that requires two hands to hold comfortably and one hand to hold with effort.

Each tag assigns a specific item to a specific person. The assignment is not random — or if it is, the randomness has produced distributions that feel deliberate.

Drug: glass bottle.
JIKOSU: book.
Rabbit: balloon.
Schwalbe: glass bottle.
Ninnginni: book.
Orphan Annihilator: balloon.
Akarimmu: glass bottle.
The Reddening Day: balloon.
Revenant: book.

And so on — the full twenty-one, each tagged, each assigned.

"Take your item," the figure says.

Twenty-one people take their items from the wall. The glass bottles are cold and smooth and exactly as fragile as they look. The balloons tug slightly upward in the grip. The books are heavy — not impossible, just heavy enough that holding one with one hand for a long time will be a negotiation with fatigue.

"Hold it," the figure says. "That is the rule. Hold your item. From this moment until the challenge ends, your item must be in your hands. Not tucked under an arm. Not stuffed in a pocket. Not pressed between your body and someone else's. Held. In your hands. Visibly."

Twenty-one people hold their items.

"If your item touches the ground, you lose. If your item breaks, you lose. Losing is permanent."

The word permanent sits in the room.

"You may stand on all fours. You may crouch, kneel, sit, lie flat. You may do whatever your body can do to maintain balance while holding your item. You cannot hold onto the walls—"

The walls move.

Not the entire wall — the lower sections, from floor to about two meters up, slide outward. Not far. Half a meter. Enough to reveal what is behind them: nothing. A gap, and beyond the gap, darkness that goes down and does not stop. The walls have pulled back to show the room's edges are not edges at all but the lip of something bottomless.

"—and you cannot hold onto another person or deliberately cause another person to lose their balance."

The room is now a fifty-meter circle of soft floor surrounded by a half-meter gap of nothing, the walls pulled back to create a perimeter of void. The floor is an island. The void is the sea. The items are the point.

"The floor will shake," the figure says. "Twenty-four levels. Each level shakes harder than the last. Every three levels, the interval between shakes decreases. Level one begins at ten-second intervals. By level twenty-four, the intervals will be very short and the force will be very high."

A pause.

"Good luck."

[[LEVEL 1-3]]
The first shake is gentle.

A tremor through the soft floor — not violent, not threatening, the kind of vibration that a building might produce if a heavy truck passed outside. The non-Newtonian surface responds: the soft floor ripples, small waves radiating from the center outward, the material behaving exactly like a liquid being disturbed. The ripples are visible — actual waves in the floor surface, moving under their feet.

Twenty-one people adjust. The adjustment is minimal — a slight widening of stance, a tightening of grip on whatever item they hold. The glass bottles clink softly in their holders' hands. The balloons bob. The books shift.

Ten seconds. The second shake. Slightly stronger. The ripples in the floor are larger — visible crests and troughs that move under the feet, the floor sloshing in slow motion.

Duccarian watches the floor with fascination. "The surface behaves like liquid when the shaking is gentle — it waves. But watch when it peaks—" The shake hits its maximum force and the floor goes rigid for an instant, the wave pattern freezing into a solid surface before softening again. "It solidifies at peak force. The harder it shakes, the more the floor switches between states."

"Useful or just interesting?" Schwalbe asks, holding his glass bottle with both hands.

"Both," Duccarian says.

Level 2. The shakes are noticeable now — not just tremors, actual movement, the floor displacing laterally by a few centimeters. The ripples are taller. The soft-to-hard transitions are faster. Feet sink slightly into the soft phase and then the floor locks and the sunken feet are held for an instant before the surface softens again.

Level 3. The ten-second interval is becoming a rhythm — shake, adjust, wait, shake, adjust, wait. The bodies are learning the pattern, the same way they learned the platform-drop timing on Day 6. The rhythm is survivable. The rhythm is almost comfortable.

Every three levels, the interval shortens.

[[LEVEL 4-6]]
Eight seconds between shakes now.

The difference is felt immediately — not in the force, which has increased gradually, but in the timing. The body had ten seconds to reset between events and now it has eight and the two missing seconds are the seconds that contained the breath, the adjustment, the moment of this is fine before the next one.

The floor is waving visibly with every shake — the non-Newtonian surface producing rolling swells that cross the room like water in a shaken bowl. Walking during the soft phase is like walking on a waterbed. Walking during the hard phase is like walking on concrete that was water a second ago and will be water again in another second.

Polaris drops to one knee during a level 5 shake — her ankle making the balance calculation that the rest of her body can't cover. She holds her book in both hands, pressed to her chest while kneeling, and the position is stable enough that she stays in it.

"Kneeling works," she calls. "Lower center of gravity."

Elv is already low — crouched on the balls of their feet, balloon gripped in one hand, the other hand on the floor for stability. The floor accepts the hand softly, the non-Newtonian surface yielding under gentle palm pressure. They ride the waves from this position like a surfer reading swells.

The Reddening Day is standing, holding their balloon with both hands, making the small constant adjustments that their body has learned from nine days of rounds — the habit of moving through spaces, of adjusting to terrain, of being present wherever they are. The balloon tugs upward with each shake, the buoyancy working against their grip, and they hold it.

Level 6. The floor is producing waves that are ankle-height — visible, rolling, the room looking increasingly like a pool of something that can't decide if it's liquid or solid. Feet impact the hard phase with jarring force. Feet sink into the soft phase with disorienting depth.

Rabbit holds his balloon close. The Bunny is somewhere in the room — it entered with them but is no longer visible in the chaos of twenty-one people managing items on an undulating floor. The Drake is at the room's edge, near the void gap, wings half-spread for balance. Soup is above — high enough that the shaking doesn't reach it, rotating slowly, watching.

[[LEVEL 7-12]]
Six-second intervals.

The force has doubled from level 1. The floor is a seascape — rolling waves that crest at knee height during the peak shakes, the non-Newtonian surface producing a disorienting cycle of soft-hard-soft that makes every step a negotiation. Step during the soft phase and your foot sinks. Step during the hard phase and your foot bounces. Time it wrong and the transition happens mid-step and the surface changes state under a planted foot and the body pitches.

NinjaFlout is holding his glass bottle in both hands, arms extended slightly, using the bottle's position as a counterbalance the way a tightrope walker uses a pole. He moves with small steps, never lifting his feet fully, sliding across the surface. "Shuffle. Don't step. The surface is more predictable if you don't lift."

"Noted," janhk says, shuffling beside him, his book heavy in both arms.

JIKOSU is low — one knee down, his book pressed between his forearms, the military practicality of someone who has identified the stable position and committed to it. He rides the waves from the knee, adjusting, his body a fixed point while the floor moves around him.

Level 9. Five-second intervals. The room shakes and the floor waves and the waves are waist-height now at peak and the non-Newtonian transitions are brutal — the surface going from liquid-soft to stone-hard in a fraction of a second, the impact of the hard phase sending shockwaves through every joint.

Akarimmu stumbles.

He is holding his glass bottle in one hand — his right — and the level 9 shake catches him mid-transfer between feet, the floor going hard under his left foot and soft under his right, and the height difference pitches him sideways. He catches himself. The bottle stays in his hand. His body hits the floor.

The floor goes hard on impact. His body bounces. The bottle stays in his hand because his hand has locked around it with the specific grip strength of someone who has just been told that dropping it is permanent.

"Still holding," he says, getting back to his knees.

Level 10. The halfway mark. The shaking is continuous now — five-second intervals meaning the floor has barely finished its response to one shake before the next arrives, the waves overlapping, the surface a chaos of competing crests and troughs. Walking is no longer advisable. Most people are on their knees or crouched or in whatever stable position they've found.

Drug is standing.

She is the only person still standing at level 10, holding her glass bottle in both hands at chest height, her feet planted shoulder-width apart, her body making the micro-adjustments that balance requires without any visible effort. The floor moves. Drug does not move. The floor goes soft. Drug's weight settles into it evenly, distributed, the same calm that held the altered rooms' geometry open now holding her vertical on a surface that is trying to throw everyone else down.

Level 11. Drug is still standing. The bottle is still in her hands. Her expression has not changed since level 1.

Level 12. Four-second intervals. The floor is a storm.

[[LEVEL 13-18]]
The waves are chest-height.

The non-Newtonian floor has become something that defies description — a surface that behaves like an ocean during the soft phase, producing rolling waves tall enough that people in the troughs can't see people on the crests, and then freezes mid-wave during the hard phase, locking the entire room into a frozen seascape of peaks and valleys before melting back to liquid chaos.

Three-second intervals by level 15. The shakes overlap completely. The floor never settles. The waves never resolve. The room is constant motion, constant state-change, the non-Newtonian cycle running so fast that the floor feels like it's vibrating between solid and liquid rather than transitioning.

Revenant loses his book at level 14.

He is on his knees, both hands on the book, holding it against his chest, and the level 14 shake produces a wave that crests directly under him and launches his knees upward while his torso stays put. The book slips. Not from his hands — from between his hands and his chest, the gap that the wave created exploiting the gap in his grip. The book slides. He grabs for it. His fingers catch the cover. The cover slips.

The book hits the floor.

The floor goes hard on the book's impact — the non-Newtonian response, the surface becoming stone under the force — and the book lands with a sound that is inappropriately small for what it means.

Revenant looks at the book on the floor. Looks up. The room continues shaking.

The floor beneath him shifts — the same mechanism, the same smooth descent. He goes down. The book goes with him. The surface closes over both.

The Floofs are seven minus one.

Level 16. Two-and-a-half-second intervals.

Akarimmu's bottle breaks at level 16.

He has been holding it — through every level, through every shake, through the fall at level 9 that should have ended him and didn't. He has been on his knees since level 10, the bottle between both palms, his body curved around it like a parenthesis around its content. The shaking at level 16 produces a wave that rises under his right side and drops under his left simultaneously — a rotational force, a twisting — and his body turns and the bottle's base contacts his knee and the knee is hard because the floor is in its hard phase and the glass and the hardened knee and the force meet at a single point.

The bottle cracks. Not shatters — cracks, a single line running from base to midpoint, and through the crack a small piece of glass separates and falls.

The piece of glass touches the floor.

Akarimmu looks at the crack. Looks at the small piece on the floor. The bottle is technically still in his hands. The piece that fell was the bottle's. The rule was if your item breaks.

The floor shifts beneath him.

He goes down without saying anything. The expression on his face is the specific expression of someone who did everything right and lost anyway, which is the facility's specialty.

The Syndicate is six.

[[LEVEL 19-24]]
Two-second intervals. Then shorter.

The room is a catastrophe of motion. The non-Newtonian floor has become something that changes state multiple times per second — soft-hard-soft-hard — the vibrations so fast and so intense that the surface is effectively both states at once, a material that is liquid and solid simultaneously, that yields and resists in the same instant. Walking is impossible. Standing is impossible. The people who remain are on all fours, on their backs, flat on their stomachs — whatever position distributes weight and minimizes height and keeps the item in their hands.

Drug is finally not standing. She went down at level 19 — not because she lost balance but because the waves crested above her head and standing meant being inside the wave rather than on top of it. She is on her knees now, bottle in both hands, the glass catching the room's light each time a wave lifts her above the surface level.

The Reddening Day loses their balloon at level 20.

They have held it for twenty levels — through gentle tremors and moderate waves and the chaos of the upper levels, the balloon's buoyancy working against them constantly, the inflated sphere trying to escape their grip with every shake. At level 20 the floor produces a wave that the hard phase freezes at peak height, and The Reddening Day is at the top of the frozen wave, two meters above the mean floor level, and the transition back to soft drops them and the drop is fast and their hands open on impact and the balloon goes up.

Not far. Just above their reach. Just beyond the tips of their fingers. The balloon floats — gentle, serene, completely disconnected from the violence below it — and The Reddening Day reaches for it and the floor shakes again and the reaching hand misses and the balloon drifts.

It touches the frozen crest of a wave. The surface is in its hard phase. The balloon rests on it for a fraction of a second.

It touched the ground.

The Reddening Day looks at the balloon on the floor. The same mechanism. The floor shifts. They go down.

The Wardens are six.

Level 22. The room is almost unsurvivable. The shaking is constant — no intervals, no gaps, the twenty-four-level escalation having compressed the spaces between events to the point where every moment is the event. The floor is a solid-liquid chaos that moves in every direction at once. The people who remain are holding on to nothing but their items and the floor beneath them and whatever margin exists between still here and gone.

Ninnginni is flat on his stomach, book clutched in both arms against the floor, his body serving as a weight on the surface. The non-Newtonian floor locks hard under his chest and he rides the frozen surface like a raft, and when it softens he sinks slightly and the softness holds him like a hand.

Galaxy is on all fours, glass bottle between his teeth — not in his hands, which are flat on the floor for balance, but clamped in his jaw, which is a hand, which is holding, which counts. The figure does not object.

Drug is on her knees. Still on her knees. The glass bottle in both hands. The surface rolling and freezing and melting and rolling around her and Drug's heartbeat is the thing in this room that is not shaking.

Level 23. Level 24.

The final shake is not like the others. It is everything the others were, concentrated. The floor moves in every direction at once, the non-Newtonian surface executing its full range — liquid to solid to liquid — in a cycle so fast it hums, and the hum is the same hum that woke them this morning, the vibration in the ground that started gentle and is ending here, in a room underground, on a floor that can't decide what it is.

The shaking stops.

The floor settles. Soft. Just soft. The waves flatten. The crests dissolve. The surface returns to its resting state — yielding, gentle, the palm-of-the-hand floor that greeted them when they entered.

Eighteen people lie on a soft floor holding glass bottles and balloons and books.

The figure's voice: "Challenge complete."

Nobody moves. The body does not trust that it's over. The body has been shaking for — nobody knows how long. Five minutes. Twenty. The time sense was destroyed somewhere around level 8 and has not recovered.

"You may release your items."

Eighteen people set down bottles and books and balloons on a floor that accepts them softly, with the gentle give of something that has been violent and is now done being violent.

The walls slide back into place, covering the void. The room is whole again. The floor is still.

Eighteen people. Three gone. Twenty-one minus Revenant, minus Akarimmu, minus The Reddening Day. Eighteen is what twenty-one becomes when three people's items touch the ground in a room that punishes touching with permanence.

[[RETURN]]
They walk back.

Through the short corridor, past the wall that Orphan Annihilator opened at the count of three, up the stairs, into the hub. The path is familiar in reverse. The floor-lights guide them in neutral white and the stairs are steady under feet that have forgotten what steady means.

The animals lead them home. The Drake at its distance. Soup at ceiling height. The Bunny three meters ahead of Drug, which is three meters ahead of Rabbit, the order having rearranged itself without anyone deciding it.

The Wardens walk with six. The gap where The Reddening Day walked — central, patient, the circuitry of their rounds woven through the formation — is the gap of a specific kind of absence. The Reddening Day's rounds. The Reddening Day's way of checking: all here. The person who said all here is not here, and the absence of the question makes the answer different.

"All here," Schwalbe says, in the corridor. Quietly. Filling the space that The Reddening Day filled.

"All here," Rally Mally says.

It means six. It used to mean more. It means six.

The Floofs walk with six. Revenant's absence leaves a gap that Elv moves into without being asked — the watchful presence filling the space that the watchful presence left, a different kind of watching in the same position. Ninnginni carries his book. He didn't set it down when the figure said he could. He carries it back through the corridor and up the stairs and into the hub, the heavy object held in both hands like something that cost too much to put down casually.

The Syndicate walks with six. Akarimmu's absence is quiet — he was quiet in the team, the tracker, the one who called geometry shifts in the altered rooms and was steady in formation. His glass broke. A single crack, a falling piece. The facility's precision — not the violence of a platform or the terror of a selection, just a crack.

Drug walks with her bottle intact. She doesn't set it down either.

They reach the dorms. The altered rooms — still wrong, still the dreamed geometry that maintenance produced — accept them back without complaint. The wrongness is almost comfortable now. The rooms that forgot their own rules have become rooms that have new rules, and the new rules include twenty-one people and three animals and seventeen items that were held through twenty-four levels of something that the floor did to them.

Eighteen people.

Someone counts. The count circulates — not announced, just passed from person to person, the way counts have moved since Day 1. Someone counts and the count is eighteen and the count is wrong.

Rally Mally counts again. Eighteen. Schwalbe counts. Eighteen. Drug counts. Eighteen.

"Eighteen," Rabbit says.

"That's not right," NinjaFlout says. "We went in with twenty-one, we lost three. That's eighteen."

"We went in with twenty-one," Rabbit repeats. "We lost three in the shaking room. We had twenty-one in the mirror room yesterday. We lost four in the mirror room. Twenty-five went into the mirror room. We were twenty-five at the start of Day 9."

"Twenty-one into the shaking room," NinjaFlout says. "Minus three. Eighteen."

The math is correct. The math has always been correct. The facility does math the way it does everything — precisely, without remainder.

Eighteen people in three sectors of six, in rooms that are still wrong, on a day that started with a vibration and ended with a different kind of vibration entirely.

Ninnginni sits in the Floofs' central room. Six people around him. Soup descends to face height.

"Six," Ninnginni says.

*Six,* Soup confirms.

He holds Loyal2Schwab's junction mark in one hand and the heavy book in the other — two objects from two challenges, one a memory and one a thing he refused to put down.

"What's next?" he asks.

*Rest,* Soup says. *Tomorrow is different.*

"Tomorrow is always different."
