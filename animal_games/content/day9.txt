[[DAY]]
DAY 9 — THE MIRROR ROOMS

[[SYSTEM_STATE]]
Day index: 9.
Participant count: 25.
Challenge type: psychological — individual isolation, identity confrontation.
Animal presence: outside rooms only.
Lethality: active. Elimination by early exit or extended exposure.

[[NARRATIVE]]
They wake at the same time.

Not approximately — exactly, the way a light switch works, the way a circuit completes. Twenty-five people in three sectors open their eyes at the same millisecond, and the synchronization is felt before it is understood. The body knows when something has been done to it.

The rooms are still altered from yesterday. The impossible geometry has settled overnight into something less aggressive but no less wrong — walls in their shifted positions, corridors curving where they shouldn't, sleeping platforms still on the ceiling in the Syndicate's sector. Nobody slept on the platforms. They slept on the floor, the way they've been sleeping since the rooms changed, the body's negotiation with a space that has forgotten its own rules.

But the rooms are quieter. The responsive quality — the listening — has dimmed. The geometry holds still. The space has stopped reading their fear, or has stopped caring, or has simply moved on to the next thing.

Drug wakes on the Syndicate's sector floor, in the spot she chose, which is the spot Mainthemainstar used to choose, which she did not choose consciously. She wakes and the spot is his and then it is hers and then it is just a spot on a floor and the layers of that sequence happen in the first three seconds of her day.

Rabbit wakes beside the Bunny, which has not moved from its position near Drug since yesterday. The Bunny's red eyes are open. They were open when Rabbit fell asleep. They are open now. The six-second-ahead distance is present in them, unchanged, the continuous forward surveillance of something that does not rest the way rest is usually understood.

In the Wardens' sector, space_fan wakes with the notebook beside her — not reading it, just keeping contact with it, the physical fact of its presence more important than its unreadable contents. She checks it. Still there. Still full of her handwriting. Still illegible.

Schwalbe is up before the rest. First to vertical, first to scan, first to determine whether conditions have changed. They have not. He files this and waits.

In the Floofs' sector, Ninnginni wakes with Loyal2Schwab's junction mark in his hands. He slept holding it. CharliSpirit is awake beside him, eyes open, staring at the ceiling with the expression of someone whose primary tool is broken and who is learning, slowly, to function without it.

"Morning," Ninnginni says.

"Morning," CharliSpirit says. She doesn't look at the ceiling the way she used to — measuring, mapping, building the spatial picture. She just looks at it. The ceiling is a ceiling. It doesn't need to be a coordinate.

Elv is awake in their corner, watching the sector entrance. They slept facing the door — the orientation of someone who wanted to see anything coming before it arrived.

The hub is quiet when they emerge. Twenty-five people filing in from three corridors, the morning silence of a group that has learned to read the facility's moods by the quality of its quiet. Today's quiet is different from yesterday's — less ominous, less charged. Not safe. Just neutral. The facility in a resting state between things it intends to do.

And on the tables:

Food.

[[MORNING FEAST]]
The tables have risen.

After two days of nothing — the Easter eggs earned by competition, then the empty morning of Day 8 — the tables have risen with food on them and the food is good. Not the extraordinary calibrated nostalgia of Day 4, not the wide international generosity of Day 6's shared breakfast. Good. Simply good. The kind of food that is excellent because you haven't eaten properly in two days and your body has been running on adrenaline and fear and the memory of flatbread.

The plates have numbers again.

Small numbered tags, team-colored — violet for Wardens, silver for Floofs, red for Syndicate — each plate assigned to a specific person, the individual markers that were absent on the shared day returned now with the quiet firmness of a system reasserting its categories. You are this person. You belong to this team. Here is your food.

Nobody objects. Today the structure is back and the structure comes with eggs and bread and something warm and the twenty-five people who are alive sit down in their team clusters and eat with the specific concentration of bodies that have been asked for a great deal and are being given something back.

Rally Mally eats with the focus of someone who has thought about food for two days and is now addressing the thought directly. Orphan Annihilator eats beside him, slower, his ankle propped on the bench edge, the injury from Day 6 a permanent background presence — not worsening, not healing, just there.

Drug eats the way she does everything — with attention, without performance. The Bunny is beside her, which is where the Bunny has been since yesterday, which is new, which nobody in the Syndicate mentions because mentioning it would require acknowledging why the Bunny moved.

The animals are present at the edges of the team clusters. Not eating — but present, in the specific positions of animals that have something to communicate and are waiting for the right moment.

The right moment is when the food is half-eaten and the hunger is half-addressed and the brain has enough fuel to process information but not enough comfort to ignore it.

Soup descends.

Not to Ninnginni — to the center of the Floofs' table, hovering at face height, luminescence shifting to something brighter, more deliberate, the light of something that is about to speak and wants everyone to hear.

*Listen carefully,* Soup says. And because Soup speaks to the whole team — not through Ninnginni, directly, the only animal whose communication reaches every member — all eight Floofs hear it simultaneously.

*The next game is a trap. A very specific trap. It will feel safe and it will feel personal and it will ask you to stay. Do not stay longer than you need to. Do not stay shorter than you're told. The window is exact. Be careful.*

The Floofs hear this. Ninnginni sets down his food. CharliSpirit sets down hers. Eight people receiving a warning from an entity that has been correct about everything it has ever said.

"How do we know the window?" Revenant asks.

*You won't,* Soup says. *That's the trap.*

At the Syndicate's table, the Bunny communicates to Rabbit. Rabbit is mid-bite — bread halfway to his mouth — and the Bunny's message arrives with the urgency that the Bunny reserves for things that cannot wait.

*The next challenge is dangerous in a way the others weren't. It targets you individually. It knows you. Tell the team: stay exactly as long as they're told. Not less. Not more. The margins are lethal.*

Rabbit puts down the bread. Opens his mouth to relay this. What comes out is: "Mmhf — the Bunny says — next game—" He swallows. "Targets us individually. Stay exactly as long as they say. Not less, not more."

"What?" janhk says.

"Stay exactly as long as they tell us. The margins—"

"You have bread in your mouth," NinjaFlout says.

"The margins are lethal," Rabbit says, clearer now, but the moment is past — the table has the quality of information half-received, the warning arriving through a mouthful of food, the seriousness diluted by the comedy of the delivery.

"Lethal margins, got it," Akarimmu says, and goes back to eating.

Rabbit looks at Drug. Drug heard. Drug always hears. She gives him the smallest nod — received, filed, stored — and continues eating, and the warning sits beside her plate like a second serving she hasn't touched yet.

At the Wardens' table, the Drake communicates to space_fan. She's eating with one hand, notebook under the other, the dual attention of someone never fully in one activity. The Drake's message arrives electric, precise, compressed:

*What comes next will try to keep you. The room will know you. Don't let it keep you longer than the time they give.*

Space_fan sets down her fork. Turns to the table. "The Drake shays — shorry —" She swallows. "The Drake says the next challenge will try to keep us in a room. We get a set time. Don't stay longer."

"Don't stay longer than what?" CrystalWolf asks.

"Than the time we're given."

"What happens if we do?"

Space_fan looks at the Drake. The Drake's electric veins pulse once.

*It takes you.*

"It takes you," space_fan relays.

Schwalbe nods. Filed. The others are eating and the warning sits at the table with them like an extra plate nobody wants to pick up, and the morning continues around it, and the food is good enough that the warning doesn't ruin the food but present enough that the food doesn't erase the warning.

The meal ends. The tables descend. Twenty-five people sit in the hub with full stomachs and animal warnings of varying clarity and the specific feeling of a morning that has been kind and is about to stop.

[[THE DESCENT]]
The animals move first.

Not toward the challenge corridors or the ramp or any of the paths the facility has used before. The three animals move simultaneously toward a section of the hub wall that has never opened, and as they approach, it opens. A doorway. Behind it: stairs. Going down.

The floor-lights are neutral white. The stairs are wide — three abreast — descending at a steady angle steeper than the Day 6 ramp but less disorienting than the Day 7 corridor. Just down. Directly, purposefully, into the facility's lower architecture.

"We've never gone down," Polaris says. Every previous challenge has been lateral or upward. Down is new. Down carries a different weight.

"The altered rooms were partially below us," Duccarian says. "The Syndicate's sector had downward stairs. The geometry implied a sub-level."

"This is deeper than that," Rabbit says. The Bunny is ahead of him, moving with purpose, and Rabbit reads its posture — ears forward, pace measured, the body language of something that knows where it's going and what's there.

The stairwell ends at a corridor. Short — twenty meters. It opens into a room.

The room is circular. Large enough for twenty-five people to stand without touching. The ceiling is domed. The lighting is warm — not gold, not facility white, something between them, the light of a space designed to feel neutral.

And around the perimeter, evenly spaced: twenty-five doors.

Each door is identical — same size, same material, same handle. But each one has a name tag. Small, printed, precise. Readable from the center if you look carefully. Twenty-five names. Twenty-five doors.

Drug finds hers. Her name on a door in a room underground. The door is closed. Through it she can hear nothing, see nothing. It is simply a door with her name on it.

"Twenty-five doors. Twenty-five names. One each," NinjaFlout says, completing a circuit of the room.

"No team grouping," Schwalbe notes. The doors are interspersed — Wardens and Floofs and Syndicate names mixed around the perimeter, team structure ignored.

The figure's voice:

"Stand in front of your door."

Twenty-five people find their doors. Twenty-five people stand facing twenty-five closed doors, arranged in a circle, backs to the center.

"Open them."

Twenty-five doors open onto twenty-five rooms. Small — three meters square, windowless, the same warm neutral light. Each room contains a single chair. Nothing else.

"Step inside. Sit down. Wait for further notice."

Twenty-five people look at each other. The last look before separation — the first time since Day 1 that they will be entirely alone. Not teams, not pairs, not formations. Individuals. Some looks are brief. Some are not.

Drug looks at Rabbit. Rabbit looks at Drug. The Bunny is at Rabbit's feet, outside his door, and it communicates one last thing:

*Thirty minutes. Count if you can.*

Rabbit nods. Steps inside. Sits down.

Twenty-five people enter twenty-five rooms. Twenty-five doors close.

Not even a second passes.

The figure's voice, in every room simultaneously:

"You will stay in this room for thirty minutes."

[[THE RULES]]
"The room will present you with a version of yourself. Not a reflection — something that moves independently, that knows things about you, that will try to convince you to stay."

A pause. The warm-controlled tone. The weight underneath it.

"Your task is simple: remain for exactly thirty minutes, then leave. There is no clock. There is no alarm. You will need to feel the time."

Another pause.

"Leave before thirty minutes and you are eliminated. Stay longer than thirty minutes and the version of you will begin to take over — slowly at first, then faster, the boundary between you and it dissolving. The longer you stay past the threshold, the harder it becomes to leave. Stay long enough and you won't be able to tell which one of you is real."

The silence after this has the quality of a room that has said what it needed to say and is now watching what the saying does.

"Your thirty minutes begin now."

[[THE MIRRORS]]
Twenty-five rooms. Twenty-five chairs. Twenty-five people sitting alone for the first time in nine days.

The first minute is just silence. The rooms are featureless — walls, floor, ceiling, chair, person. Nothing happens. The body adjusts to solitude the way it adjusts to water temperature: first the shock of it, then the accommodation, then the forgetting that it was ever different.

Then the rooms change.

Not all at once. Not identically. Each room shifts in a way that is specific to its occupant, the space responding to the individual the way the altered sectors responded to collective fear — but more precisely, more intimately, the calibration of a system that knows each of the twenty-five people in a way that goes beyond observation into something closer to comprehension.

[[DUCCARIAN'S ROOM]]
The version of Duccarian appears sitting in a second chair that wasn't there a moment ago.

It looks like him. Exactly like him — the same build, the same face, the same way of holding the head at a slight angle when considering something. But the eyes are different. Not in color or shape. In depth. The version's eyes have the quality of someone who has been thinking for longer than Duccarian has been alive.

"You've been thinking about whether this place is real," the version says.

Duccarian looks at it. At himself. At the thing that is wearing his face and speaking with his voice and sitting in a chair in a room underground with the posture of someone who has been waiting for this conversation for a very long time.

"I've been thinking about whether the question of its reality is the right question," Duccarian says.

"It isn't," the version says. "The right question is whether your thinking about it changes anything. Whether the philosophy you carry — the frameworks, the analysis, the habit of asking why before asking how — whether any of that matters here."

"It matters to me."

"Does it? Or does it just keep you occupied while the facility does what it does regardless of your understanding of it?"

This is precise. This is the exact question that Duccarian has been avoiding since Day 1 — not whether the facility can be understood, but whether understanding it matters. The version is going directly to the thing he hasn't said out loud, the doubt that lives underneath the analysis.

"Understanding is its own value," Duccarian says.

"Understanding is a delay tactic," the version says. "You understand the geometry of the altered rooms. You understand the fear mechanic. You understand the system's selection process as well as anyone can. And what has that understanding produced? The same outcomes as ignorance. The same losses. The same progression."

"The same survival," Duccarian says.

The version smiles. It is Duccarian's smile, which is rare and specific and arrives only when he is genuinely engaged. Seeing it on another face is the most disorienting thing that has happened in this room.

"Stay," the version says. "We could talk about this properly. For as long as we want. Nobody interrupting. Nobody needing you to be tactical or useful or on-mission. Just the thinking."

Duccarian feels the pull of this. It is real. It is the specific temptation of someone who has been subordinating his primary mode of engagement — philosophy, inquiry, the love of the question itself — to the practical demands of survival. The version is offering him the thing he hasn't had since he arrived: an intellectual peer, an infinite conversation, the pure engagement of mind with mind.

He counts. He has been counting since the figure said now. He doesn't know if the count is accurate. He doesn't know if thirty minutes feels like thirty minutes in a room designed to distort your sense of time.

He counts anyway.

[[DRUG'S ROOM]]
The version of Drug does not appear.

Mainthemainstar does.

He is sitting in the second chair, in the posture she has memorized without trying — the slight lean, the soft-focus attention, the way his hands rest on his knees when he is present in a room without needing to do anything in it. He looks like himself. He looks alive.

Drug doesn't move.

"Hey," Mainthemainstar says.

His voice is correct. Not approximately — exactly, the specific timbre and pace and quiet that she heard every day for eight days and then stopped hearing because the floor opened and took him down. It is his voice and it is in this room and it is saying hey the way he said hey, and Drug sits in her chair and the distance between her body and the thing in the other chair is three meters and three meters is the closest she has been to Mainthemainstar since yesterday and Mainthemainstar is dead.

"You're not him," Drug says.

"I know things he knew," the version says. "I know you sat in the spot he slept in. I know you didn't choose it consciously. I know the Bunny moved to you and you put your hand on its back and it was the first time anyone other than Rabbit touched it."

"The system knows those things. That doesn't make you him."

"Does it matter? If I know what he knew and I sound like he sounded and I'm here — does the distinction matter?"

"Yes," Drug says.

"Why?"

Drug is quiet for a moment. The version waits the way Mainthemainstar waited — without filling the silence, without redirecting, just present in the room because the room needed someone in it.

"Because he said he wasn't going anywhere," Drug says. "And then he went somewhere. And you're not somewhere. You're a room."

The version looks at her with Mainthemainstar's soft-focus attention, covering everything, fixing on nothing. "Stay," it says. "I'll be here. As long as you stay, I'll be here."

Drug counts. She has been counting since the figure said now. The count is steady. Her heartbeat is steady. Her breathing is steady. She is the calmest person in twenty-five rooms because she has decided to be, and the decision is not different from any other decision she has made in this facility — the choice to observe, to name, to remain level when the ground is not.

The version of Mainthemainstar sits in the chair and waits and Drug counts and does not stay.

[[ASHBORN'S ROOM]]
The version of Ashborn appears and Ashborn's body responds before his mind does.

His ankle — the Day 6 injury, the thing that held but held loudly, the background presence that hasn't been background since Day 8 — sends a sharp signal. Not because the ankle has worsened. Because the version of Ashborn standing in front of him has no ankle injury. The version is whole. Uninjured. Standing with the balanced weight of someone whose body is complete.

"You're hurt," the version says. "You've been hurt since Day 6 and you've been pretending it's manageable."

"It is manageable."

"For how much longer? The challenges are getting harder. The physical demands are increasing. How many more days can you manage before managing isn't enough?"

This is the fear. Not the room, not the version, not the facility's trick — the fear that Ashborn has been carrying since the platforms, the one he's managed with Rally Mally's help and The Reddening Day's proximity and the Wardens' formation that always leaves space for him to brace. The fear that he is becoming a liability.

The version sits down. "You could stay. There's a chair. It's comfortable. Nothing here will ask your ankle to do anything."

Ashborn's count is shaky. He started counting but the pain spiked and the count stuttered and he's not sure if he's at minute six or minute eight and the version is right — the chair is comfortable and nothing in this room is asking him to move.

He tries to restart the count. He can't find where he was.

At what he believes is minute twenty-two — but might be minute eighteen, or might be minute twenty-six — he stands. His ankle protests. He walks to the door. He opens it.

The circular room is empty. No one else has come out yet. Or everyone else has come out and left. He doesn't know. He stands in the doorway and he doesn't know if he was early or late and the not-knowing is its own kind of fear, different from the ankle, older than the facility.

He was early. He doesn't know this yet.

[[CRYSTALWOLF'S ROOM]]
The version of CrystalWolf presents her with a pattern.

Not a verbal argument, not a philosophical debate — a pattern. On the walls of the room, appearing gradually, a dataset: every event since Day 1, every challenge outcome, every death, every team score, every system selection, laid out in a visual structure that CrystalWolf's pattern-recognition instinct latches onto the way a hand latches onto a railing.

"You see it," the version says.

CrystalWolf sees it. She sees something in the data — a shape, a logic, the underlying structure of the facility's decisions, the algorithm that selects who dies and who survives. She has been looking for this since Day 1. She has been noting patterns, filing observations, building the dataset in her head. And now it's on the walls, complete, organized, waiting to be read.

"Stay," the version says. "You're close. Another few minutes and you'll have it."

CrystalWolf counts. She has been counting. She is at minute twenty-eight. She knows this with reasonable confidence because CrystalWolf's internal clock is good — not perfect, good, the clock of someone who notices patterns in time the way she notices patterns in everything.

Minute twenty-nine. The pattern on the wall is almost resolved. The shape is almost legible. One more minute and she'll have the answer.

Minute thirty. She should leave. The count says thirty. The door is there.

The pattern is almost complete.

One more minute.

She stays.

The pattern adds another layer. The walls shift. New data appears — data she hasn't seen, data about what comes next, data about Days 10 through 15, the facility's complete plan visible for the first time. She just needs another minute.

She stays.

The version of CrystalWolf smiles. It is CrystalWolf's smile, the one that appears when a pattern resolves, when the data assembles into sense. The version smiles and the room gets warmer and the chair gets more comfortable and the pattern on the wall keeps unfolding, always almost complete, always one more minute from resolution.

CrystalWolf stays.

[[HOLLOW CAMILLIA'S ROOM]]
The version of hollow Camillia tells her she's not finished.

"You haven't done enough," it says. It looks like her — jaw set, eyes steady, the posture of someone who does not back down from things. "The Syndicate lost JIKOSU today. The Syndicate lost Mainthemainstar yesterday. The Syndicate lost Kiwi on Day 5. The team is shrinking and you haven't done enough to stop it."

"I can't stop the system's selections," hollow Camillia says.

"You could if you understood them. Stay. Think about it. There's a logic to who gets selected and you haven't found it because you haven't had time to think without interruption."

Hollow Camillia's count is good. She has been counting steadily, the discipline of someone who was told to count and is counting. She is at minute twenty-six.

"You always leave too early," the version says. "Not from rooms. From conversations. From plans. From the moment when staying a little longer would give you the thing you need. You leave because leaving feels like strength and staying feels like weakness and you have confused the two since before you arrived here."

This lands. It lands because it's true — not facility-true, not system-generated accuracy, true in the way that a thing you've always known about yourself is true when someone finally says it out loud. Hollow Camillia has always left. Has always been the person who moves first, decides fastest, exits before the exit becomes complicated.

Minute thirty. She should leave.

"If you leave now, you leave the same person who entered," the version says. "If you stay, you might leave different. Better. Finished."

Hollow Camillia stays.

She doesn't mean to. The count says thirty and her hand doesn't reach for the door and the not-reaching is not a decision, it's an absence of a decision, it's the specific paralysis of someone whose pattern has been named and who is sitting in the naming of it.

She stays.

[[LUMALLIE'S ROOM]]
The version of lumallie is her own voice telling her to run.

Not a separate figure — a sound, filling the room, lumallie's voice speaking to lumallie in the specific tone that lumallie uses inside her own head when something is wrong: *Move. Now. Don't wait. The room is closing and the time is wrong and you need to go.*

lumallie's instinct has always been speed. She is the one who covers what CharliSpirit moves through too fast. She is the complementary system, the person who fills gaps at pace, the team member whose contribution is velocity applied to detail. Her body's default is motion. Her mind's default is now.

The voice says: *The count is at thirty. You've been counting. It's thirty. Go.*

lumallie stands. She has been counting. The count feels like thirty. The voice — her voice, her instinct, the thing that has kept her alive and useful for nine days — says thirty.

She opens the door. Steps out.

The circular room is empty except for Ashborn, standing at his door with the expression of someone who doesn't know if he's early or late.

lumallie is early. She knows this immediately — not from a clock, not from the figure's voice, from the quality of the room's silence, which has the texture of a space that is still waiting for something that hasn't happened yet. She was not at thirty. She was at twenty-three. Her instinct lied to her. The voice that said move was not the time telling her to go — it was the room telling her to fail.

She stands in the circular room and the door behind her closes and does not reopen.

[[THE OTHERS]]
Twenty-one rooms hold twenty-one people who stay and leave.

Some leave easily. Rabbit's count is precise — the Bunny said thirty minutes and Rabbit has been translating the Bunny's communications for nine days and the translation has trained him to attend to exact quantities. At minute thirty he stands, walks to the door, opens it. His version — a Rabbit without the Bunny, a Rabbit who chose differently on Day 1 — says stay and Rabbit says no and that is the end of it.

NinjaFlout leaves at what he believes is minute thirty-one, deliberately — the extra minute a margin of safety, the tactical decision to risk one minute of the version's influence rather than risk leaving one minute early. His version showed him the view from the highest platform on Day 6, the perspective he had from elevation, and offered him permanent height. He declined.

Ninnginni's version showed him a room where all ten original Floofs were present — Loyal2Schwab at the wall making marks, Golden at the edge being quietly present. The version said: *Stay and they stay.* Ninnginni counted to thirty and said "They wouldn't want me to stay" and left, and the room with ten Floofs in it became a room with zero.

Elv's version was silent. It sat in the chair and looked at them and said nothing, and the nothing was the hardest thing because Elv's version understood that Elv's specific fear is not being seen, and a version that refuses to speak is a version that refuses to see. Elv counted, carefully, and left at thirty with the precision of someone who was not going to give the room the satisfaction.

Polaris left at twenty-nine and a half, almost early, her ankle making the count unreliable, the pain a distortion in her time-sense that she corrected for imperfectly. She made it. Barely.

Duccarian left at thirty. His version offered him the infinite conversation and he wanted it — genuinely, fully, with the specific desire of someone whose primary mode of engagement has been starved for nine days — and he left anyway, because understanding is not worth anything if you're not alive to apply it.

Drug left at thirty. Her version of Mainthemainstar said stay and she said no and the no was the hardest word she has said in the facility, harder than twenty-seven, harder than his name, harder than anything, because the version was right that as long as she stayed he would be there, and she left anyway, and he wasn't.

Space_fan left at thirty with the notebook in her hand. Her version showed her the notebook's contents — readable, finally, the words resolving into meaning. She closed the notebook without reading it and walked out. Some things you wait for. Some things wait for you. This was the second kind.

Schwalbe left at twenty-nine and fifty seconds. Early by ten seconds but within margin. His version was direct and tactical and offered him command of the full group — not the Wardens, everyone — and Schwalbe said "I already have a team" and left.

One by one, twenty-one doors open. Twenty-one people step into the circular room. They look at each other and count heads and the count is twenty-one and the count should be twenty-five.

Four doors remain closed.

[[THE COUNT]]
The figure's voice arrives in the circular room.

"Twenty-one present."

Twenty-one people look at the four closed doors. Four names on four doors. Ashborn. CrystalWolf. lumallie. hollow Camillia.

"Ashborn and lumallie exited before thirty minutes. They have been eliminated."

The two doors — Ashborn's and lumallie's — open. The rooms behind them are empty. Not just unoccupied — empty in the way that a room is empty when the person who was in it has been removed rather than having left. The chairs are there. The people are not.

"CrystalWolf and hollow Camillia remained past thirty minutes. They have been eliminated."

The two remaining doors do not open.

Rally Mally looks at Ashborn's empty room. Ashborn who helped him, who he helped, who carried the ankle injury through every challenge, who panicked in a room where the version of himself was whole and uninjured and the panic broke his count and the broken count broke the margin.

Schwalbe looks at CrystalWolf's closed door. CrystalWolf who found patterns in everything, who identified the egg grid layout on Day 7, who noticed the spacing and the structure and the logic. The room gave her the biggest pattern of all and she stayed to see it and the staying was the trap and the pattern was the bait.

Drug looks at hollow Camillia's closed door. Hollow Camillia who pulled herself to the highest platform on Day 6, who set her jaw the way jaws set when setting them is the alternative to something worse. The room told her she always leaves too early and she believed it and she stayed.

Ninnginni looks at lumallie's empty room. lumallie who covered what CharliSpirit moved through too fast, who was the complementary system, the one who filled gaps at pace. The room used her speed against her — told her to go when going was the wrong thing, used her instinct as the weapon.

"Twenty-one remain," the figure says.

The circular room holds this.

"Teams," NinjaFlout says. His voice is flat. He is running the arithmetic. "Wardens lost two. We lost one. Floofs lost one."

The math settles in the room.

Wardens: nine minus two. Seven.

Floofs: eight minus one. Seven.

Syndicate: eight minus one. Seven.

"Seven each," Rabbit says.

The room is quiet.

"Seven each," Duccarian says, and his voice carries the specific tone of someone who is looking at a pattern and seeing it clearly for the first time. "The system equalized the teams."

Twenty-one people stand in a circular room underground, in front of twenty-one open doors and four that are closed or empty, and the number that matters is not twenty-one but seven — seven and seven and seven, three teams made equal by a challenge that killed by precision, that targeted the exact people in the exact numbers needed to produce symmetry.

The facility doesn't do anything by accident.

"The system equalized the teams," Duccarian says again, quieter. "That means what comes next requires equal teams."

Nobody asks what comes next. The answer is always the same: something worse. Something designed. Something that the facility has already built and is already waiting in and is already watching them process the loss of four more people in a room that smells like nothing and is lit like nowhere and has twenty-five doors, twenty-one of which opened and four of which will never open again.

[[RETURN]]
The stairs go up the same way they went down — steady, purposeful, the neutral white floor-lights marking the path back to the level they've lived on for nine days.

The animals are at the top of the stairs. They waited. The Drake at its distance. Soup at the ceiling. The Bunny three meters from whatever boundary it has set for itself today, red eyes tracking the group as they emerge.

The Bunny counts. Twenty-one. Its ears settle into the position that does not mean complete.

The Wardens walk with seven. The gap where Ashborn walked — beside Rally Mally, near The Reddening Day — is present the way all gaps are present. CrystalWolf's absence is different — she was taken by curiosity, by the thing that made her most useful, the facility weaponizing her strength. Ashborn was taken by fear. CrystalWolf was taken by wanting to know.

The Floofs walk with seven. lumallie's absence restructures the team's movement — the complementary system broken, CharliSpirit without the person who covered her speed, the dual mechanism now singular. CharliSpirit walks and does not map and does not have lumallie behind her and the not-having is a new kind of loss, different from Loyal2Schwab and Golden, more functional, more immediate.

The Syndicate walks with seven. Hollow Camillia's absence makes the team quieter — she was the one who said things directly, who named the situation, who set her jaw and spoke. Without her the Syndicate has Rabbit's translation and Drug's observation and NinjaFlout's arithmetic and janhk's touch and Lyrix's presence and Akarimmu's tracking and the specific quality of a team that has lost its bluntness.

They reach the hub. The dorms are as they left them — still altered, still wrong, still the rooms that dreamed themselves into different shapes. But the wrongness is familiar now, the way a scar is familiar, and twenty-one people enter twenty-one sleeping spaces in rooms that are not the rooms they started with and are, somehow, still home.

Ninnginni sits in the Floofs' central room. Soup descends to face height.

"You warned us," Ninnginni says.

*I did.*

"lumallie didn't make it."

*No.*

"Could you have warned her specifically?"

Soup is quiet. The luminescence holds steady — not brightening, not dimming. The pause of something that has an answer and is deciding how much of it to give.

*I warned everyone equally,* Soup says. *The trap is individual. The warning can only be general. What each person does with the warning is theirs.*

Ninnginni holds Loyal2Schwab's junction mark. Two dead teammates' absences and one eliminated teammate's absence and the difference between dead and eliminated is a distinction the facility has not clarified and Ninnginni has not asked about because asking might produce an answer he doesn't want.

Seven Floofs. Seven Wardens. Seven Syndicate members.

Equal.

He closes his eyes.
