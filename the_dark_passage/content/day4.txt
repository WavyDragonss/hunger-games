[[NARRATIVE]]

[[SECTION]] THE MEOWING

The meowing woke her.

Not a cry, not a growl — a meow. Clean and ordinary and wrong, because there were no cats here anymore and the dorm was sealed and thirty-four — twenty-eight — twenty-eight people were sleeping around her in the dark and there should not have been a sound like that.

space_fan sat up.

The dorm was dark and still. Everyone else was asleep. And somewhere in the dark, past the end of the bed rows, something moved. A shape, low to the ground, and a tail — grey-tipped, familiar in a way she had no language for, the way a word in a forgotten language is familiar, meaning present before definition — disappearing around a corner that had not been there when she went to sleep.

She was on her feet before she thought about it. She was following before she chose to.

The corridor the corner opened onto was not any corridor she recognised. Red-tinted stone, narrow, and ahead of her the tail turned again, unhurried, and she heard herself say something that came out before she could catch it — a name, or what tried to be a name, two syllables that her tongue reached for and fumbled, like a word in a language she used to speak and had lost. It meant something. She could feel that it meant something. It came out as sound without shape and she said it again anyway, louder, running now, because the tail was turning another corner and she was not going to lose it, she could not lose it, she had lost it once before and she did not know when but she knew it was true —

The lights went red.

All of them, at once, the corridor walls flushing deep red, and then a sound that was not the voice they knew — mechanical, flat, buzzing at the edges:

*UNAUTHORISED ACCESS DETECTED.*

She stopped.

*ELIMINATING INTRUDER.*

The ceiling moved. She looked up at it the way you look at something you cannot believe is happening, and it came down steadily, without hurry, the way everything in this place was without hurry, and she pressed herself against the wall and opened her mouth and what came out was the name again — clearer this time, almost right, almost the actual word — and the ceiling came down and the walls pressed in and she heard nothing after that.

She woke up screaming.

[[SECTION]] MORNING

Twenty-eight faces around her bed.

Polaris was the closest, crouched at her side, one hand on her shoulder — not gripping, just there. "You're here," Polaris said. "You're in the dorm. Look at me."

space_fan looked at her. The dorm. The warm, even air, unchanged from the moment they'd walked in — the one constant in this whole place. Her own breathing, ragged, no mist to it at all.

"There," said Polaris.

"I—" space_fan looked around at the circle of people. CrystalWolf with both hands over her mouth. WavyDragons frozen mid-crouch. Schwalbe standing back, not crowding, watching. "I'm fine," space_fan said. "Sorry. It was—"

"You were running," said Polaris. "In your sleep. You kept — you were crying and running in place and you were shouting." She paused. "A name. A flower name."

space_fan felt the colour rise in her face. The name was gone now — she'd had it, had been calling it down a red corridor, and now it sat in the place where things go when you wake too fast. A flower. That was all that was left of it. A flower she could not name, a tail she could not follow, a word she could not say properly even in her own dream.

"It was a nightmare," she said. "It was nothing. Go eat."

She said it more sharply than she intended, and people went.

The floor at the centre of the dorm groaned, and a table rose out of it — stone, seamless, pushing up out of the ground the way the parkour course had pushed out of the walls, and on it, already steaming in the warm still air, was food. Real warmth this time, which was the first surprise — the smell of it reached her before she could see it, rising off the table in visible curls, and she stood and breathed it in for a moment like something given back. The second surprise was the shape: each portion was moulded into the form of a small cat, sitting upright, round-headed, tail curled. She stood in front of hers and looked at it and said nothing.

Around her, everyone else was laughing.

"It's a cat," said Galaxy.

"It's shaped like a cat," said Toad.

"It's the same cats from yesterday," said Retard 2.0. "Recycled."

"Don't say that," said lumallie, who was already eating hers with visible affection.

"Do the cats know they're food-shaped cats," said NinjaFlout.

"Philosophically," said Elv, "we're all food-shaped."

"That means nothing," said Duccarian.

"Exactly," said Elv.

space_fan ate her portion and did not comment on the shape and thought about a grey-tipped tail disappearing around a corner, and thought about how the warmth of the food sat in her chest like the memory of something she was not supposed to have.

[[SECTION]] BONJOUR

The voice came while they were still eating.

*"Bonjour."*

The French accent, smooth and settled in the warm still air of the dorm. By now they all knew it — the second they heard that first syllable the laughter stopped and the food went untouched and twenty-nine people sat with the specific quality of attention you give to something that has your life.

*"I hope you all enjoyed the food."*

As he spoke the table began to — not fall, not break — dissolve was not quite right either. It was more like the table remembered what it was made of, and the parts reorganised themselves, sinking back into the floor grain by grain, atom by atom, the food going with it. All of it, everywhere, returning to stone in a slow tide that moved while he talked, so that by the time he was done there was no trace it, or the table, had ever been there.

*"We are now going up one more stage. Hoodies are waiting for you at the exit."*

A pause.

*"I suggest you take one."*

The exit door was open.

[[SECTION]] THE NUMBERS

The hoodies were piled at the exit — twenty-nine of them, thick dark fabric, identical except for a white number stitched on the back of each. They were warm in the hand in a way the food had been warm: genuinely, not residually. space_fan pulled hers over her head and the warmth settled on her like a hand on the shoulder and she pulled the hood up without thinking and stood there for a second with her eyes closed.

Schwalbe had his on already. He'd gone through the pile first, not to claim a particular one but to look at the numbers, get the range. Then he'd turned to the group.

"I'll call them," he said, and nobody argued, because nobody else would have thought to.

He went through the pile one by one, holding each up and reading the number on the back:

"One — space_fan. Two — CrystalWolf. Three — that's mine." He kept it. "Four — Duccarian. Five — Ashborn. Six — Rally Mally." He handed it over with a look at the ankle, which Rally Mally met with a small nod. "Seven — The Reddening Day. Eight — Toad. Nine — Retard 2.0. Ten — CharliSpirit. Eleven — lumallie. Twelve — Galaxy. Thirteen — Polaris. Fourteen — Revenant. Fifteen — Elv. Sixteen — Golden. Seventeen — Rabbit. Eighteen — Drug. Nineteen — janhk. Twenty — Kiwi. Twenty-one — Akarimmu. Twenty-two — JIKOSU." He held it out and JIKOSU took it without looking at the number. "Twenty-three — Lyrix. Twenty-four — NinjaFlout. Twenty-five — Mainthemainstar. Twenty-six — duck179. Twenty-seven — Nightmare. Twenty-eight — SomeDucks. Twenty-nine — WavyDragons."

Twenty-nine numbers. Twenty-nine people. 

"Everyone has one?" Schwalbe asked.

Nobody said otherwise.

They filed through the exit.

[[SECTION]] THE ICE

The cold hit them in the doorway. Not the ambient chill of the dorms or the working cold of the parkour chamber — this was cold with intention, cold that had been here a long time, cold that lived in the walls and the floor and the air and was not going anywhere. space_fan's first breath in the new chamber felt like drinking ice water from the inside. She pulled the hoodie tighter. Around her she heard the sound of twenty-eight people doing the same.

The chamber was large and low-ceilinged, and in the centre of it was the ice.

A full sheet, rectangular, pale blue-white, its surface marked with concentric rings of red and blue at the far end — painted, or frozen into the surface — and at the near end, in a rack mounted to the wall, sat a row of heavy rounded stones with handles. Beside them, a collection of long-handled brooms.

Nobody knew what they were looking at. Not immediately.

"That's a sport," said Mainthemainstar, slowly.

"How do you know?" said SomeDucks.

"I don't," said Mainthemainstar. "It just looks like one."

The voice confirmed it.

*"What you are looking at,"* Ninnginni said, from everywhere, *"is a curling sheet. The stones are granite. The target at the far end — the house — is what you are aiming for. Two teams slide stones toward the house and use the brooms to control where each stone travels. The team whose stone rests closest to the centre scores. Simple in theory."*

A pause, precisely placed.

*"In practice — well. You have five hours to determine that for yourselves."*

Another pause, shorter.

*"One more thing. After six hours, the team with the most points will have two of their members randomly eliminated."*

Silence.

*"The winning team,"* Ninnginni said, as if there had been any ambiguity, *"loses two."*

*"Number thirteen — Polaris — you will serve as referee. I trust your judgement."*

Then nothing.

Twenty-eight people stood on the edge of an ice sheet in the cold dark, four sections above the warmth that had held them on the first day, and nobody said anything for a very long time.

"So we have to *lose*," said Galaxy.

"Or we have to *win,*" said Nightmare. "Depending on how you look at it."

"That's the same thing," said Kiwi.

"No," said Nightmare. "It's really not."

Polaris stood at the edge of the ice with her arms folded and her expression doing the thing it did when she was working through a problem she didn't like the shape of. "Teams," she said. "We need teams first. Everything else is secondary."

Schwalbe had already been thinking about it. "Fourteen and fourteen," he said. "Polaris sits. Everyone else plays." He divided them where they stood, quickly, trying to split skills roughly even — the fast thinkers, the steady ones, the ones whose bodies were compromised. Rally Mally went to Team One. JIKOSU went to Team One. Nightmare went to Team Two, which put him opposite space_fan, which felt right for reasons nobody said aloud.

Team One: space_fan, CrystalWolf, Schwalbe, Rally Mally, The Reddening Day, CharliSpirit, lumallie, Revenant, Elv, Golden, Rabbit, janhk, JIKOSU, WavyDragons.

Team Two: Duccarian, Ashborn, Toad, Retard 2.0, Galaxy, Drug, Kiwi, Akarimmu, Lyrix, NinjaFlout, Mainthemainstar, duck179, Nightmare, SomeDucks.

"This is insane," said Drug, looking at the ice.

"Yes," said Schwalbe. "Get on it."

[[SECTION]] FIVE HOURS

The first hour on the ice was painful in two senses.

The physical sense: the surface was real ice and none of them had spent meaningful time on ice and the first ten minutes were largely people discovering that their feet had opinions about this. Toad went down immediately and lay there for a moment evaluating his choices. Galaxy found that if he kept his arms out and moved slowly he could stay upright, and announced this as though it were a personal achievement. CrystalWolf fell twice and got up both times faster than anyone expected.

The second sense: the problem of winning. Nobody wanted to get good at this, because getting good meant risking your team winning, which meant two of you died. But nobody wanted to be clearly *bad* either — there was something in the room, a pressure none of them could name, that made deliberate failure feel wrong in ways that went beyond strategy. Polaris watched from the edge with flat eyes and said nothing.

The stones were heavy. Much heavier than they looked, and releasing one down the ice without it veering wildly required something that only started to feel possible after an hour of practice. Sweeping, they discovered, was not decorative — it made a real difference to speed and direction, and the brooms were tools, not props. Schwalbe grasped this in the first forty minutes and drilled it into Team One with the same calm precision he'd applied to the parkour course. Team Two had Nightmare, who grasped it by instinct and immediately used it to be better than everyone around him, which was not the same as being a team.

"You're sweeping too early," he told Mainthemainstar, watching his technique.

"I'm doing what you said," said Mainthemainstar.

"I said *sweep when it curves.* That's not the same as sweeping because you're bored."

"I'm not bored, I'm—"

"Sweep when it curves," Nightmare said, and that was the end of that.

Rally Mally played with his bad ankle strapped hard and said nothing about it, but space_fan watched him and could see the cost of every slide. The Reddening Day found, in the third hour, that a low slow release followed by heavy sweeping consistently put the stone close to the centre, and started teaching it to anyone who would listen. CharliSpirit was a natural sweeper — coordinated, reading the stone's drift quickly, adjusting. lumallie was not a natural thrower but she was precise once she understood the mechanics, and by hour four her stones were reliable.

JIKOSU played with one good arm and was somehow better for it. He released with his left, adjusted his grip, and three times in five hours put a stone within five centimetres of the button. Nobody commented on it. Nobody commented on most things JIKOSU did, because commenting seemed to embarrass everyone involved.

"Is anyone else thinking about what happens if we're good?" CharliSpirit asked, at hour three, quietly, to lumallie.

"Yes," said lumallie.

"And?"

"And I don't know." lumallie watched her stone travel down the ice toward the house. It sat three centimetres off centre. "I don't think I can make myself bad at it on purpose. Is that — is that stupid?"

"No," said CharliSpirit. "I can't either."

They looked at each other.

"Play well," said CharliSpirit finally. "Whatever happens, play well."

"Yeah," said lumallie. "Okay."

Polaris refereed the training period with the same efficiency she brought to everything. She called fouls cleanly, tracked what each team was learning, and when Kiwi accused Revenant of releasing past the hog line she assessed it in ten seconds, called it clean, and that was that. Both teams accepted her calls without argument, which was the only thing about the day that was simple.

At hour five, the voice spoke again.

*"Five hours."*

That was all.

They lined up.

[[SECTION]] ONE HOUR

The match ran one hour and the scoring was close in a way that made the problem of winning feel alive in every stone.

Team Two took the first end on a clean shot from Nightmare that sat the button and could not be removed. Team One answered in the second end — Schwalbe read the line perfectly, took out Team Two's stone, left their own sitting top of the house. The Reddening Day added a guard. Polaris scored it, one to one, neutral expression.

By the midpoint Team One were ahead by two. Space_fan threw a stone that did more than she intended — she swept it hard with CrystalWolf and it curled beautifully into the four-foot and she felt the satisfaction of it and then felt complicated about the satisfaction of it and put the broom back on her shoulder and went to the hack without saying anything.

"Nice," said Schwalbe.

"Don't," said space_fan.

He looked at her. He understood. He didn't say it again.

Team Two came back in the fifth end. Nightmare threw a double takeout — two Team One stones removed in a single shot — that drew actual silence from both teams. Even Polaris looked up from her scorecard. He stood at the near end of the ice and watched his stone come to rest and did not look pleased with himself, which was somehow more alarming than if he had.

It finished four to three. Team One.

The ice was still. Twenty-eight people stood on both sides of it, and the ones who had just won stood with the exact expression of people who were not sure what to do with that.

*"Well played."*

The same voice. Unhurried, as always.

*"Team One. Please remain where you are."*

[[SECTION]] THE DRAW

Team Two moved to the side wall. Team One stayed on the ice. Fourteen people, space_fan among them, in numbered hoodies, in the cold.

*"The two members to be eliminated will be determined now."*

A pause that had no mercy in it.

*"Number ten."*

CharliSpirit.

She was standing two spots to space_fan's left. She didn't move. She looked straight ahead.

*"Number eleven."*

lumallie made a sound — small, involuntary, quickly controlled. She was standing right next to CharliSpirit. They'd been standing next to each other all day.

*"Thank you both for your participation."*

space_fan's throat closed.

"No," said CrystalWolf, from somewhere beside her, and it wasn't defiance, it was just the sound a person makes when the word *no* is the only true thing left.

CharliSpirit turned to lumallie. lumallie was looking at her. They didn't say anything — some things had been said already, at hour three, on the ice, and some things were beyond words, and they seemed to know which was which. CharliSpirit put her arm around lumallie's shoulder. lumallie leaned into it. They stood like that on the ice for the moment that remained.

"Race you," said lumallie. Her voice was steady.

CharliSpirit's was too. "No," she said, and held on.

The mechanism was the same as it always was — not visible, not dramatic, not anything space_fan could describe to herself afterward without the description feeling inadequate. They were there and then they were not. The ice under where they'd stood showed no mark.

Polaris's scorecard was still in her hand. She folded it once, twice, and put it in the pocket of her hoodie.

"Move," said Schwalbe, to everyone, quietly. "Come on. Move."

They moved.

[[SECTION]] THE DORM

The dorm was different.

The beds were no longer frames set on the floor. They had been carved directly into the wall itself — recessed alcoves, stone-hewn, arranged in a true grid, four across and seven down less one, the geometry of it exact in a way that felt like an announcement. Twenty-seven beds for twenty-seven people. And the warmth was back, immediate and total the moment the door sealed behind them, the same constant even heat the dorms always held, so different from the ice chamber that space_fan's skin ached readjusting to it. space_fan found hers — she didn't look at the number — and sat down and put her face in her hands for a moment, not crying, just covering.

Polaris sat beside her without being asked.

"I keep thinking about what she said," space_fan said. "CharliSpirit. At hour three."

"I know," said Polaris.

"She knew. She knew and she played well anyway."

"Yes."

"I don't know if that's — I don't know what that is."

Polaris was quiet for a moment. "I think," she said carefully, "it's the only kind of brave that matters. The kind that doesn't wait to see how it turns out."

space_fan took her hands away from her face. The grid of carved beds was lit in the same low warm glow the dorms always had, and twenty-six other people were finding their way into them, and the room was quiet in the way rooms are quiet after they've been full and aren't anymore.

Across the grid, Nightmare lay on his back with one arm over his eyes. He had played the best curling of anyone in the room today. Team Two had lost and he was still alive and Team One had won and they were not and the specific arithmetic of that sat somewhere on his face that he was covering with his arm.

Schwalbe sat on the edge of his bed — number three — and did not lie down for a long time. He sat and looked at the space where beds ten and eleven were empty and their hoodies were folded on the mattresses, and something moved through his face that he didn't let stay.

JIKOSU lay still and looked at the ceiling.

Rally Mally unwrapped his ankle in the dark, slowly, and looked at it, and wrapped it again.

space_fan lay back and stared at the ceiling grid and thought about a flower name she couldn't remember and a tail she'd almost caught and the way lumallie had said *race you* and the way CharliSpirit had said *no* and held on. She thought about the voice — *well played* — and how composed it was, how precisely it had waited until the last stone settled before it spoke, and how none of it was cruelty in the way she understood cruelty. It was something more patient than that. Something that had a plan.

Four sections up. Cold enough now to feel in the lungs.

Sixteen to go.

She closed her eyes. Whatever had chased her through the red corridor did not come back that night — or came back and left no mark she could find in the morning.
