[[NARRATIVE]]

[[SECTION]] MORNING

The dorm was colder than the one before.

Not unbearable — not yet — but the kind of cold that sits in the joints and makes the first waking movement an effort. space_fan could see her breath. She hadn't been able to do that yesterday. She lay still for a moment watching it mist in the pale chamber light and thought about the warmth three sections below her and how quickly a thing you'd taken for granted could become something you mourned.

The provision shelf had been restocked in the night. Grain-cakes, water. She took hers and the first bite told her immediately that something had changed. Cold all the way through — not chilled like water, but cold the way stone is cold, like it had been sitting in a place that didn't care about temperature. It sat in the mouth without pleasure. Beside her, Polaris ate the same way she did everything — methodically, without complaint, watching the room.

"It's going to get worse than this," Polaris said, not looking at her.

"I know," said space_fan.

"Good." Polaris swallowed. "Just making sure we both do."

Schwalbe was already on his feet, moving between people, quiet words here and there, not performing comfort — something more practical than that. Checking who had slept. Checking who was shaky. space_fan watched him and thought: *he knows something is coming.* But then again, something was always coming. That was the shape of this.

Galaxy tried to palm a grain-cake into his sleeve for later. Toad watched him do it. Neither said anything.

The Reddening Day stared at his water flask like it had personally failed him. "It's ice," he said.

"It's not ice," said Duccarian.

"It's practically ice."

"Drink it anyway," said Schwalbe, passing, and The Reddening Day drank it anyway.

[[SECTION]] THE VOICE

It came between bites.

Not from any visible source — no speaker, no grille, no person standing in a doorway. It came from the walls, or from everywhere at once, the way the heat had come at the bottom. Except this was not heat. This was a voice. Male, unhurried, with a French accent that landed in the cold air of the dorm like something pressed and deliberate.

*"Good morning."*

Thirty-five people stopped chewing.

*"I hope y'all had a great stay."*

The accent smoothed the words into something almost cordial, which made them worse. space_fan felt the hairs on her arm rise.

*"Today we will test your agility."* A pause, measured and calm. *"Easier said than done."*

Then: silence. The kind that asks nothing and offers nothing.

"Who—" CharliSpirit started.

The provision shelf retracted into the floor.

Not slowly. Not gradually. One moment it was there, and the next moment there was a seam in the stone where it had been, flush and sealed, as if it had never existed. The grain-cakes still in people's hands remained. The ones on the shelf were gone. The ones people had tried to save — the ones tucked into pockets, folded into sleeves — those went too, in a different way: a smell first, sharp and wrong, and then the food itself going dark in their hands, collapsing, blackening, a rotten smell hitting the cold air in a single wave.

Galaxy dropped what was in his sleeve and stared at his hand.

"They know," said Polaris, flat. "They know exactly what we have and where it is."

Drug said, precisely: "I'd like to say something very rude right now."

"Save it," said Schwalbe. He was already at the far wall. "Everyone get to the walls."

"Why—" Rabbit started.

The walls answered. A deep, low vibration, felt in the feet before it was heard, rising through the stone floor. Then a sound like the inside of a mountain adjusting itself — and the walls *moved.*

[[SECTION]] THE COURSE EMERGES

It came out of the stone like something that had always been inside it, waiting.

Handholds first: stone blocks pressing outward from the walls in clusters, offset, irregular, a pattern that almost made sense but didn't quite. Then platforms — ledges wide enough to stand on, some narrow as a shoe, others broad as a table, emerging at heights that ranged from a metre off the floor to somewhere in the dim region near the ceiling that the light didn't fully reach. Ropes dropped from points high above — thick, knotted, swaying with the momentum of their own emergence. A series of bars extended horizontally from the far wall, spaced for swinging. Two glide-lines ran diagonally across the space from high anchor points to lower platforms, each fitted with a handgrip.

The course went *up.* That was its whole logic — start at the floor, end at the hatch that had opened high in the far wall, wide enough for a person, lit from beyond with the cold pale light of whatever came next.

No harnesses. No safety lines. No net below.

Schwalbe looked at it for three seconds. Then he turned to face thirty-four people. "Groups of three," he said. "Stay in visual range of your group at all times. If you see a hold retract, you call it. Loud. You give the person on it a half-second of warning and half a second is enough if they hear it. Don't freeze on a hold — keep moving. Momentum is safer than stillness." He looked around. "Questions later. Move now."

"How long do we have?" said Mainthemainstar.

The voice came again, as if it had been listening — because it had:

*"Six hours."*

Then nothing.

"Move," said Schwalbe.

[[SECTION]] THE CLIMB

The first retractions happened at the eighteen-minute mark.

Three holds, scattered across different parts of the wall, sliding back into the stone without warning — there one moment, flush with the wall the next. Nobody was on them. The group learned what it looked like when it happened, and the learning cost nothing, and that was the last time the learning cost nothing.

hollow Camillia was at what seemed like a safe height. Twenty feet, maybe twenty-five — high enough to need the course, low enough that the eye said *survivable* in the easy, unreliable way the eye says things. She was on the rock face, moving left toward the next hold, and the hold she was reaching for pulled back into the wall as her fingers were a half-metre from it. She jerked away from the motion and in doing so shifted her weight off the hold under her right foot, which also retracted — and then she was swinging on two hands from a ledge that was already shaking.

She fell.

The sound of it silenced the room completely. space_fan was fifteen feet below and to the left and she saw it and she looked away before the end and that was the first mercy she had given herself in three days and she did not know if she deserved it.

Nobody spoke for a moment. Then Schwalbe's voice: "Keep moving. Keep *moving.* Stopping on a hold is how you end up like that."

It was brutal and it was right and space_fan hated that it was right and she kept moving.

The course had a logic, once you were in it. The handholds came in clusters with dead zones between them — and the dead zones were where the retractions happened most often, as if the game wanted you scared and reaching at the same time. Polaris, ten feet above space_fan and slightly left, had already identified something.

"They come in sets," she called down, not loud — steady. "Three or four at once, then a pause. The pause is maybe ninety seconds. *Move in the pauses.*"

"You sure?" Schwalbe called across.

"No," Polaris said. "But I'm more right than wrong so far."

Schwalbe applied it. The people near him felt it — a rhythm emerging from the chaos, Schwalbe calling *now, now* in the windows Polaris was reading, and the group moving in pulses rather than a continuous terrified scramble. It didn't make the course safe. It made it slightly less random, which was not the same thing but was the only thing available.

Rally Mally was moving well until the rope section at hour one. He'd grabbed the first rope, was mid-swing toward the platform, and the rope gave — not fully, not gone, but a lurch, a partial retraction that shortened it by thirty centimetres in a single jerk. He lost the arc. He didn't make the platform. He hit the ledge below it instead, both feet, the wrong angle, and the sound he made was not a scream but a single sharp sound that carried. He stayed on the ledge, hunched, both hands gripping the edge.

"Rally," said space_fan, near him.

"Ankle," he said, through his teeth. "It's — I'm on it. Give me a second."

He took twenty seconds. Then he straightened, tested his weight, and made a noise that was controlled and private and deeply unpleasant. "Still works," he said.

"It might not by hour six," said space_fan.

"Then I'll deal with that at hour six," said Rally Mally, and started climbing.

pinkfml died at hour two.

She had been doing well — better than anyone watching her from the outside would have predicted. The cat room had steadied something in her, or maybe the cold had, or maybe she had just decided. She was on a glide-line, grip in both hands, weight committed, and the anchor retracted at the midpoint of the line. The line went slack. She went with it.

space_fan heard it but was on a rock section and couldn't look. She heard Schwalbe's voice, very quiet, say her name once, and then move on, and that quiet was worse than shouting would have been.

JIKOSU was somewhere above most of them — he moved fast and alone and said nothing, which was not unusual. At hour two and a half a hold retracted under his left hand as he was transferring weight to it. He didn't fall. He caught the wall with his forearm, dragged, and got a hand on a lower hold, but the drag across the stone took skin off his forearm from elbow to wrist and left a long dark mark on the rock that space_fan passed twenty minutes later and didn't look at directly. When she reached the platform where he'd paused to wrap his arm in a strip torn from his sleeve, he was already moving again. He did not mention it.

"Does it need more than that?" she asked.

He looked at the wrapping. "No," he said, and kept going.

Shroud died at hour three.

He was in the upper-middle section of the course, the part where the heights started to mean something, where a fall was not a question but a statement. He'd been moving well — fast, even, taking risks that paid off several times in a row, and that was the problem: the risks that pay off teach you that they pay off. He reached for a hold that looked stable and it was stable, and then three seconds later, as his full weight shifted onto it, it retracted. He had no secondary. His hands found the wall and found nothing.

space_fan did not see it happen. She heard it in the reactions of the people above her — a collective sound, breath pulled in. She did not ask what it was.

*Keep moving,* she told herself. *Keep moving. That's what Schwalbe said. That's the only—*

HeyNoQuest died at hour four, but not quickly.

He was stranded.

The hold under his right foot retracted, and the one to his left retracted two seconds after, and he was left with two handholds and nothing else — hanging on the wall at a height that had no good answer, the nearest solid hold forty centimetres below and to the right, just far enough that swinging for it was a decision and not a reflex. He called it out, because that's what they did. "Hold's gone. Hanging."

Schwalbe heard him. "Left side, six o'clock. There's a platform."

"I can't reach it."

"Wait for the retraction pause. It'll come back."

It came back. A different hold, not the one he'd lost — one two feet higher than he needed, useless. It went again. He waited. His forearms were shaking. From below, space_fan watched and could not help and the helplessness was a specific kind of pain she did not have a name for. She watched him hold on. She watched him hold on for a long time. She watched his arms give.

Reaps died at hour five and ten minutes, in the high section, in a sequence of four rapid retractions that nobody could have predicted — not Polaris, not Schwalbe. The course seemed to pulse sometimes, going random in a long burst, every pattern it had shown you becoming noise. This was one of those bursts. Reaps had been three-quarters of the way to the exit hatch when it hit. He was not the only one it hit — NinjaFlout lost a hold in the same burst and caught himself on a rope with both hands and hung there, breathing, until the burst ended. Reaps did not catch himself.

Polaris, near the top, did not look down. space_fan, further below, did. She had stopped doing herself the mercy of not looking.

[[SECTION]] THE LAST MINUTE

Loyal2Schwab was the last one still on the course at the five-hour-fifty mark.

He was not struggling — that was the thing. He was moving, had been moving steadily, had helped three people through a difficult section at hour three and lost time doing it, and now the debt came due. He was in the mid-section as the others filtered through the exit hatch above, and space_fan, who had made it through at hour four and a half, had been watching from the other side, through the hatch, willing him to move faster, and he was moving as fast as he could.

The voice said nothing. The timer needed no announcement. The course announced it.

At hour six exactly the floor ignited.

Not like fire spreads — not from a point outward — but all at once, every section of the chamber floor catching simultaneously, white-hot, the kind of heat that did not wait. The bottom of the course became impassable in a second. Everything below the first ten feet of wall was gone. And with the fire the course began retracting rapidly — not in the testing, pulsing way it had all day, but everything, everywhere, the holds and ropes and platforms pulling back into the wall in a wave that moved upward.

Loyal2Schwab felt the wave coming. space_fan saw his face in the distance, through the hatch, and she saw that he understood. He moved fast — the fastest he'd moved all day — reaching and pulling and not waiting for pauses because there were no pauses now, just the wave of retraction rising to meet him and the heat below. He got higher. The exit hatch was there.

The wave got there first.

The rope he was on retracted. He found a hold. The hold retracted. He found the wall with both hands and both feet and held it by friction alone for one second — two — and then there was nothing left to hold and the wall was smooth and the fire was below him and the hatch was above and the distance between was too much.

He fell into the white.

space_fan turned away from the hatch. The fire's light died as the hatch sealed behind the last survivor through. She was standing in a corridor. Around her, twenty-eight other people stood in the same silence, the kind that follows something you cannot undo.

Schwalbe's voice, after a moment: "Is anyone hurt."

"Ankle," said Rally Mally, from the floor, where he had sat down against the wall. "Bad."

"Here," said JIKOSU, unwrapping the makeshift bandage on his arm and looking at the damage underneath.

"Anyone else," Schwalbe said.

Nothing. No one.

"Okay," said Schwalbe. He sounded like a person spending something they cannot refill. "Okay. Keep moving."

[[SECTION]] THE DORM

It was warmer than the corridor, which was not warm. But it was enough.

Twenty-nine beds. The provision shelf held twenty-nine sets. Cold food again, cold water again — nobody touched it tonight. They filed in and found beds and sat or lay and none of them talked much because the day had already said everything it intended to say.

space_fan sat on her bed and looked at her hands, which were scraped from the rock, and thought about HeyNoQuest hanging on the wall and the way time had made the decision for him. She thought about Loyal2Schwab losing thirty seconds to help three people and what those thirty seconds had cost. She was trying to decide if that made it worse or better and she couldn't, and she didn't think she ever would.

Polaris sat across from her, cleaning a scrape on her palm with the corner of her blanket. "You're doing that thing," she said.

"What thing."

"The thing where you carry them."

space_fan didn't answer.

"It's not wrong," Polaris said. "Just — make sure you can still carry yourself."

Schwalbe had found the beds on either side of Rally Mally and sat between them, not sleeping, not talking, just there — a presence that said *you're not alone tonight* without requiring words. Rally Mally stared at the ceiling. His ankle had been wrapped properly using strips from Schwalbe's sleeve and had been elevated on the folded blanket and it hurt, but it would hold.

JIKOSU lay still. His arm was cleaned as well as it could be and wrapped again. He looked at the ceiling with the same flat attention he gave everything.

Across the dorm, Nightmare was already asleep, or appeared to be, which was somehow more unsettling than if he'd been awake.

space_fan lay back. The cold had worked its way into her now — not just the air, the place itself, the stone and the altitude and the absence of the warmth that had held them on the first day. Three sections up from the core. Three days of climbing. Seventeen left.

She thought about the voice. *Easier said than done.* The composure of it. The faint warmth of the accent against the cold. She thought about how he had known exactly when they'd eaten enough, and exactly when to take the food away, and exactly when to light the floor. She thought about the word *agility* and how it had said nothing about what the day would actually be.

*Easier said than done.*

She would remember that. She closed her eyes.
