DAY 2 — THE CAT ROOM

[[NARRATIVE]]

[[SECTION]] MORNING

The passage opened before anyone touched it.

That was how day two began — stone grinding on stone in the pre-dawn quiet of the cool chamber, and forty people waking to find that the game had already decided it was time. The air coming down the new passage had an edge that hadn't been there yesterday.

space_fan had been awake for an hour. She'd lain still counting the others' breathing — thirty-nine of them, all present — and given up on sleep before the door moved. Polaris was already standing when she sat up.

"Slept?" space_fan asked.

"No," said Polaris. "Thought too much."

"Same."

"About?"

"The flashes," space_fan said. "The animal one, at the end. The faces."

"Don't focus on them." Polaris turned back to the passage. "Focus on what's in front."

The others woke in stages. Drug stretched and said "something smells different," and she was right, though nobody could name what. CrystalWolf was on her feet immediately, already counting: "Twenty-seven, twenty-eight — okay, we're all here." Retard 2.0 went straight to the provision shelf and found it set exactly as before, forty grain-cakes and forty flasks, and said "every time, exactly forty" to no one in particular. WavyDragons said "where's the food" and found it. Ashborn stood in the middle of the chamber, looked at the ceiling, and said "we're higher than yesterday." He meant the temperature. He was right.

They ate. They filed into the passage.

[[SECTION]] THE ROOM

The room at the top of the passage was a dome.

The ceiling curved up into shadow at a height that made space_fan's neck ache to estimate. The walls were worked stone, dressed flat and smooth, and from floor to crown they were riddled with ledges, shelves, crevices, alcoves — a vertical landscape of shadow and rock. The floor was broad and pale and clear, except for a line of forty numbered stands near the entrance, each bearing a shallow basket.

space_fan saw the cats before she understood they were cats.

They were everywhere, once you knew. Stone-grey, smoke-grey, pressed into the angles of the room in exactly the posture of things that had been still so long they'd learned to be architecture. One on a ledge six feet up, flat and motionless until its tail twitched. One in a niche at knee height, eyes half-closed, the colour of the stone behind it. The room was full of them — hundreds — and none of them were moving.

On the entrance wall, in the same scored script:

12 HOURS. 5 CATS PER PERSON. PLACE YOUR CATS IN YOUR BASKET.
WHEN THE BELL RINGS, ALL BASKETS LOCK.
THOSE WITH FEWER THAN FIVE DO NOT CONTINUE.

A beat of silence.

"These are real cats," said SomeDucks, with vast, sincere relief.

"They'll scratch," said Lyrix.

"So will I," said Drug.

"Five each, forty people — there's easily two hundred in here," said Mainthemainstar. "This is doable."

"Then why doesn't it feel doable?" said pinkfml.

"Because nothing here feels what it is," said Polaris, reading the wall again. "Twelve hours is long enough to get comfortable and short enough to get stupid. Don't get comfortable."

PopcornRya looked at the baskets, at the cats, at the ceiling. "Okay," he said. "Okay. I can do this." He said it like a person who wasn't sure yet.

"Of course you can," said CharliSpirit. Then she walked in.

Nightmare laughed — the laugh that arrives before you choose it. He looked at Ink'dDiamondPaintingQueen and said: "Don't worry. Some of us were born for this."

She looked at him without expression. "I wasn't aware this was about you."

"It's always about me," Nightmare said, and walked into the room.

JIKOSU said nothing. He looked at the cats, then at Nightmare's back, then walked directly to stand number twenty-five without checking the numbering on any of the others.

"How did he know which one was his?" said Kiwi.

Akarimmu watched him go. "I don't know," he said. "But he did."

[[SECTION]] THE HUNT

The first hour was the worst kind of easy.

Cats were findable near the ground, and the relief of finding one made you careless. space_fan found her first in under ten minutes — a grey tabby on a ground-level shelf, so still she'd read it as stone until it blinked. It came to her basket without a fight.

"One," she said to herself.

"One," said Polaris, arriving at her own basket with a similar grey shape tucked against her collarbone. Their eyes met. Something like steadiness passed between them — not comfort, something more useful. Then they both turned back to the room.

By hour two most people had two or three. By hour three the easy ones were gone.

The cats still in hiding had better instincts than the first wave. They pressed into crevices, climbed higher, sat tighter in the corners where the dome's ambient glow didn't reach. Finding them meant climbing, or patience, or both.

Duccarian found one per hour, methodical as clockwork, and put his fifth in at hour five and sat down against the wall and closed his eyes. Toad said: "You're not sleeping." Duccarian said: "Resting my eyes."

duck179 worked the low ledges only. "Low is where I'd hide," he explained to no one. Five by hour four.

Schwalbe moved fast and said only: "Easy." Three hours. Done.

"Easy for *you,*" said Shroud, who was still on his third.

Golden moved quietly through the room and put his fifth in at hour four. He exhaled a long slow breath and said "okay" to the air, like paying off something owed.

JIKOSU found all five in the first hour. He returned to his basket and did not move again. Rabbit passed him at hour six and slowed. "All done?"

JIKOSU said: "Yes."

Rabbit accepted this and kept moving.

Revenant found his fourth cat sitting in Galaxy's basket. "That one's yours," Galaxy said.

"I know," said Revenant.

"How'd it get in mine?"

"Cats go where they want." Revenant took it and left. Galaxy then found one under his own basket stand. It had been there since the beginning. "We don't talk about this," he told the cat, and put it in.

"I saw," said Revenant, three metres away, not looking up.

"You didn't," said Galaxy.

"Sure," said Revenant.

NinjaFlout found his fifth in a ceiling crack, jumped for it, and when he landed said "YES" loud enough that three cats startled off nearby ledges and scattered.

"Sorry," he said to the room.

"It's fine," said Elv.

"Less fine for the people who needed those cats," said hollow Camillia.

"They'll settle," said Elv. "Cats always settle."

They did settle. Not always where the right person could reach them.

The Reddening Day found two immediately and then lost four hours on his third, walking tightening circles, before he stopped in the middle of the room and stood completely still and let his eyes adjust to the specific texture of camouflage. Three cats in twenty minutes after that. He found Rally Mally and said: "Stop moving. Let everything settle. Then look."

Rally Mally stopped. Looked. "Oh," he said. "*Oh.* There's one right *there.* How did I miss—"

"You were moving," said The Reddening Day.

janhk finished by hour three and spent the rest sitting by his basket. HeyNoQuest found him at hour eight. "You're just done?"

"Done," said janhk.

"How?"

"Slowly first," said janhk. "Then quickly."

Loyal2Schwab and Kiwi arrived at the same ledge-cat at the same moment. Loyal2Schwab gestured Kiwi forward. "I've got three in sight from here."

"You sure?"

"Take it."

"Cheers," said Kiwi.

lumallie found her fifth at hour seven and held it a moment before setting it in the basket. "I love cats," she said, absolutely sincerely. It was purring.

Elv found all five by watching what other people accidentally flushed from hiding. "Redirect ecology," they said to themself, not for anyone else.

Mainthemainstar finished and began announcing finds to the room. "Cluster near the north wall, second shelf from the bottom, three together—"

"I need one," said Reaps, already moving.

WavyDragons, done at hour five and watching: "There's another behind the entrance sign. Brown-grey, sits like a lump."

"That's my sixth," said Reaps, who was already putting it in regardless.

CharliSpirit and lumallie worked the ledge-structure in parallel. "One zone, one cat, back to base," CharliSpirit said at hour three.

"I'm on four," said lumallie.

"Four," said CharliSpirit.

"Race you," said lumallie.

"No," said CharliSpirit, and kept climbing.

Akarimmu finished at hour seven and came to sit near space_fan, who was on four. "East alcoves," he said, without preamble. "Third row from the top. Grey one, doesn't move even when you reach for it. Pick it up."

"Thank you," said space_fan.

He shrugged. "Easier together."

pinkfml at hour ten: still on four and nearly running. "Where are they, where are *they*—"

"Stop," said space_fan.

"I don't have time—"

"Two hours left. Slow down. Look at the shadows the right *size.*"

pinkfml stopped. Breathed. Her eyes changed. She found her fifth at hour eleven and sat down on the floor and stared at the ceiling and said nothing for a long time.

[[SECTION]] NIGHTMARE

He had five by hour two and nothing useful to do with the other ten.

By hour four he was orbiting Godly_Aura, who was on his third and showing it. "You've been at that ledge thirty minutes," Nightmare said.

"I've got two more in sight," said Godly_Aura.

"In sight and in hand are different things."

"Leave him," said Loyal2Schwab, not looking up.

"I'm just talking," Nightmare said pleasantly, and moved on.

He found Acoustyx in the south corner at hour five, still on two, working the same patch of wall with the focus of someone who had run out of ideas.

"Buddy," said Nightmare.

"Don't," said Acoustyx.

"SomeDucks told you about the south cluster? Already cleared. You're in a dead zone."

"Then I'll go somewhere else."

"Sure," said Nightmare. He waited until Acoustyx moved off, then walked calmly to the section Acoustyx had been about to try next and began working it himself.

At hour eight he found Ink'dDiamondPaintingQueen on the upper ledges. She'd climbed high — braver than most — and had three. She was working the next shelf up, methodical, not looking at him.

"Four yet?" he called up.

"Three," she said. "And I'm fine."

"Didn't say you weren't."

"You were about to."

Nightmare said nothing. The silence he left was worse than anything he could have said, and he knew it, and he let it sit there, and walked away.

space_fan had watched all of this from across the room. Polaris passed her at hour nine, basket full, and caught her watching Nightmare. "After," Polaris said, quietly.

"After," said space_fan.

[[SECTION]] THE BELL

It came from everywhere at once — a low resonant strike, felt through the floor and up into the chest. Done.

Forty people stood by their baskets.

space_fan counted hers. Five. Polaris: five. She looked down the row and read faces instead of baskets.

Godly_Aura stood with his hands at his sides. His basket had four. He'd seen a fifth near the ceiling apex, been certain of it, climbed for it, and found the ledge empty when he got there. He didn't say anything. He looked at his basket, then at the far wall, then at nothing.

Doomfox had three. He had worked the room carefully and his section had been the worst-lit in the dome and he knew none of that would matter but he said it anyway, quietly, to the air: "I had three. I saw more up there. I just needed more time." A pause. "Three."

PopcornRya had four. She'd known since hour ten. She'd spent two hours rechecking ground she'd already covered, and said now, in a small voice: "I only have four." Like she still wasn't sure it was real.

Ink'dDiamondPaintingQueen had four. She had climbed higher than anyone else in the room and she still had four. She looked at the entrance wall — the instructions — and she did not look at Nightmare.

Acoustyx had four. He arrived from the south corner at a run and counted before he stopped moving. Then he put his hands in his pockets and stood there.

The door at the far end of the dome opened. A passage beyond it, warm-lit, and beyond that the faint suggestion of somewhere to rest.

The thirty-five walked through. space_fan went last, and looked back once.

The five stood by their baskets. The dome was quiet. In the shadows, unhurried, the cats were beginning to move again.

The door closed.

[[SECTION]] THE DORM

The passage beyond was short and sloped gently upward and ended in a room that was, without question, a dormitory. Rows of narrow beds, real beds — frames, mattresses, folded blankets, a small pillow each. Numbered. Thirty-five of them. The provision shelf: thirty-five sets.

Several people stopped in the doorway.

"Beds," said CrystalWolf.

"Real beds," said CharliSpirit.

"We shouldn't read into it," said Polaris. She found hers — number fifteen — and sat down. The mattress held her. After everything else that was almost enough to undo her. She sat very straight instead.

space_fan found hers and lay back and stared at the low plain ceiling, which was much less threatening than the dome. Around her, thirty-four others settled. Someone cried very quietly in the dark and nobody spoke to it and it ran its course.

Nightmare found his bed and lay down and said nothing. Five cats in his basket. Same as everyone else still alive. He let that be enough.

JIKOSU lay down and closed his eyes.

space_fan was cold. Not very cold — not yet — but the warmth of the core was two sections below her now, and for the first time she felt its absence as a weight. A taken thing. Something that had been hers and wasn't anymore.

Two sections up. Two days of warmth lost.

Eighteen to go.

She closed her eyes. The flashbacks did not come tonight, or she was asleep before they could, and she did not know which was better.
